# Glossary — Design Thinking and Innovation Metrics

Terms as defined or used in Michael Lewrick, *Design Thinking and Innovation Metrics* (Wiley, 2023).
Chapter references point to `chapters/chNN-*.md`.

**AARRR** — Dave McClure's innovation-accounting funnel: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral. Metrics depend on business model, industry, and ecosystem configuration (Ch 6)
**Abduction (design abduction)** — the cognitive mode of design thinking: two unknowns (WHAT and HOW) leading to a process of creative exploration; after Dorst (2017) (Ch 4)
**Activity metrics** — the large-organization name for vanity metrics; used to demonstrate how busy teams are and to justify innovation theater (Ch 1)
**Advanced AI** — future AI systems that surpass human intelligence in all domains (Ch 18)
**AKIs (Aspirations and Key Insights)** — a naming for OKR-like systems that emphasises the aspirational side for innovation teams, grounded in customer insights (Ch 1, Ch 15)
**Ambition level** — the intended stretch of an objective: Moonshot, Roofshot, or Everest (Ch 15)
**Anterior cingulate cortex (ACC)** — brain region where mixed messages and contradictory objectives are perceived; impaired resolution reduces flexible thinking, the neural argument for transparency (Ch 19)
**Ansoff matrix (adapted for MVPs)** — existing/new offers × existing/new markets; design thinking and lean start-up focus on new markets / new offers (Ch 6)
**Assumptions-to-knowledge ratio** — design-challenge metric tracking conversion of unknowns into knowns (Ch 13)
**Automated AI** — automation of the ML pipeline from task formulation to result summary and recommendation; time savings mainly in data preparation and feature engineering (Ch 18)
**Bayes' theorem** — "a classic of probability theory… a calculation rule used to calculate the (conditional) probability of an event"; used to update probability of innovation success after each experiment (Ch 6)
**Black-box experiment** — design-thinking-cognition experiment where multiple conditions are set to perform a task or apply a tool (Ch 19)
**Black-ocean strategy** — the class of strategy Everest objectives enable (Ch 15)
**Bowl of trust** — the foundational team-of-teams condition; also a typical internal OKR objective (Ch 4, Ch 15)
**Bring it home** — the transport half of hunting-and-transport: delivering results (Ch 4)
**Captivateability** — number and intensity of interactions in a business ecosystem; a scaling focal point (Ch 6)
**Causation vs correlation** — causation triggers change before things go wrong; association never implies causality (Ch 8, Ch 14)
**Central connectors** — ONA nodes who seem to know everyone and influence all teams quickly (Ch 4)
**Chi-square test** — measure of the *significance* (not strength) of association between two variables (Ch 14)
**Circle of WE** — the cultural end-state of team-of-teams expansion (Ch 4)
**Confidence level (experiment)** — Insights (I) × Evidence (E), each on a 1–3 Likert scale; C = I × E, max 9, min 1 (Ch 6)
**Confidence level (OKR)** — the pre-set aspirational target level for scoring: e.g. 10% for Moonshot stretch, 90% for Roofshot, 70% for Everest (Ch 15)
**Critical items diagram** — tool that fixes eight critical items (four experiences, four functions), placed as a daisy flower with equal-weight petals (Ch 6)
**Csikszentmihalyi's model (1999)** — creativity measured as social value through person, domain, and field; the field determines survival within the domain (Ch 12)
**Data ethics** — part of the entire development cycle, spanning privacy, accountability, security & safety, explainability & transparency, non-discrimination & fairness, human control of technology, professional responsibility (Ch 17)
**Data Ideation Canvas** — discover/create/deliver canvas linking existing data, applied data, and new data to prototypes and metrics (Ch 17)
**Data literacy (for innovation)** — the continuous learning process creating the ability to recognize, understand, interpret, create, communicate, and compute information (Ch 3)
**Declare the behavior** — the design thinking principle of openly stating whether the team is hunting or transporting (Ch 3, Ch 4)
**Deep being brain** — the accumbens layer governing motivation and focus aligned with resources; correlates with HBDI profiles (Ch 19)
**Descriptive analysis** — analytics answering "what happened?" (Ch 14)
**Design fixation** — a construct measured under the design-thinking-cognition paradigm (Ch 19)
**Design Thinking Canvas** — documentation canvas covering success definition, vision, MVP requirements, problem and solution space across the micro process (Ch 6)
**Desirability** — customer/user need dimension of the design thinking lens (Ch 3)
**Diagnostic analysis** — analytics answering "why did this happen?" (Ch 14)
**Discrete choice analysis** — WTP method based on actual purchase data or stated preference among attribute sets (Ch 6)
**Double materiality assessment** — narrowing ESG metrics to those with both greatest business value and greatest stakeholder relevance (Ch 6)
**Drift method** — naïve forecasting variant where projections change over time by the average historical change (Ch 14)
**Ecosystem capital** — metric quantifying the value of network connections, configuration, and value streams in a business ecosystem (Ch 6)
**Effectuation** — logic of action treating the future as controllable and means-oriented; contrast causation (Ch 7)
**Empathy map** — SAY / DO / THINK / FEEL synthesis tool (Ch 6)
**Everest objective** — objective aimed at something new to the customer and the market; rarely achieved alone; enables black-ocean strategies (Ch 2, Ch 15)
**Exploration grid** — expected return × degree of evidence; four quadrants of market opportunity (Ch 6)
**Exploration map** — documents novelty and degree of change in every experiment over the macro cycle (Ch 3)
**Exploration metrics** — see *minimum viable set of exploration metrics* (Ch 10)
**EXPLOIT** — optimizing the existing business: efficiency, cost, output, ROI; the "superhighway" (Ch 1, Ch 2)
**Exploitation metrics** — appropriate mix of relevant metrics applied after product/market fit to quantify business performance (Ch 11)
**EXPLORE** — realizing new market opportunities: creativity, experimentation, new capabilities; the "jungle" (Ch 1, Ch 2)
**F-pattern** — the upper-left to bottom-right reading pattern observed for web pages and dashboards (Ch 14)
**FAST goals** — Frequently discussed, Ambitious, Specific, Transparent (Ch 15)
**Feasibility** — the reality that a design challenge can be realized with reasonable effort and cost (Ch 3)
**Fixation duration metric** — total time all viewers spend looking at a dashboard area (Ch 14)
**Fractional anisotropy (FA)** — technique measuring white-matter structure, used to validate Guilford-model creativity measures (Ch 12)
**Future-back approach** — the basis of most Moonshot objectives: reasoning from an envisioned future backwards (Ch 16)
**Generative models** — data-science technique producing alternative prototypes, wireframes, and A/B simulations in the solution space (Ch 17)
**General AI** — AI matching human intelligence in all domains and tasks (Ch 18)
**Groan Zone** — the hardest macro-cycle area: selecting the features and experiences that reach the final prototype (Ch 3)
**Growth mindset scale** — Dweck's three-item scale, used for individual measurement (Ch 13)
**Guilford model** — creativity of a person measured by fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration (plus connections) (Ch 12)
**Health metrics** — five to seven weekly, threshold-based vital-sign measures the responsible team can directly influence, linked to the North Star (Ch 11)
**HBDI (Herrmann Brain Dominance Instrument)** — personality profile correlating with deep-brain-area measures (Ch 19)
**HMW ("How might we…")** — the actionable reframing of a design challenge into a question that opens the exploration space (Ch 3, Ch 6, Ch 16)
**Hunting and transport** — Larry Leifer's model of design thinking behavior: hunt for the opportunity, then bring it home (Ch 4)
**Hybrid intelligence** — the future end-state of design thinking *with* AI, beyond the hybrid model (Ch 18)
**Hybrid model** — the combination of design thinking and big data analytics as a joint process framework, toolkit, and mindset (Ch 17)
**Hyperscanning** — neuro technique measuring several people simultaneously, e.g. teams on empathy (Ch 19)
**Imitation** — level 1 of Nilsson's taxonomy: a similar or almost similar product to one in existence (Ch 12)
**IMS (Innovation Measurement System)** — the organization's overall system of innovation metrics; matures IMS 0 → IMS 3 (Ch 7)
**IMS 0 (Experimental)** — awareness of a balanced portfolio; manual data collection; static information (Ch 7)
**IMS 1 (Professionalized)** — accepted EXPLORE measures; key metrics operationalized; single-mode automation (Ch 7)
**IMS 2 (Data Driven)** — advanced forecasting; hybrid model; multimodal automation; real-time information (Ch 7)
**IMS 3 (AI Driven)** — AI part of objective setting; dynamic multimodal automation; AI-supported decisions (Ch 7)
**Ill-defined measurement** — an object requiring the problem to be made tangible and described, e.g. purpose, creativity, mindset, brand image (Ch 7)
**Innovation accounting** — lean start-up practice of objectively demonstrating conclusions from MVP experimentation (Ch 6)
**Innovation ecosystem** — loosely connected network of actors co-creating innovations around a technology, often in clusters (Ch 13)
**Innovation self-efficacy score** — Berny Roth (Stanford) measure of growth mindset in relation to design thinking (Ch 13)
**Input metric** — measures assets and resources, e.g. budgets for design thinking training (Ch 8)
**Insight velocity** — rate at which customer interactions generate useful insights; a canonical exploration metric and kill-metric enabler (Ch 10)
**Jobs to be done (JTBD)** — "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected result]"; measured on importance × satisfaction with existing solutions (Ch 6)
**Key result** — quantitative measure with a start value and a target value showing progress toward an objective; never a solution or a task (Ch 15)
**Kill metric** — a pre-defined measurable criterion that terminates an otherwise promising project; canonical form "If X hasn't happened until $1, we'll shut it down" (Ch 10)
**Kurtosis** — measure of a distribution's tails containing extreme values (Ch 14)
**Lagging indicator** — metric measuring past performance; slow to change; confirms later-stage results (Ch 7)
**Leading indicator** — predictive metric measuring the activities needed to reach the goal; trackable on an ongoing basis (Ch 7)
**Lean Canvas** — one-page business/monetization model documenting problem, solution, UVP, key metrics, channels, segments, costs, revenues (Ch 6)
**Level of measurement** — nominal, ordinal, interval, or ratio; determines which statistical analyses are available (Ch 14)
**Love it, change it, or leave it** — the standing action set after every experiment (Ch 6); also applied to metrics themselves (Ch 2)
**Metric** — a meaningful derivative of measures, e.g. a calculation between two or more measures with defined business meaning (Ch 7)
**Micro process / macro cycle** — the Double Diamond iteration (understand→test) and its repetition toward final prototype and launch (Ch 3)
**Minimum viable ecosystem (MVE)** — the smallest ecosystem configuration that tests value streams, collaboration, and core value proposition (Ch 6)
**Minimum viable exploration metrics / MVM** — a small set (typically 4–5) of metrics that inform business decisions by measuring means relative to ends, applied before product/market fit (Ch 10)
**Model-based learning** — top-down learning from a pre-recognized model; parallels goal-directed reinforcement in humans (Ch 18)
**Model-free learning** — bottom-up learning from stored action rewards; parallels habit formation (Ch 18)
**Moonshot objective** — stretch target beyond what the team thinks possible; success is 60–70% achievement (Ch 2, Ch 15)
**Multidisciplinary team** — every member an expert advocating their specialization; solutions are often compromises (Ch 4)
**Naïve method** — forecasting using last period's actual values without adjustment; used for economic, financial time-series, and health metrics (Ch 14)
**Narrow AI** — AI equal to or surpassing human intelligence only in a very specific task (Ch 18)
**Neurodesign** — design paradigm in which design thinking teams and cognitive neuroscientists work as one team, using brain knowledge to measure creativity and design better offerings (Ch 17, Ch 19)
**Nilsson's Taxonomy of Creative Design (2011)** — imitation → variation → combination → transformation → innovative creation (Ch 12)
**North Star metric(s)** — usually a *set* of top-line metrics expressing growth ambition and direction; anchor of the whole IMS (Ch 9)
**Objective** — qualitative, outcome-oriented, ambitious, inspiring statement of what needs to be achieved (Ch 15)
**OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)** — performance measurement system sitting between KPIs, MBOs, and BSCs; invented by Andy Grove, brought to Google by John Doerr in the 1990s (Ch 15)
**OMTM (one metric that matters)** — the single early-incubation start-up metric (Ch 1)
**ONA (Organizational Network Analysis)** — structured method visualizing communication, information, and decision flow through nodes and links (Ch 4); AI-enabled active variant (Ch 18)
**Opportunity recognition** — exploration form with low initial uncertainty leading to incremental improvement; contrast value creation (Ch 7)
**Outcome metric** — result arising immediately after a defined project activity, e.g. new customers won with a new service (Ch 8)
**Output metric** — the immediate-term result, e.g. number of in-depth interviews conducted (Ch 8)
**Pearson's correlation coefficient** — strength of a linear relationship between two continuous variables (Ch 14)
**Persuasion** — the fifth P: convincing others the creative work merits support (Ch 12)
**Physiological condition mining** — training models to decode customer signals from ECoG, EEG, patch clamp, audio/video analytics (Ch 17)
**Point of view (PoV)** — synthesis statement (who / needs / in order to / because) reframing the challenge into an actionable HMW question (Ch 3, Ch 6)
**Portfolio vitality index** — percent of revenues from market opportunities realized in the last three years (Ch 13)
**Predictive alignment** — aligning organizational behavior with the North Star and objectives using predictive analytics (Ch 17)
**Predictive analysis** — analytics answering "what will happen?" (Ch 14)
**Prescriptive analysis** — analytics answering "what should be done?"; the most complex type (Ch 14)
**Problem statement** — "How might we (re)design [WHAT] for [WHOM], so that [THEIR NEED] is satisfied?" (Ch 6)
**Problem to Growth and Scale Framework** — the book's spine: PROBLEM → SOLUTION → TEST & VALIDATE → LAUNCH & EXECUTE → GROWTH, gated by problem/solution fit and product/market fit (Ch 1)
**Problem to Scale and Growth Innovation Funnel** — EXPLORATION → VALIDATE → PROVE → INVEST → SCALE, tracking design challenges and their validation steps (Ch 13)
**Process metric** — measures efficiency or productivity of the innovation process, e.g. time to complete validation of product/market fit (Ch 8)
**Project-based learning** — the delivery vehicle for capability building: missions to complete inside a capability programme (Ch 4)
**Q-learning** — model-free reinforcement learning without an end goal, learning by trial and error from action rewards (Ch 18)
**Question-Analyze-Builder** — design thinking tool used to sharpen questions before building data models (Ch 17)
**Requirements model** — creativity measured against criteria defined before the product is built; measures relevance, efficiency, value (Ch 12)
**Rhythmicability** — network effects and repetitions of interaction in a business ecosystem; a scaling focal point (Ch 6)
**Roofshot objective** — operational, short-term objective the team should achieve 100 percent (Ch 2, Ch 15)
**Sanity check (of a metric)** — ask what the team would do differently if the figure went up, down, or stayed flat; no answer means discard it (Ch 8)
**S-curve / Sigmoid curve** — the change-dynamics curve whose inflection point triggers the next IMS cycle and the second-curve decision (Ch 2, Ch 14)
**Second curve** — the new growth curve that must start *before* the first one peaks (Ch 14)
**Shadow players** — ONA nodes disconnected from the rest of the teams; an innovation and attrition risk (Ch 4)
**Skewness** — measure of a data set's symmetry; long left tail = negative, long right tail = positive (Ch 14)
**Spearman rank-order correlation** — strength of a monotonic relationship for ordinal or rank data (Ch 14)
**Strategic foresight** — looking 10+ years ahead and asking what customers will value; distinct from 2–3 year strategic planning (Ch 16)
**Systems/actors fit** — the ecosystem-design fit, realized as an MVE (Ch 6)
**Taxonomy of Creative Design** — see *Nilsson's Taxonomy* (Ch 12)
**Team-of-teams** — organizational form based on teams outperforming individuals in complex contexts because no individual comprehends the whole (Ch 4)
**Ties** — the formal and informal relationships between ONA nodes (Ch 4)
**T-shaped profile** — vertical bar of specialist skill plus horizontal bar of many cultures, disciplines, and systems (Ch 4)
**Value creation (exploration form)** — high initial uncertainty with unknown outcomes, embedded in a design thinking and growth mindset, leading to breakthrough innovation (Ch 7)
**Vanity metrics** — feel-good, marketing-friendly, easily visualized indicators giving no clear indication of concrete action; classics are number of ideas, number of patents, R&D spending, R&D headcount (Ch 1)
**Viability** — the long-term understanding that the project is worth the time and cost given all effort and potential returns (Ch 3)
**Vicarious reinforcement** — building causal models by observing the consequences of other people's behaviors; a design thinking capability ML lacks (Ch 18)
**VUCA** — volatility, uncertainty, complexity (also given as chaos), and ambiguity (Ch 15, Ch 19)
**Well-defined measurement** — a metric with a clear, precise designation and a universally valid definition; typical of EXPLOIT (Ch 7)
**Willingness-to-pay (WTP) analysis** — pricing validation via discrete choice / conjoint analysis, yielding a market demand curve and price elasticity (Ch 6)
**Working capital metrics** — current assets minus current liabilities; indicator of immediately available capital (Ch 11)
**XYZ hypothesis** — "At least X% of target market Y will Z"; the expected-result format for probability-of-success experiments (Ch 6)
