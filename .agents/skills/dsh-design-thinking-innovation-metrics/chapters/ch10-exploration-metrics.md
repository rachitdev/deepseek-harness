# Chapter 10 — Working with Exploration Metrics

*(printed pp. 175–186)*

## Core Idea

**Minimum Viable Exploration Metrics (MVM)** — the term "has been deliberately chosen in order to
distinguish this type of metric from all the other efforts and existing key performance
indicators typically circulating in organizations."

Why they work: they avoid "overwhelming the patience of decision makers, while providing the
necessary space, time, and appropriate framework for transformation and creating radical new
ideas and solutions."

The chapter's investor analogy: "The decision-making and support process can be compared to that
of a **venture capital firm** looking for evidence of value creation, delivery, and capture in
different rounds of investing. This starts with the evidence and confidence of the team in
working on a real customer problem and continues to the product/market fit to the team/product
fit for the later implementation of the solution."

And the virtuous cycle: "The more the design thinking and innovation teams can minimize the risk
in their actions and throughout the design cycle, the more trust is created, and the support and
sponsorship activities increase."

The chapter's second half introduces **kill metrics** — "part of the impact comes from stopping
innovation projects, initiatives, and design challenges."

## Frameworks Introduced

### The MVM lifecycle — Preparation → Introduction → Integration

```
PREPARATION  →  INTRODUCTION  →  INTEGRATION
  ├ Already known metrics & measurements
  ├ Experimenting with a minimum viable set of metrics
       SET UP EXPERIMENT  (supported by data analysis process)
       MEASURE
       EVALUATION
       ADAPTION
       OPERATIONALIZATION  → part of a meaningful measurement system
```

Definition printed on the diagram: **"Minimum viable metrics (MVMs) inform business decisions by
measuring means relative to ends. They are best applied before product/market fit, with a strong
focus on learning and improving."**

### The MVM Canvas

| | **PREPARATION** | **INTRODUCTION / ACTIVATION** | **ADOPTION / INTEGRATION** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Row 1** | **Problem to tackle** — What is the focus of the exploration metrics? Which problems or challenges are addressed? How has it been measured so far? | **Vision & innovation strategy** — How to envision the future? What are the North Star metrics? What is the innovation strategy? What are important capabilities to be developed? | **Conclusions / next steps** — Derive questions, indicators, and actions from the results of measurement. What are the most important findings from measuring? Can the measurement scenario be adapted? |
| **Row 2** | **Decision maker / team** — Who does the metric help? At what level does the measurement have the most effect? | **Design of actionable metrics** — What is the best way to describe the metrics? Formulate the potential metrics as simple, understandable **questions** for the team/decision maker | **Operationalize** — How often are measurements taken and does the series of measurements have an expiration date? How are the results visualized, shared, and communicated? Is there a possibility to automate the measurement? |
| **Row 3** | **Potential activities** — Which activities might lead to new success in behavior, culture, mindset, and innovation derived from the metrics? | **Measure** — Run measurement from leading and/or lagging indicators and measure at the appropriate level (process, input, or outcome). **Costs, schedule, & frequency** — What are the costs of measuring and how frequent/long should measuring be? | **Evaluate** — What should be learned from the initial measurement? How can the metrics be gradually improved? Have the metrics enough impact to be operationalized? Compare the outcomes between teams or similar projects. **Summary of the impact measuring metrics led to.** |

Header field: *"Name of minimum viable metrics — give the metrics a name or call them **X** as a
starting point."*

**The count heuristic:** "Practical experience shows that initially **four to five metrics** are
sufficient to see if better decisions can be made."

### Kill Metrics

> "Starting something isn't the hard part — **killing an idea is.**"
> "Defining success and evaluating the confidence levels makes it easier to make the tough
> decisions before it's too late."

Key mechanism: "Metrics like **insight velocity** support decision makers by **separating the
success of each idea (ROI) from the success of the team.** This means innovation and design
thinking teams can be successful by showing that an innovation initiative will be unsuccessful."

The named practice: **"Google, for example, uses consequent kill metrics in selected design
challenges with moonshot ambitions."** At the start of such a challenge "the design thinking team
is encouraged to define several kill metrics as measurable criteria that determine whether an
otherwise promising project should be terminated."

The canonical kill-metric form the book prints: **"If X hasn't happened until $1, we'll shut it
down."**

Why they work: kill metrics "help bring teams back to the facts, as the riskiest parts of a
design challenge are identified from the beginning, **before the team becomes too emotionally
engaged**," and they "help direct the available budget from the initiatives that generate real
value."

### The Realism / Optimism Matrix
Kill metrics need "a healthy balance between the optimistic and visionary attitude of design
thinking and an enthusiastic skepticism that critically questions the visionary ideas with the
possibilities of reality." The chapter's 2×2 (Realistic ↔ Unrealistic × Pessimism ↔ Optimism):

| | Pessimism | Optimism |
|---|---|---|
| **Realistic** | *"We are not able to create a solution for the problem statement."* | ✅ *"We will figure out by iterating and stop if the kill metrics apply."* |
| **Unrealistic** | *"We never realized any market opportunity and we will fail again."* | *"We will succeed anyhow by spending all available resources."* |

## Key Concepts

- **Metrics have lifespans.** "Some of these metrics will have a short lifespan as the initial
  measurement goal is achieved after a while. Other metrics will turn out to be very useful and
  will be operationalized later. Yet other metrics will not meaningfully improve anything,
  provide insights for concrete actions, or be useful in measuring progress."
- **Combine old and new.** "The application of exploration metrics usually combines already
  established, powerful, approved metrics with the experimental minimum viable exploration
  metrics."
- **Two domains of application** — "minimum viable exploration metrics find application in
  measuring progress and effectiveness for **both projects/business as well as teams/culture**."
- **Purpose statement.** "The primary purpose of experimenting with minimum viable metrics is to
  **positively change behavior and innovation work, and not to further optimize it or limit its
  degree of freedom.**"
- **Teams should be able to finish their own ideas.** "It has also proven to be good when teams
  finish their ideas themselves as soon as there is evidence for it. A culture and reward system
  that supports such behavior does not put the team in the position of having to finish something
  by hook or by crook."
- **"Failure is success"** — innovation work is defined by the saying.

## Mental Models

- **Measure means relative to ends**, not activity volume.
- **Stopping generates immediate ROI.** "Realizing and informing that an innovation initiative
  will not be successful generates immediate ROI simply by stopping the project."
- **Evidence has a ceiling.** "Caution is warranted, as an **overly evidence-based approach can
  also inhibit creativity, out-of-box thinking, and particularly radical innovation, thus killing
  great opportunities for market opportunity in the nucleus.** For true innovation, which by
  definition means something novel or new, it is often difficult to bring in evidence or rigorous
  research to support this."

## Anti-patterns

- **"Define Success" without measurability.** "Most innovation projects and design thinking
  challenges use tools like Define Success to start the project. However, they usually **lack
  measurability and thus remain without effect throughout the entire design cycle**."
- **Emotional over-investment before risk identification.**
- **Gaming.** Part of the evaluation stage is explicitly "apply them and see if the team will game
  the metrics or accept it."

## Reference Tables

### A minimum viable set of exploration metrics helps the team…
- to define meaningful leading indicators and build a measurement system step by step
- to identify metrics that allow the later **operationalization of real-time analysis** for
  actions on the ground
- to iteratively identify the most pressing problems and challenges to solve with regard to
  culture, collaboration, processes, and so on
- to overcome the dilemma of using only one kind of measurement for improving, for example,
  process efficiency

### Typical Kill Metrics
| # | Kill metric |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Early customer feedback** on various aspects of the vision prototype, which might influence replacing it with existing alternatives |
| 2 | **Customers' willingness to pay** (with different monetization options) for an experience, service, or product has no evidence for significant revenues |
| 3 | **Very high costs** for creating a first functional prototype, or expenditures exceeding the maximum available budget |
| 4 | **Critical functions and experiences do not meet quality and quantity expectations** for realizing the solution |
| 5 | **Technology is not ready** for realizing the prototype, or other constraints and dependencies that cannot be changed at present |

metrics · operationalize, apply advanced analytics · measure ecosystem (MVE), business, and

measurement system and automate if possible · measure product (MVP), business, and innovation
success on lagging outcome metrics · benchmark across the organization or different locations
globally · measure impact of new performance measurement systems.

**The six recommended actions the overview supports:** ensure strategic alignment of design
thinking and innovation activities with business and innovation strategy (North Star) · identify
where to build capabilities · create a basis for communicating value of design thinking and
innovation activities · inform resource allocation decisions · establish validation of progress
made and basis for corrective actions · optimize processes, frameworks, and the application of
tools and methods.

## Worked Example

### Designing a minimum viable exploration metric — the needfinding case, all seven steps

**The proposition to prove:** *"The team intends to prove that needfinding efforts have a positive
impact on the success of a new product."* → `NEEDFINDING → INNOVATION SUCCESS`

**Step 1 — Assumptions about a potential MVM in conjunction with needfinding and product success**
- Design thinking teams with more insights about the customer/user have a better chance of success.
- To get more insights about the customer/user, design thinking teams need to conduct customer
  research and in-depth interviews.
- → **"As a result, the more research and needfinding interviews, the better the likelihoods of
  success."**

**Step 2 — What is the vision/North Star and principles of the innovation strategy?**
The core principles of the innovation strategy are:
1. Innovation comes from everywhere
2. Focus on the customer/user
3. De-risk through early experiments and prototypes
→ **"Needfinding is a core element which contributes to two out of three principles."**

**Step 3 — Design of potential metrics leading to actions**
- **Metric 1:** Measuring velocity of needfinding interviews (**customer interview velocity**)
- **Metric 2:** Measuring velocity of conducting insights (**insight velocity**)
- "Both might be leading indicators of success."

Formulated as simple, understandable team questions:
- → *"Did the design thinking team conduct **at least five needfinding interviews this week**?
  Yes or no? How many?"*
- → *"Did the customer interviews generate any insights this week? Yes or no? **How useful are
  the insights on a scale from 1 to 10?**"*

**Step 4 — Start measuring** (four teams on the same design challenge globally for local markets)

| Team | Useful insights generated as % of time engaging with customers/users |
|---|---|
| Design Thinking Team A | **40%** |
| Design Thinking Team B | **30%** |
| Design Thinking Team C | **70%** |
| Design Thinking Team D | **55%** |

**Step 5 — Evaluate actionable metrics** (over the entire design thinking cycle)
→ "The measurement shows a **wide range** in terms of the conversion of engagements with
customers and valuable insights."

**Step 6 — Discuss the impact of operationalizing the metrics**
→ "The team has noted that the **week-over-week data should be aggregated into a two-week-sprint
rolling metric** (based on changing process steps and different focus in each Double Diamond)."
→ "The metrics provide an **early warning sign if the team is not leaving the comfort zone** by
engaging with potential customers/users."

**Step 7 — Derive questions, indicators, and actions from the results**
Question asked: *"What is the cause of Team D's better insights compared to Team A and B's lower
scores?"*
Candidate causes to interrogate: **cultural environment? · engagement type (physical versus
virtual)? · limited resources? · maturity of the team?**
Example actions:
- "Hands-on project coaching to upskill teams with more needfinding techniques and professional
  ethnographic research methods"
- "Continue to apply the metrics based on a defined setting from step 6 to see if the number of
  useful insights increases, or test other potential actions for a defined time."

### The kill-metric ROI case — the Mega Metaverse Wellness Oasis Ecosystem

**The economics the book states:**
- A typical business ecosystem design project takes **at least a year** to go from problem
  definition → product/market fit → first minimum viable ecosystem (MVE).
- The assumed cost is **at least $2.2 million** in resources.
- By applying heavy ethnographic research, collecting insights, and creating many points of view
  as a basis for rapid prototyping and testing, "the team might find out that the problem to
  solve **does not even exist**, or potential solutions are not fitting customer needs."
- Defining a powerful kill metric early means "the initial exploration might have cost only
  **$200,000, instead of spending $2.2 million over an entire year.**"
- → **"The work of the design thinking team is returning $2 million to the business that would
  have otherwise been burnt without any business impact."**

**The design challenge:** *"How Might We Design the Mega Metaverse Wellness Oasis Ecosystem"*

**The finding:** "Early customer/user feedback and first prototypes to test with neurotech
revealed that virtual experiences related to wellness have **limited acceptance** and the impact
on well-being is **less intensive than physical or hybrid experiences**."

**The three kill metrics defined for it:**

| # | Kill metric | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Improvements of mental and physical health | **by 30%** |
| 2 | Customer/user acceptance to visit | **every week** |
| 3 | Willingness to pay for treatment (based on immersion of light, sound, travel, social interaction from different communities) | **> $45** |

**The book's own summary:** "Beginning at the beginning, with the design thinking mindset, tools,
and methods as well as the set of metrics for the minimum viable exploration, saves money by
aligning the design challenge to the kill metrics and not taking a full year to validate market
acceptance. In the example outlined, based on easy-to-understand metrics, only **$200,000 is
spent and $2 million in savings are realized.**"

## Key Takeaways

1. Start with **four to five** minimum viable exploration metrics — no more.
2. Express each metric as a simple yes/no + magnitude question the team answers weekly.
3. Customer interview velocity and insight velocity are the book's two canonical exploration
   metrics.
4. Aggregate weekly data into a two-week-sprint rolling metric to smooth phase changes.
5. Define kill metrics with explicit thresholds at the *start* of a challenge, before emotional
   investment.
6. Kill metrics separate idea ROI from team success — a team can succeed by proving an initiative
   will fail.
7. Guard against over-evidencing: radical innovation cannot always produce evidence up front.
