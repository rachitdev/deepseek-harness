# Chapter 13 — Reflecting Measurements: Measurement Options for Each Level of EXPLORE

*(printed pp. 211–222)*

## Core Idea

A level-by-level catalogue of what to actually measure in EXPLORE — individual, team,
organization/culture, design challenge/project, portfolio, business ecosystem, and the transition
to scale. The chapter's framing warning:

> "The measurements presented here should serve as inspiration for creating new exploration
> metrics, but **they should by no means simply be adopted for the organization or the team
> without question.** … the central question always is, **why should something be measured and
> what decisions can be derived from it.** Also, how much is the additional information worth and
> what impact will it create?"

The chapter quotes Peter Drucker: *"Unless we determine what shall be measured and what the
yardstick of measurement in an area will be, the area itself will not be seen."*

And it explicitly rejects the tidy matrix: **"A matrix and a meticulous breakdown of measurements
according to stakeholder and function in the company is not recommended,"** because transparency,
openness, and a moderated EXPLORE→EXPLOIT transition require understanding the different
measurement worlds rather than partitioning them.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The seven measurement levels (bottom to top)
`Individual → Team → Organization/Culture → Design Challenge/Project → Portfolio → Business
Ecosystems → Transition to Scale`

Split into two families:
- **Team and culture** (individual, team, organizational culture) — "mainly about the measurement
  of human behavior, mindsets, and capabilities to be developed"
- **Project and business** (design challenge/project, portfolio, business and innovation
  ecosystem) — "from measurements on the tool level to the adjustment of the balance between
  EXPLORE and EXPLOIT in the portfolio"

> "The map of exploration metrics and measurements will look different for every organization and
> team."

### The Problem to Scale and Growth Innovation Funnel

Stages: `EXPLORATION → VALIDATE → PROVE → INVEST → SCALE`

| Stage | Basis | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| **Exploration** | Customer need (problem identified) | — |
| **Validate** | Problem/solution fit | Validate hypotheses |
| **Prove** | Product/market fit | Test MVP/MVE |
| **Invest** | Talent to value | Agile delivery |
| **Scale** | — | Accelerate growth |

Fed by two inputs — **today's customer needs** and **tomorrow's customer needs** — with a
**discontinued initiatives and design challenges** exit at every stage.

"In contrast to a stage-gate process for innovation, the Problem to Scale and Growth Innovation
Funnel has the purpose of **tracking the developments of different design challenges and
initiatives and documenting the respective validation steps**."

**Decision rights:** "Once the innovation teams have been given the freedom to innovate radically
or the mandate to explore within the North Star, the only confidence needed is in the team and
its capabilities throughout the design cycle. This approach allows that **the decisions are made
by the team itself**, which has the most information about the customer, market, and willingness
to pay. This approach works best when decision makers and boards **allocate funds according to
their risk tolerance, leaving the persevere, pivot, or kill decision to the team.**"

### Innovation Ecosystem vs Business Ecosystem
| | **Innovation ecosystem** | **Business ecosystem** |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | "A loosely connected network of actors collaborating with the goal of mostly creating innovations related to a specific technology"; purpose is new solutions through co-creation and a shared set of knowledge and skills | Companies apply the business ecosystem design methodology "to collaboratively create new value propositions for customers/users **orchestrated by one or two actors** in the system" |
| Typical form | Clusters — e.g. **the FinTech cluster in Singapore**, **the IT security cluster in Tel Aviv** | Very often builds on existing innovation, transaction, or knowledge ecosystems |
| What is measured | Effectiveness | Performance |

## Key Concepts

- **Do not use individual OKRs in EXPLORE.** "In the case of OKRs, it is recommended **not** to
  agree on individual OKRs in EXPLORE, as different skills and collaborative efforts are usually
  required to be successful."
- **Linear vs exponential organizations.** "Research also shows that **linear organizations have
  a linear function of employees and profit, while exponential organizations have an exponential
  function of employees and profit.**"
- **Innovation self-efficacy score** — "developed by **Berny Roth from Stanford University**. The
  approach measures the growth mindset in relation to design thinking." Because motivated EXPLORE
  team members usually already have a growth mindset, it is recommended to add "an internally
  judged measure of **outcome value and novelty**, based on an average of anonymous ratings
  provided by the team members."
- **Growth mindset scale** — "measuring individuals based on the **three-item growth mindset
  scale (Dweck, 2006, 2016)**."
- **Culture typologies** — "more sophisticated measurement systems apply typologies of
  organizations ranging, for example, from a culture of **adhocracy, clan-thinking, and hierarchy
  to market culture**. The competing values are mapped against organizational dimensions
  referring to **internal–external** and **stability–flexibility**, leading to the four
  organizational types that indicate whether a culture fits, for example, EXPLORE with attributes
  of **dynamic, entrepreneurial, and people orientation**."
- **Service design self-assessment** — "a five-level scoring system (0–4) ranging from good
  service to bad service … applied to validate existing and (re)designed services based on
  pre-defined user criteria (e.g. easy to find) and performance criteria (e.g. respond rate to
  change)."
- **Breakthrough rate.** "Design thinking and innovation teams of large organizations with a high
  maturity in terms of mindset, processes, and tools usually realize **one or two breakthrough
  innovations over a given time, based on hundreds of design challenges.** Teams with a low
  maturity and limited appropriate skills and measurement systems need even more effort, time,
  and failure."

## Mental Models

- **Diffuse measurement still counts.** "Regardless of how diffuse the measurement is, it is still
  a measurement if it provides the team or organization with more information about the
  phenomenon than previously known."
- **Culture is a reflection of leadership.** "The assumptions and beliefs of each team member
  drive behavior, and the collective behavior of all teams determines the results. … Culture can
  also be seen as a direct reflection of organizational leadership."
- **Implementation is a multiplier, not an add-on.** "Any final prototype, MVP or MVE is only a
  multiplier of well-planned implementation and professional scaling activities."

## Anti-patterns

- **Forced rankings and vitality curves.** "Individual measurements based on forced rankings or
  vitality curves do not fit the purpose of creating high-performing innovation teams and a
  culture of U-shaped teams. Consequently, **it is not recommended to rank employees by asking
  managers to just create a list of the best to the worst employee**, as a way of measuring
  individual performance."
- **Copying another organization's metric map.**
- **Keeping the EXPLORE portfolio amateur** while EXPLOIT is professionally managed.

## Reference Tables

### Examples of measuring individuals
- Tracking activities performed by a team member to reach the goal
- Tracking of individuals, timelines, progress, and confidence level contributing to the design challenge
- Tracking revenues per employee (new products versus existing products)
- Soliciting **360-degree feedback** (preferred for individuals with customer interaction) or simplified **180-degree feedback**
- Using **human capital innovation ROI** to assess the value of human capital (knowledge, habits, social and personal attributes) related to the work in EXPLORE
- Measuring individuals based on the **three-item growth mindset scale (Dweck, 2006, 2016)**
- Learning and development of new skills based on qualitative feedback about behavioral change
- Increasing knowledge and skill of the individual based on learning, training programs, or project-based learning activities

*Measuring individuals on behavior and mindset helps:* to understand whether individuals are team
players · identify entrepreneurial behavior and attitudes · extend the view of skills and
capabilities based on T-shaped profiles · learn about individual thinking preferences to create a
holistic thinking pattern in a team · detect tendencies toward a fixed or growth mindset.

### Examples of measuring teams
- Team diversity and degree of interdisciplinary capability within the team
- Ratio of people with design, ethnographic, and user research skills compared to other team members
- Increase in speed of innovation based on new mindset and behavior
- Time needed for the team to make decisions to achieve the right impact, at the right time, with the right intent
- Degree of trust between teams and top management
- Number of conflicts within the team that were resolved constructively
- Measuring the **authenticity** of team members in workshops, meetings, and bilateral conversations
- Measure **learning agility** determined by applying reflective thinking and tracking of the changes instigated within the team
- Propensity to initiate conversations in which the status quo is challenged, based on regular rounds of feedback
- Whether all team members are present and connected during workshops and meetings — "indicators include phones are turned off, interruptions and repetitions are kept to a minimum, and details are memorable for everyone"

### Examples of organization / culture metrics
- C-levels/decision makers have available calendar time for EXPLORE
- Number of employees performing extra and on-top activities to explore new market opportunities
- Number of employees attending capability build programs in design thinking and applying learning in project work
- Amount of improved internal collaboration and knowledge sharing based on team-of-teams setting
- Talent benefits based on the impact on recruiting and retaining talent, triggered e.g. by externalized showcasing of innovation success
- Satisfaction and purpose score of teams and individuals
- Measuring work performed between exploring, exploiting, and sustaining the business
- Openness and co-creation activities with potential customers and users in problem space and solution space
- **Ratio between number of design challenges and breakthrough innovations realized**

### Design challenge / project metrics, by design thinking phase
| Phase | Metrics |
|---|---|
| **Understand** | Number and quality of assumptions tested; number and quality of hypotheses developed; participation rate of relevant stakeholders in co-creation |
| **Observe** | Number of intentional versus unintentional learnings; depth of interactions with customers/users |
| **PoV** | Number and depth of new insights derived from understanding, observing, and testing |
| **Ideate** | Quality of ideas generated; diversity of applied creativity techniques |
| **Prototype** | Number of prototypes built and distributed over the exploration grid |
| **Test** | Number of intentional versus unintentional learnings; overall efficacy of prototypes built to prove assumptions/hypotheses; number and depth of customer interviews and tests per cycle |

Cross-cutting: team confidence about problem to solve or prototype to build · time to
problem/solution fit or product/market fit · time-to-market · **improved assumptions-to-knowledge
ratio** · usability and customer effort scores · adoption rate of features contributing to value
proposition.

### Portfolio, funnel health, and strategic alignment metrics
- Number of innovation initiatives or design challenges per playing field
- Money invested per design challenge over the entire design cycle
- **Number of design challenges killed based on kill metrics**
- Number of pivot or preserve decisions made per design challenge and/or over the entire portfolio
- **Number of portfolio/funnel decisions made by the design thinking team versus top-down decisions**
- Ratio between initiatives based on current customer needs versus future customer needs
- Percent of original total of customer problems (today and tomorrow) observed in the market
- Size comparison of initiatives based on potential innovation success and impact
- Percent of portfolio elements moving along the problem to scale and growth framework
- Percent of portfolio elements aligned with North Star
- **Portfolio vitality index** — percent of revenues from realized market opportunities over the last three years
- **Expected commercial value of the portfolio** (risk-adjusted NPV)

### Ecosystem metrics
| **Innovation ecosystem** | **Business ecosystem** |
|---|---|
| Estimated savings of innovation ecosystem initiatives relative to comparable costs for in-house problem-solving | System/actor fit (realization of MVE) |
| Increase in corporate-wide open innovation culture through collaboration of innovation ecosystems | Engagement level of core actors in the system to provide the value proposition |
| Increase of reputation among other actors in the innovation ecosystem | Number of new active customers obtaining services, products, and experiences from the business ecosystem |
| Degree of top management commitment to open innovation initiatives | Created ecosystem capital based on e.g. number of actors, value streams, and reach to customers |
| Degree of freedom given to innovation teams to establish search fields beyond the North Star | Satisfaction of business ecosystem actors; customer satisfaction with delivery of value proposition |
| Customer benefits from innovations created and/or provided by the actors | Contribution margin per transaction; revenue per customer/user |
| Expected increase of revenues from new customers/users as percentage of total sales based on collaboration with actors | Retention costs per customer and/or actor; acquisition costs per customer and/or actor; share of revenue from expansion of core value proposition |

### Structural moves for the EXPLORE→EXPLOIT transition — with the book's numbers
| Move | The book's stated figure |
|---|---|
| Tie board/CEO compensation to exploration metrics | **Up to 20 percent** of compensation tied to measurements in the EXPLORE portfolio and/or impact of EXPLORE activities |
| Leadership time reserved for EXPLORE | Responsible leaders reserve **between 50–100 percent** of available time for support, alignment, financing, and measuring of EXPLORE |
| Nominate a co-CEO concerned only with EXPLORE | Some companies "divide the CEO position between two people who manage EXPLORE and EXPLOIT, respectively" — aligned North Star or two different North Stars, different cultures, but aligned to moderate the transition together |
| Budget allocation rule of thumb | **10 percent of current revenues and 1 percent of profits** should be allocated to EXPLORE activities (excluding R&D in technology) to stay on track in the medium to long term — "these references vary depending on the industry and market dynamics" |

## Worked Example

### The innovation funnel with the book's own conversion figures

The chapter prints a fully numbered funnel example. Starting cohort: **200 customer
problems / design challenges.**

| Stage | Number of customer problems / design challenges | Percent moving along the framework | Percent of original total observed in the market |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exploration** (customer need / problem identified) | **200** | — | 100% |
| **Validate** (problem/solution fit) | **70** | **35%** | — |
| **Prove** (test MVP/MVE, product/market fit) | **25** | **35%** | **12.5%** |
| **Invest** (agile delivery, talent to value) | **5** | **20%** | **2.5%** |
| **Scale** (accelerate growth) | **≈2** | **~10%** | **1%** |

Read down the left column: 200 → 70 → 25 → 5 → ~2. Read the stage-to-stage conversion:
35% → 35% → 20% → ~10%. Read the survival against the original cohort: 100% → 12.5% → 2.5% → 1%.

This is the quantitative statement of the earlier claim that mature teams "realize one or two
breakthrough innovations over a given time, based on hundreds of design challenges."
The funnel's own instruction sits alongside it: **"Understand customer/user needs and create
value for them!"**

### The value-creation × implementation multiplier

The chapter's rule of thumb "demonstrates what the impact of excellence in value creation, and
implementation might have on growth and scale":

```
Excellent Product/Market Fit = 10        Excellent Implementation = ×1 Million
Randomly Selected Idea       =  1   ×    Poor Implementation      = ×1
```

Worked out:

| Combination | Value |
|---|---|
| Randomly selected idea with poor implementation | **1 USD** |
| Validated ideas with poor implementation | **10 USD** |
| Excellent product/market fit with good implementation | **10 Million USD** |

The lesson the chapter attaches: validating the idea buys a 10× improvement; **implementation
quality is the millionfold multiplier.** Hence "knowing how to bring it home is key to success."

## Key Takeaways

1. Measure at the level that matches the decision; there is no universal priority ordering across
   the seven levels.
2. Individuals: measure behavior and mindset, not ranked output. No individual OKRs in EXPLORE.
3. Teams: trust, constructively resolved conflicts, decision speed, learning agility,
   authenticity, presence.
4. Portfolio: track kills, pivots, team-made vs top-down decisions, portfolio vitality index, and
   risk-adjusted NPV.
5. Give the persevere/pivot/kill decision to the team; boards allocate funds by risk tolerance.
6. Structural transition levers: ~20% of exec comp on EXPLORE metrics, 50–100% of leader time,
   10% of revenues and 1% of profits to EXPLORE.
7. Expect roughly 1% of identified problems to reach scale; implementation quality is the
   dominant multiplier.
