# Chapter 17 — Data-Driven Innovation 301: Analytics & the Hybrid Model

*(printed pp. 310–334)*

## Core Idea

The hybrid model is "the first attempt to combine big data analytics and design thinking to solve
wicked and ill-defined problems." The division of labour is stated crisply:

> "While design thinking is still the main cornerstone to find out about the **WHY**, with data
> analytics, decisions are based on what future customers really want. **Together they ensure
> that risky bets are not made based on instinct only, but also on evidence.**"

The complementarity runs both ways: "data science can help designers think outside the box,
providing relevant inspiration and alternative points of view. Moreover, **design thinking is
applied for data analytics, to identify the appropriate questions** for data analytics."

The limit: **"even the most advanced technology is still not able to replace the ability to show
empathy or tap into human intuition and experience."**

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Data-Driven Innovation stack
Ordered from raw to advanced (low → high interdisciplinary team effort, low → high impact on
innovation):

`RAW DATA → CLEAN DATA → STANDARD REPORTS → AD HOC QUERIES → DATA FILTERING → CLUSTERING →
TREND, FORECASTING → STATISTICAL ANALYSIS → PREDICTIVE ANALYTICS → MACHINE LEARNING →
ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE → DEEP LEARNING → ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORKS`

with the three bands: **DATA MANAGEMENT** → **DESIGN THINKING & BIG DATA ANALYTICS** →
**DESIGN THINKING & ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE / NEUROSCIENCE**.

The capability layer stack printed elsewhere in the chapter (bottom to top):
`STATISTICS → DESIGN THINKING → SYSTEMS THINKING → DATA ANALYTICS → HYBRID MODEL →
MACHINE LEARNING → BASIC AI → DEEP LEARNING`

> "AI and machine learning for creativity and innovation are very different from the established
> areas where AI has replaced traditional operational tasks."

### The discipline overlap
"The terms and fields of data-driven innovation are not always clear. Rather, the individual
disciplines overlap and serve each other. In the context of this book, **data-driven innovation
requires the intersection of design thinking and data science.**" The Venn the chapter draws:
**computer science · mathematics · machine learning · statistical model · neuroscience · design
thinking.** "AI is defined as computer science, but uses statistical methods as part of its
process."

**Neurodesign** is defined here as "a design paradigm in which design thinking teams and
cognitive neuroscientists work as a team, using knowledge of what makes the human brain tick to
understand how creativity and design thinking are measured."

### The Hybrid Mindset — eight elements
1. **Combine human and data insights** over the entire design cycle (e.g. development of points of view)
2. **Combine an analytical and intuitive way of thinking** in a holistic approach
3. **Encourage the team to be interested in the unknown** and create clarity through a creative and analytical approach
4. **Accept uncertainty at the beginning** of the design challenge and interpret correlations in the context of the user
5. **Create an environment that allows you to merge expertise and mutual inspiration** from human insights and visualized data from T-shaped team members
6. **Apply an optimistic mindset** and perform iterations throughout the design cycles for both thinking states (design thinking and data analytics)
7. **Generate stories** from data, experiences, customer feedback, and innovation metrics, and mirror the results in prototypes and experiments
8. **Reflect constantly** on the approach and enhance the data capabilities step by step; **learn and expand the toolbox** of creative and analytical tools constantly, experimenting with new measurements, technology, and ways of generating evidence

### The evolution of Design Thinking & Data Analytics (2005 → 2040)

| Contribution | What it delivers |
|---|---|
| Combining the toolbox of data analytics and human-centered design | Understanding the customer from a different perspective |
| Increasing the efficiency and effectiveness of the innovation process | Improving the decision confidence |
| Combining deep customer insights and deep learning from data | Smoothing, validation, and alignment of insights |
| Generating synergies by combining the qualitative aspects of design thinking and the quantitative methods of data science | Validating assumptions and justified inferences; proofing of solutions based on intuition and enhanced evidence; contextualizing data in order to link data insights with stories; accelerating the iterative problem-solving process |

Named variants: **design thinking for data analytics** · **data-enhanced design thinking** ·
**hybrid model** (combined process of design thinking and data science, based on hybrid mindset
and toolkit) · future: technology-aided design and business decisions based on data science, AI,
ML, in combination with neurodesign.

### The Hybrid Process — data mining mapped onto the Double Diamond

| Design phase | Data science technique applied |
|---|---|
| Problem space (understand/observe) | **Focus on options** · **Focus on physiological conditions (neurodesign)** · **Focus on processes** |
| Point of view | **Focus on insights** · **applying clustering** · **applying data visualization** |
| Ideate/prototype | **Applying generative models** |
| Test | **Focus on feedback** · **focus on physiological conditions (neurodesign)** · **focus on change and dynamics** |

Detail on each focus:
- **Focus on options / feedback / change and dynamics** — "data mining aims to extract and analyze
  customer/user responses. The analyses range from simple queries of current satisfaction with a
  service or product to influences of social media on current and future offerings. For
  optimization and redesign… comments about products on marketplaces can be extracted and tested
  for correlation with ratings using **factor analysis**."
- **Focus on physiological conditions (neurodesign)** — "**Physiological condition mining** trains
  models to decode customer/user signals. Here, the ML first learns from a data set and then
  predicts responses from new data collected from customers/users. In a laboratory setting, these
  signals can come from **electrocorticography (ECoG), electroencephalography (EEG), patch clamp
  recordings**, and also from more accessible means, such as **audio and video analytics**. For
  example, video analytics and **eye tracking** can be used to track eye movement to measure
  attention. Such measurements are particularly easy to perform in **virtual settings where
  customers/users are already using VR devices.**" Also: **kinetics** to analyze communication in
  a consultation or sales talk; **QR codes** with product information or **RFIDs** to understand
  customer actions.
- **Focus on processes** — "data can be collected analogously to the current customer journey or
  at specific touchpoints… **Comparing the actual journey with the journey designed in the
  business ecosystem** can help prioritize actions." Examples cited: data on specific traffic
  congestion and frequency of critical infrastructure to movement patterns.
- **Focus on insights (PoV)** — "the insights of the data scientists and the insights of the
  design thinkers often come together. Additionally, data mining can help to neutrally examine and
  process the gained insights and give corresponding **predictions about the relevance**."
- **Generative models** — "alternative views can be generated quickly and efficiently. Based on
  previous design thinking challenges, new prototypes and variants are generated… used for
  wireframe sketches in digital interactions. **User testing (A/B testing) also makes use of
  generative models that, in conjunction with eye tracking, can simulate where the human gaze
  tends to orient itself** for certain prototypes. The clusters that are generated from the data
  often provide insights into new categories that were previously unseen."

### Data-science techniques per design thinking phase (the chapter's per-phase list)
| Phase | Techniques |
|---|---|
| **Understand** | Process mapping; structure cross check; correlations / quantitative insights |
| **Observe** | Video analytics; heatmaps of customer/user interaction; chat analytics tools |
| **Define PoV** | Correlation between pain points and [data] |
| **Ideate** | Hyperparameter optimization; digital process re-design |
| **Prototype** | Behavioral models; data acquisition strategies |
| **Test** | Deepfake; large-scale testing; measuring outcomes / acceptance / churn |

### Enhancing Innovation Metrics with predictive analytics

**The key inversion:**
> "The development of metrics is based on the initial identification of the problem, and in the
> second step, which is crucial for this approach, **new metrics are created from the data instead
> of defining or adapting metrics based on assumptions.** These data-based metrics are used again
> for analysis of the targeting purposes to achieve the desired effect in terms of EXPLORE or
> EXPLOIT. **The data enables new ways of looking at the problem that go beyond the imagination
> and the current prevailing assumptions.**"

**Predictive alignment** — "incorporating predictive alignment changes assumptions and metrics
that align organizational behavior with North Star, company, and team objectives."

"The basic principle of the learning loops remains the same in the predictive procedures, but
**the test of the respective hypotheses is not performed backwards, but based on predictive,
prescriptive, and machine learning principles.**"

## Key Concepts

- **The expansion of accountability to include data.** "The design thinking and innovation team
  will be accountable not only for their performance on a metric or key result, but also for **how
  well the measurement system itself is able to test existing assumptions and formulate new
  hypotheses.**"
- **Transparency with respect to analytics tools.** "The risk of organizational misalignment
  increases with **unequal access to advanced analytics**. Unequal access can reinforce cultural
  differences, fuel employee dissatisfaction, and undermine the alignment of employee behavior
  with strategic outcomes."
- **The four transparency moves:** transparency and remove silos · allow self-service access to
  analytic tools · make data simple and accessible · create data-originated metrics → drive more
  intelligence faster than ever before.
- **Skills required for the hybrid approach:** probability and statistics · structured query
  language · statistical programming (e.g. R or Python) · ML · data management · data
  visualization (e.g. Tableau) · econometrics.
- **The Question-Analyze-Builder** — the design thinking tool named for helping data scientists
  ask the right questions "before building data models and performing analysis."
- **Teams working with data-driven innovation transition faster.** "Companies which already
  focused on customer behavior and applied data-driven innovation tools are managing the
  transition much faster than other companies."
- **Data ethics is not a bolt-on.** "Data ethics is part of the **entire development cycle** from
  problem identification, implementation, and ecosystem realization to scaling… It is the
  foundation for good evidence-based innovation work and part of the hybrid mindset. It is also
  part of a data-driven leadership culture and well-defined governance structures across corporate
  boundaries."

## Mental Models

- **No boilerplate recipe.** "Even when applying the hybrid model, there is no boilerplate recipe."
- **Mixed teams win.** "Experience shows that **mixed teams ultimately achieve better results.**"
- **Tools are a means to an end** — "for collecting and analyzing new types of user information
  and adding statistical power and validation to the design thinking work."
- **Data as a strategic asset.** "Some companies are making data the core business strategy, the
  main success factor for developing future business success, and going beyond just using data to
  explore the problem space, new ideas, and innovation."

## Anti-patterns

- **Pure data solutions that overload the human.** See the ski case below — "most of the ski and
  snowboarders are not enjoying the purely data-driven and digital solution, **causing an
  information overflow on a nice day out skiing.**"
- **Design thinkers guessing at data.** "Design thinking teams tend to make the **wrong
  assumptions about data**."
- **Deadlocked assumptions about measurement.** Teams and leaders "must **set aside their
  deadlocked assumptions about measurements** and understand the factors that influence metrics."
- **Unequal analytics access** creating organizational misalignment.

### Data ethics — themes, values, and purpose

| Theme | Examples of values and purpose |
|---|---|
| **Privacy** | Control over the use of data based on relevant data laws; ability to restrict processing, building consensus, or erase data |
| **Accountability** | Verifiable and replicable with governance via monitoring bodies; ability to evaluate, audit, and create impact assessments |
| **Security & safety** | Technical robustness of data models, protocols, and algorithms; security by design and predictability |
| **Explainability & transparency** | Transparency about data and algorithms (e.g. open source); notification in the event of decision making of AI |
| **Non-discrimination & fairness** | Prevention of bias; striving for fairness and equality; application of representative and high-quality data |
| **Human control of technology** | Human agency and oversight for automated decisions; ability to opt out of automated decisions |
| **Professional responsibility** | Access to technology; human values and human flourishing; focus on environmental and societal well-being |

### The Data Ethics Canvas — three columns

| **1. Principles** | **2. Assign cycle** | **3. Implementation** |
|---|---|---|
| **General purpose** — What is the core purpose for using data? | **Data sources & rights** — What are the data sources? Internal or external? Who owns the data? | **Actions** — Which steps, considerations, and compliance clarifications are needed before implementing? |
| **General values** — What values apply for our company or region? | **Data limitations** — What are the limitations of the data source? What is influencing the outcomes (data or timeline gaps, omissions, general bias in data)? | **Engagement** — How is the customer informed about data collection and uses of data? What is the mechanism to erase data? |
| **General policies & laws** — What data protection legislation applies? What sector-specific data sharing policies are to be considered? | **Data sharing** — Is the data shared with other departments, regions, or companies? What is the policy for data sharing? | **Enhancement** — What data points help to improve the product, service, or experience? How might the data set help to expand the core value proposition? How can the data be combined to obtain better insights? |
| **Definition of core principles** — What are the defined core principles working with data, AI, and the application of neuroscience? Are the principles communicated with the wider organization, the core team, and the stakeholders (including customers/users)? | **Minimizing negative impact** — What measures can reduce any limitations with regard to the data? How is the potential negative impact monitored? What benefits are related to the actions applied? | **Impact of data** — What is the impact of the data? Better quality of service? Shorter development cycles? Better customer engagement? New revenues? |

## Worked Example

### The ski-accident design challenge — 10+ years of comparing pure and hybrid teams

**The design challenge from an accident insurance company, as printed:**
> **"HOW MIGHT WE (RE)DESIGN THE SKI TRAFFIC ROUTING AND SIGN POSTING TO PREVENT SKIING ACCIDENTS
> ON HIGH-RISK SLOPES?"**

**The experiment.** "The design challenge stated about preventing ski accidents on slopes has
been given to **pure data teams, professional design thinking teams, as well as mixed innovation
teams working in a hybrid setting over 10+ years.**"

**What each approach produced:**

| Team type | Result |
|---|---|
| **Pure data science** | "Data scientists tend to create solutions based on many data points, integrating the information in augmented realities, and inform ski and snowboard riders constantly about potential danger zones, high density on slopes, weather conditions, and much more information. However, **most of the ski and snowboarders are not enjoying the purely data-driven and digital solution, causing an information overflow on a nice day out skiing.**" |
| **Pure design thinking** | "Design thinking teams tend to make the **wrong assumptions about data** — assuming for example that more accidents are happening on ski slopes with bad weather conditions (e.g. heavy snowfall and fog). In addition, **observation and deep interviews with snow sports enthusiasts alone did not lead to any superior solution** to prevent accidents." |
| **Pure neuroscience** | "Going into pure neuroscience paths to test different campaigns for preventing accidents **resulted in more attention at the targeted audience but did not help to reduce the number of accidents.**" |
| **Hybrid** | "**The best results have been achieved in the creation of a solution which included both the power of ethnographic research and insights from data, topped-up with learnings from neuroscientific research.**" |

**The hybrid process as it actually ran:**

| Step | Data science side | Design thinking side |
|---|---|---|
| **Start** | "What data is available? Hypotheses about correlations? What data is needed to solve the problem?" | "Where is the problem? Who is the user/customer? What are the pains/gains/jobs to be done?" |
| **Explore** | "Start exploring with **descriptive analytics**…" | "…and observing skiers and snowboarders on the slopes. Be curious about what happens **before, during, and after** the activity to get a complete picture." |
| **Synthesis** | "Sharing of insights, storytelling based on experienced stories and animation and visualization of data." | |
| **Ideate, prototype, test** | "Build prototype, test, and validate assumptions. **Generate more data and integrate data analytics while testing.**" | |
| **Solutions** | "Implement the best and appropriate solution for the targeted audience with the objective to prevent accidents on slopes." | |

**The actual findings, as printed:**

*From the data side:*
- **"No correlation between bad weather and the number of accidents"** — falsifying the design
  thinkers' assumption
- **"No correlation between amount of alcohol consumed and reported accidents"**

*From the design thinking side:*
- "Children's and young people's skis often **incorrectly adjusted**"
- "**Unconcentrated in the late afternoon** and condition of the slopes worse than in the morning"

**The prototypes that followed:**
| From the data side | From the design thinking side |
|---|---|
| Create **early Après Ski Program at valley station** | **Free ski service in hotels for families** |
| Create **dynamic sign posting and rerouting** | **Financial kickback for going off the slopes at 3PM** |

**Outcomes targeted:** *fewer accidents* **and** *good experiences* — both, not one at the expense
of the other.

**Why the mixed team worked** — the shared and distinct strengths the chapter names:

| | **Big data / analytics** | **Design thinking** |
|---|---|---|
| Shared | **Curiosity** | **Curiosity** |
| Distinct | Deriving deep **learnings** | Deriving deep **insights** |
| Distinct | Story from **data** | Story from **experience** |

Note the mechanism that makes this case the book's flagship: the *design thinking* hypothesis
(bad weather causes accidents) was **falsified by the data**, while the *data* solution
(continuous AR warnings) was **rejected by the ethnography**. Neither discipline could have
reached the 3 p.m. concentration insight alone.

## Key Takeaways

1. Design thinking answers WHY; data analytics grounds what customers actually want — together
   they replace instinct-only bets with evidence.
2. Use design thinking *for* data analytics (asking the right questions) as much as data *for*
   design thinking.
3. Map data-mining techniques onto the Double Diamond: options/processes/physiological conditions
   in the problem space, clustering and visualization at PoV, generative models in the solution
   space, feedback and dynamics in test.
4. Let new metrics emerge **from the data**, not only from assumptions — that is the predictive
   step change.
5. Accountability expands: teams answer for how well the measurement system tests its own
   assumptions.
6. Democratize analytics tool access, or misalignment follows.
7. Run the Data Ethics Canvas across the whole development cycle, not at the end.
