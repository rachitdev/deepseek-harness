# Chapter 9 — Defining the North Star Metric(s)

*(printed pp. 167–174)*

## Core Idea

The North Star is the first building block of the IMS and the anchor everything else hangs from:
"exploration metrics are formulated, activities are managed, and EXPLORE and EXPLOIT portfolios
are aligned with their guiding principles based on the North Star. For these reasons, the North
Star also has an impact on the entire innovation measurement system."

> "The concept of North Star metrics is in many cases **a set of metrics** that is used as a
> focus for growth ambitions in different stages from problem definition to growth and scale."

The failure the chapter names: "Often it is seen that companies and decision makers focus too
long on a North Star metric that does not fit." The consequence: "organizations tend to focus on
short-term optimization and miss new market opportunities. As a result, **innovation teams and
other parts of the organization work aimlessly on initiatives to look operationally busy.**"

The remedy: **know the signals of the S-curve and act accordingly.**

## Frameworks Introduced

### The seven core principles of a powerful North Star metric
The canvas opens by reminding everyone of these:

| # | Principle |
|---|---|
| 1 | Metrics are **quantifiable** |
| 2 | They **represent vision** |
| 3 | They are **aligned to strategy** |
| 4 | They are **leading indicators** |
| 5 | They are **actionable** |
| 6 | They are **easy to understand** |
| 7 | **Vanity metrics are omitted** |

### The North Star Metric(s) Canvas
Structure, from top (leading) to bottom (lagging):

| Band | Question the canvas asks |
|---|---|
| **The work to be done « metrics »** | "Apply for example minimum viable exploration metrics for new product, service, or experience initiatives to contribute to the North Star" |
| **Directly related input metrics** *(LEADING)* | "Create leading indicators based on type of business (e.g. breadth, depth, or frequency of customer/user engagement)" — Inputs 1–6 |
| **North Star metrics** | "What is the company, business unit, and ecosystem initiative's North Star?" — define by considering the seven core principles |
| **Mid-term business results and customer value** | "What is the mid-term impact for the business?" |
| **Long-term business results and customer value** *(LAGGING)* | "What is the long-term impact for the business?" |

The canvas "supports the design of cause and effect and documents the leading and lagging
indicators."

**Level of the North Star:** company, business unit, ecosystem, or product level, "depending on
the business and the governance model applied. The results might be discussed mid- or long term,
depending on the dynamics in the industry (see potential turning points and indicators of the
S-curve)."

**The digital-age input model:** "a common model about the design of new products, services, and
experiences in the digital age refers to **breadth, depth, or frequency of customer/user
engagement**."

### Why North Star metrics matter — the growth strategies they serve
The chapter lists the strategy types a North Star can express (two entries are partly obscured
by the illustration in the source; see Scope & Limits):

- **Growth Strategy** — ambitions of new growth opportunities, business ecosystems, with radical
  innovation
- **[Customer] Growth Strategy** — increase in number of customers/users
- **Growth / Efficiency Strategy** — massive gains in applying scale effects and automation
- **User Experience Strategy** — improved customer experience based on product and service
  experience of the entire customer journey
- **Consumption Growth Strategy** — higher-intensity usage of services, experiences, and products
- **Business Development Strategy / Go-to-Market Strategies** — higher revenues for existing
  portfolios of products and services
- **Engagement Growth Strategy** — increased engagement of users/customers with services,
  products, and experiences

## Key Concepts

- **Output vs input in the canvas.** "Top-line metrics … usually represent an **output metric**
  with defined results. The input metrics are in most cases **drivers and actions of work** that
  result in innovation success and/or business growth."
- **Ecosystems make North Star definition harder.** "The North Star definition is particularly
  challenging in business ecosystem design and orchestration tasks, as ecosystems are made up of
  different elements, and actors' distinctive priorities need to be aligned with them. In the
  end, it is important that the elements in the system work well together so that **coevolution
  as well as symbiosis** emerges."
- **The canvas is a communication tool** supporting design thinking, innovation, and
  growth-hacking teams "in better understanding the WHY, HOW, and WHAT." When formulating OKRs it
  "provides orientation and facilitates focused innovation work in given bandwidths."
- **Deliberate North-Star-free initiatives are allowed** — "innovation teams are asked to think
  of previously unimagined growth possibilities. But these initiatives also need **a clear
  mandate to the teams to disregard the North Star and existing guardrails.**"
- **Observed benefits of a well-defined top-line metric**: positive impact on targeted resource
  allocation; improved team collaboration; reduced frustration and disorientation.
- **Observed cost of no North Star**: "almost all teams are struggling with the WHY, as it is
  difficult to identify the purpose and the role of each individual and the team in the larger
  context."

## Mental Models

- **The North Star shows where the journey goes next.** "A powerful North Star shows where the
  journey is going next and where the energy and effort of the teams should be directed."
- **A North Star that is a lagging indicator is not enough by itself.** Even a good top-line
  metric may adjust "far too late for tactical/strategic changes or the reprioritization of
  innovation projects to be made" — so it must be decomposed into leading input metrics.
- **Revisit regularly.** "It is recommended to regularly revisit the North Star metrics to ensure
  they reflect the strategy and predict results."

## Anti-patterns

- **Sticking with a North Star that no longer fits** — the S-curve signal was ignored.
- **Operational busyness as a substitute for direction.**
- **Treating a single lagging top-line metric as the whole system.**
- **Vanity metrics inside the North Star set.**

| Stage | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Identifying the output metrics (A)** | What drives growth and revenues on our platform? |
| **Getting started on input metrics (B)** | What is the current condition? · What is needed now? · How can the situation be improved? |
| **Applying input metrics (C)** | How to align the current efforts more closely with the chosen inputs? · What decisions should be taken now and in the near future? · Are the causes and effects defined supporting those decisions? · Is it possible to trace the work to be done? · **What is the confidence level for the chosen inputs and North Star metrics?** · What are the lessons learned after the first implementation of activities? |

## Worked Example

### The streaming-platform North Star — the chapter's full worked case

**Setup as the book states it:** "Let's imagine a company is growing into a market of streaming
content, like music, podcasts, or other content relevant to a broad audience with a
**subscription or freemium business model**. The main objective might be for the company to grow
**the time customers/users spend on the platform**."

**Step 1 — the naive setup (A and B).**
The flow of input metrics (B) and output metrics (A) "seems to be a good start because it
directly indicates how likely a customer/user is to stay with the provided services or leave for
alternative offerings."

**Step 2 — why it is not good enough.**
"However, the setting of the metric is far from optimal, as the North Star metric (e.g., **time
spent engaging with content**, in the chosen example) is a **lagging indicator**, meaning that
this data point is adjusted far too late for tactical/strategic changes or the reprioritization
of innovation projects to be made."

**Step 3 — add a second layer of input metrics (C).**
"Other input metrics, based on exploitation and exploration metrics, will most likely be needed
(C), which can be optimized and analyzed accordingly to predict the impact on the most important
metrics."

**Step 4 — state the assumption chain.**
Assumption: *features are necessary for time spent on the platform.* Two levers follow:
- **Initializing a higher frequency of visits**
- **Measures that increase time spent per visit**

**Step 5 — design the mechanisms for frequency.**
"To bring users/customers back more frequently, process ideas can be developed that encourage
their engagement, such as:
- highlighting new content creators, and/or
- offering **AI-based recommendations**, and/or
- **peer-to-peer recommendations**."

**Step 6 — validate, then go one level deeper.**
"If these measures work and have been validated, by applying iterations and the concept of
**minimum viable exploration metrics**, the next level is to test and measure mechanisms that
keep users/customers on the platform **longer**. Potential ideas might emerge in terms of
**playlists that are data-generated and thus custom fit to the needs, current moods, or schedule
of the active customers.**"

**The resulting cause-and-effect chain, drawn right to left in the book:**

```
Input Metrics (C)  ←  Input Metrics (B)  ←  Output Metrics (A)
   LEADING                                        LAGGING
```

**Why the visualization matters:** "The visualization of the causes and effects provides
confidence about the defined action fields for innovation teams by focusing on the ideate state
of the current conditions based on the North Star."

## Key Takeaways

1. The North Star is usually a **set** of metrics, not one number, and it drives the whole IMS.
2. Test every candidate against the seven principles — quantifiable, vision, strategy-aligned,
   leading, actionable, understandable, no vanity.
3. A lagging top-line metric must be decomposed into leading input metrics, then into a second
   layer of input metrics you can act on this quarter.
4. Use breadth, depth, or frequency of customer engagement as the default input model for digital
   businesses.
5. Watch the S-curve; a North Star past its inflection point produces busywork.
6. Teams may operate without a North Star only under an explicit mandate to disregard it.
