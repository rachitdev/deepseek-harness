# Chapter 19 — Design Thinking & Neurodesign

*(printed pp. 359–379, including "A Final Word")*

## Core Idea

**Neurodesign** is "a design concept in which design thinking teams and cognitive neuroscientists
work as a team, using the knowledge of what makes the human brain tick to understand how
creativity and design thinking are measured, as well as how to better design products and
services by understanding customers and their brain activities as associated to a product or
service."

The motivating observation: "design thinking teams have become increasingly aware that **it is
often misleading to merely ask the customer/user for their feedback for a first high-resolution
prototype. Measuring the response of the customer/user brain, on the other hand, can in some
cases tell us a lot more about perception.**"

> "Neurodesign emphasizes the importance of understanding customers'/users' **non-conscious
> behaviors, reactions, and interactions** and how it can be applied in designing new or improved
> products, services, and experiences."

The honest boundary: **"It is not possible to read minds with neuroscience alone. Based on brain
activity, workload and motivation can be measured, but only limited conclusions can be drawn
regarding brain activity and the content or experience a test subject is currently exposed to."**

## Frameworks Introduced

### The lineage and evolution of neurodesign (1960 → 2040)

| Era | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Early days (1960)** | "First research about the intersection of psychology and design and initial application of design thinking and neuro." Origin "based on initiatives by **Larry Leifer (ME310)**. He was one of the first researchers at the intersection of design and neuro at Stanford in the 1960s" |
| **Adaptation (1980)** | "Practical models about creativity and visual thinking which are still applied in many design thinking tools" — **Bob McKim and Jim Adams** developed these |
| **Today (2020)** | "Neuroscience and design thinking as a combined discipline to quantify the why of human behavior." "In the 2020s, the neurodesign research initiative has become more popular at Stanford University and many other leading universities" |
| **Future (2040)** | "A new area of human-centered design with strong evidence on real-time data, which is based on the real behavior; seamlessly integrated in physical, VR, and AR environments and experiences" |

### The Three Brain Systems and the 10 / 90 split

| Layer | Brain region | Character | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Thinking brain** | **Prefrontal cortex** | **LOGICAL — the future** | *What someone says.* **Slow decision system**: declarative preferences; "weakly or uncorrelated, for example, when it comes to actions and purchase decisions" |
| **Emotional & doing brain** | **Limbic system** | **EMOTIONAL — the now** | *What the brain tells one to do.* **Fast decision system**: 100 percent unconscious; survival and sex |
| **Deep being brain** | **Accumbens** | **MOTIVATION & FOCUS aligned with resources** | *What someone is really like.* Depends on thickness of deep brain areas; "correlates with **Herrmann Brain Dominance Instrument (HBDI)** profile and other personality tests"; "unconsciously modulates humans' decisions" |

**The proportions the chapter prints:** ~**10%** is "what someone explains or perhaps thinks";
~**90%** is the "subconscious world for someone, which is based on how and what the brain decides
and articulates."

> "Reason and emotion work together."

### The completeness argument for neurodesign

`DESIGN THINKING (empathy · personas · solution tests)` + `MARKET DATA (POS · clicks · panels)` =
**design thinking & traditional market research**, capturing **explicit customer/user data**
(focus groups · surveys).
Add `NEUROSCIENCE` — **implicit customer/user data** (unconscious behaviors · neural measures) —
and the union is **NEURODESIGN** → *a more complete view of the customer/user.*

### The three paradigms for measuring design thinking activities

| # | Paradigm | Typical measurements |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Measurement of physiological behaviors in performing design thinking** | Eye movements; spectral analysis of heart rate variability; electrodermal activity; standard functional assessment of the autonomic nervous system |
| **2** | **Measurement of the connections between cognitive processes and brain activity** | Electroencephalogram (EEG) test that detects electrical activity in the brain; functional near-infrared spectroscopy; functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) |
| **3** | **Measurement of design thinking cognition** — "the most known and developed procedure" | Reasoning, processes, and patterns; divergent and convergent thinking; **design fixation**; design creativity; visual reasoning; space co-evolution; collaboration with cognition tools. Aims to detect design thinking characteristics, measure cognitive behavior, and perform **black-box experiments** where multiple conditions are set to perform a design thinking task or apply a certain tool |

**Purpose of measuring design thinking activities:** "to generate better insights into each
individual's and team's brain, body, and mental activities. This provides the basis for enhancing
the individual or team performance, **triggering self-reflection**, as well as managing better
**self-regulation** in performing or facilitating design thinking."
And a political use: "In many organizations the work of design thinking teams is constantly
challenged, and **new measurements can help to overcome the bias.**"

### Design Thinking vs Neuroscience vs Neurodesign

| | **Design Thinking** | **Neuroscience** | **Neurodesign** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mission** | Problem identification and problem solving by applying design thinking mindset, tools, and methods | Understanding the thinking of users and customers as well as the thinking of individuals and teams over the entire design cycle | Design, (re)design, and implementation of customer-led services, products, experiences, and processes based on the latest neuroscience research |
| **Starting point** | Problem statement or design challenge | Hypotheses based on first observations or assumptions | Problem statement with findings of cognitive and affective neuroscience |
| **Reasoning** | **Abductive** | **Deductive** | **Combining abductive and deductive** |
| **Maturity of applications** | High | Medium | Low — "mainly research, sandbox experiments, and first initial implementations" |
| **Experimentation performed** | Ideate, build prototype, and test | Controlled experiment | Neuroscience theories and tools integrated over the entire design cycle |
| **Material & enabler** | Design thinking tools and methodologies, material for prototypes | Scientific tools and methodologies with digital enabler technologies (machine learning and automation), application of brain imaging | Combination of physiological, neurological, and cognitive patterns with powerful design thinking tools |

### The Neurodesign Project Canvas

| Block | Fields and questions |
|---|---|
| **1. Empathy & ethnographic** | **Feelings** — "Collection of sensory inputs to the brain; tapping into subconscious stored memory" → *"We know from memory…"* · **Imaginations** — "Applying cognition based on already-acquired knowledge; tapping into experience and/or sensory perception" → *"We expect…"* · **General values** — Who has the problem? What are the pains/gains, jobs to be done, use cases? Is the problem related to social activities with other persons? · *The persona might respond or take a specific action for a specific schema, interaction, or touchpoint* · **Critical assumptions to be verified** — Which specific schema, interaction, or touchpoint is most critical? Which behavior needs verification? What needs are not understood? Which environmental setting might have influence? |
| **2. Experimental neurodesign set-up** | **Tools** — "Select the appropriate tools from the neurodesign toolbox (e.g. **hyperscanning, heart rate, eye tracking**)" · **Set up the experiment** — What should be measured? What additional measurements should be applied? How is the measurement correlating to other measurements? · **Capture, analyze, & visualize data** — What data was collected? How do the outcomes fit with existing knowledge and where are there contradictions? How can the outcomes be visualized and shared with an extended team? |
| **3. Limitations & results** | **Limitations of the experiment** — What are the potential limitations of applied tools? How to overcome limitations with different or more advanced experiment settings? What other social science fields help to overcome limitations (e.g. **social psychology, social anthropology**)? · **Results** — What are the main findings? What is the impact of the findings to the project? **How can the patterns be used in the broader spectrum of machine-readable ontologies, neural networks for better classification, and AI?** |

### Neurodesign measurement levels

| Level | Measuring design thinking activities | Applying neuroscience over the entire design cycle |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual** | Mental stress · expertise | Design thinking tasks · tool preferences |
| **Team** | Collaboration, shared goals · brain synchronization | Distribution of creativity · reflection and learning patterns |
| **Customer/user** | Engagement level · mindfulness | Co-creation patterns · emotional response insights |
| **Organization** | Cross-organizational · improved tools | Organizational learning · engagement levels |
| **Ecosystems** | Co-evolution of thoughts · symbiotic behavior | Customer-led behavior · data to expand offerings |

## Key Concepts

- **Named brain findings from neurodesign research:**
  - "The divergent phase and associations of different approaches activate the brain (i.e. the
    **left frontopolar cortex**), which is associated with creativity."
  - "The thinking of design thinking teams during the **active ideation phase** occurs mainly in
    the **parieto-occipital area**, an important area for visual function and cognitive control."
  - "**Transparency is enormously important**, which in turn leads to better coordination of
    measurements. The reason for this lies in the **anterior cingulate cortex (ACC)**. Here, mixed
    messages and seemingly contradictory objectives and priorities can be perceived. As a result,
    **flexible thinking and openness are impaired when the brain cannot resolve these dilemmas.**"
  - "There is **no specific part of the brain responsible for creative thinking**, but rather
    several areas of the brain are activated, depending on the nature of the design challenge and
    the complexity of the problem… Both hemispheres of the brain are active."
- **Great ideas are not random.** "These studies confirm that great ideas are not random, but
  rather the result of a more or less orderly process. **Every brain has the natural ability to
  generate these sudden insights. The good news is that every team member can be creative.**"
- **Design thinking deliberately disrupts the brain's rush to a solution.** "In design thinking,
  we try to **actively disrupt this step of finding a solution quickly from our brain**, as
  experience shows that a more in-depth exploration of the problem space leads to better solutions
  later on. This responds very well to the motto **'begin at the beginning.'**"
- **Frustration can help.** "Studies show that sometimes, **a little frustration combined with
  emotion is also helpful**. In particular, when emotions have a positive effect on memory
  performance, team members tend to remember the challenge better."
- **The incubation effect.** "The brain also continues to process the design challenge and to think
  about solutions as soon as the team members turn their attention to other activities or simply
  relax… the brain begins to change its activation patterns, as seen in the fMRI. It appears as if
  the brain is changing strategy to determine if other brain regions have something valuable to
  contribute. **Research has shown that it is helpful to relieve the prefrontal cortex from heavy
  work by doing something that requires less concentration. A nap, walk, jog, or other low-stress
  leisure activity** … the brain continues to work on the design challenge continuously and
  subconsciously, continuing divergent thinking."
- **Localizing capabilities** — "Cognitive neuroscience methods and tools are clustered by the
  **location capabilities** (spatial sensitivity) and the **timescale** over which a particular
  measurement is obtained. While an MRI is able to detect large regions of the cortex, measurements
  performed by the **patch clamp** methods provide information in milliseconds and low spatial
  resolution." Signals recorded via patch clamp, intracellular, extracellular, and mass unit
  recording, plus **EEG** and **ECoG**. "For operating in a more real environment, for example
  measuring teams simultaneously on empathy, **hyperscanning** is usually used." The timescale
  axis runs milliseconds → seconds → days → years; techniques plotted include task fMRI, resting
  state, fiber connection dMRI, cytoarchitecture, sulcal/gyral boundaries.
- **Larger samples raise validity.** "A larger sample of potential customers/users as test cases
  correspondingly increases the validity of the data points identified."
- **Context matters more than isolated features.** "It is also important to consider the data in
  the appropriate context… This seems particularly important in experiments in which **only
  individual functions or features are tested and analyzed**. A comprehensive view that takes into
  account how the individual elements are interrelated is today confirmed from the results of
  various neurodesign projects."

## Mental Models

- **Culture as neural precondition.** "The entire design cycle, from building empathy, to defining
  points of view, to ideation, requires a corporate culture and environment **based on the neural
  response of each team member**. For example, it needs **psychological safety** in a
  team-of-teams setting."
- **Mutual openness is the collaboration requirement.** "It is necessary that each team is open to
  the other's approach. This is best achieved when **neuroscientists actively participate in design
  thinking activities** throughout the design cycle and **design thinkers learn and develop their
  scientific curiosity and analytical thinking** through active engagement in neuroscientific
  research."
- **A culture clash to expect.** "There is an **artistry in design which might clash culturally
  with the technical rationality** of data and neuroscience."
- **The constraint of the lab.** "Neuroscience adds value… because it follows a deductive and
  inductive reasoning that allows new knowledge creation based on what is already known, **but with
  the constraint that the complexity is limited to experiments in the lab environment.**"

## Anti-patterns

- **Asking the customer about a high-resolution prototype and believing the answer** — the ~10%
  declarative layer is "weakly or uncorrelated… when it comes to actions and purchase decisions."
- **Testing isolated features** without the interrelated whole.
- **Expecting neuroscience to read minds.**

### The Final Word — eight closing recommendations
| # | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Define a North Star** and hold all relevant teams accountable for defining their objectives and key results |
| 2 | **Introduce the measurements for exploring step by step and in iterations, but with the same professionalism as the measurements for exploit** |
| 3 | **Begin at the beginning** with design thinking, because changes on the final result are expensive to reverse or pivot |
| 4 | **De-risk development** by continually examining evidence across desirability, viability, and feasibility dimensions to evaluate highly uncertain, transformative innovation initiatives |
| 5 | **Place the customer/user at the center** of all activities, and break the internal silos and barriers to external partners to create winning value propositions |
| 6 | **Expand the toolbox** with data-driven tools, methods, and automation over the entire design cycle and establish new ways of measuring |
| 7 | **Be aware that some of the signals and indicators that worked for measuring success in the past may not work for radical new products, services, and experiences** |
| 8 | **Gradually increase the organizational maturity** in design thinking and innovation measurement systems, while aligning business/project objectives with efforts to change culture/behavior |
| 9 | **"Don't forget that metrics used to evaluate the existing business (EXPLOIT) usually have no relevance to innovation initiatives (EXPLORE)."** |

### The four phases of the measurement-system cycle (from A Final Word)
| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Preparation** | Set and dynamically adapt the North Star and company objectives; apply minimum viable exploration metrics for EXPLORE and select appropriate metrics for EXPLOIT; moderate the transition from EXPLORE to EXPLOIT and sustain the business |
| **Introduction** | "Increasingly characterized by activities around **collecting and validating data at all levels of measurement**" |
| **Activation** | "Data is used for meaningful measurements, as well as **establishing data-driven innovation** as part of the innovation work" |
| **Integration** | "Operationalization of data and measurements and enables **data-driven management decisions**, up to the establishment of algorithms that support the design and innovation work. The respective reports and data feeds are shared more frequently and as needed" |

### Governance separation stated in the closing
> "Exploratory design thinking activities and innovation projects require **their own governance
> and new capabilities, separate from the efficiency-oriented exploitation activities**. Unlike the
> mature business, which is trimmed for efficiency with predictable timelines, traditional
> financial metrics, and predefined 'go' or 'no go' decision criteria, the **exploration portfolio
> uses an iterative testing and learning process, and evidence-based funding criteria.**"

And the three substitutions that mark a working system:
- "**Customer evidence will take the place of spreadsheets**"
- "**Metered funding will take the place of big bets**"
- "**Asking questions will take the place of expressing opinions**"

VUCA is footnoted in the closing as **Volatility, Uncertainty, Complexity, and Ambiguity**.

## Worked Example

### The neurodesign performance comparison — the impact chart

The chapter prints a comparison of measurement approaches by their impact on product performance
and experience, running from traditional single tools to the full neurodesign toolbox. The stated
values:

| Approach | Impact on product performance and experience |
|---|---|
| **Facial coding** (single tool) | **−5%** |
| **Biometrics** (single tool) | **−25%** |
| **EEG** (single tool) | **−50%** |
| **Combined tools** | **−80%** |
| **Neurodesign** (full combination) | **−4-D** *(the extraction of the final bar's label is degraded in the source; the chart's direction is that the combined neurodesign toolbox exceeds all preceding options)* |

The chapter's own framing of what the chart shows: "the most obvious differences can be identified
when testing and comparing **traditional methods**, with **single tools and methods from
neurosciences**, as well as when applying a **combined toolbox of all methods and paradigms**."
The reproducible lesson: single neuro-instruments each add measurable resolution, but the
step-change comes from combining them — and combining them with design thinking.

### Running a neurodesign project — the canvas as a sequence

1. **Empathy & ethnographic.** Fill *"We know from memory…"* (feelings — sensory inputs, tapping
   into subconscious stored memory) and *"We expect…"* (imaginations — cognition based on
   already-acquired knowledge). State who has the problem, the pains/gains and JTBD, and whether
   the problem is related to social activities with other persons.
2. **Name the critical assumption.** Which specific schema, interaction, or touchpoint is most
   critical? Which behavior needs verification? What needs are not understood? Which environmental
   setting might have influence?
3. **Select tools** from the neurodesign toolbox — hyperscanning (for measuring teams
   simultaneously on empathy), heart rate, eye tracking; add EEG, biometrics, face coding, or
   self-report as the question requires.
4. **Set up the experiment.** What should be measured? What additional measurements? **How is the
   measurement correlating to other measurements?**
5. **Capture, analyze, visualize.** What data was collected? How do the outcomes fit with existing
   knowledge and **where are there contradictions**?
6. **State limitations honestly.** What are the potential limitations of applied tools? How to
   overcome them with different or more advanced settings? Which other social science fields —
   social psychology, social anthropology — help?
7. **Report results and their onward use.** What are the main findings, what is the impact on the
   project, and **how can the patterns be used in machine-readable ontologies, neural networks for
   better classification, and AI?**

### The brain's own design cycle — the chapter's account of solving a problem

| Stage | What the brain does |
|---|---|
| **First contact** | "The **prefrontal cortex** has the task of evaluating this problem. At this stage, the realization that there is a challenge to be solved occurs. The brain tries to understand what the problem is and checks whether a solution is in sight" |
| **Elaboration** | "The brain consciously works to understand, specify, and elaborate the challenge by gathering raw material and finding a solution" |
| **The design thinking intervention** | The brain wants to jump to a fast solution using regions "that seem suitable to complete the task" — design thinking **actively disrupts** this |
| **Feeding the brain** | "By actively and moderately seeking more insights, the brain is fed with inputs to approach the challenge from different perspectives: **logical reasoning, trial and error, or even by applying design thinking tools which aim to find analogies**" |
| **Persistence** | "If the brain does not find a solution right away, it is important to **keep pushing**, as more connections to other brain regions are activated" |
| **Incubation** | Attention shifts to something less demanding — "the brain starts to discover new combinations… changing strategy to determine if other brain regions have something valuable to contribute" |

## Key Takeaways

1. Roughly 90% of the customer's decision layer is subconscious; declarative answers cover ~10%
   and correlate weakly with actual purchase behavior.
2. Neurodesign = design thinking + market data (explicit) + neuroscience (implicit) → a more
   complete view of the customer.
3. Three measurement paradigms: physiological behavior, cognition-to-brain-activity connection,
   and design thinking cognition.
4. Design thinking is abductive, neuroscience deductive; neurodesign combines both.
5. Combining neuro tools beats any single tool; combine them with design thinking to get the
   step-change.
6. Transparency has a neural basis — contradictory objectives impair flexible thinking via the ACC.
7. Build in incubation: low-concentration activity keeps divergent thinking running.
8. Closing rule of the book: metrics for the existing business (EXPLOIT) usually have no relevance
   to innovation initiatives (EXPLORE).
