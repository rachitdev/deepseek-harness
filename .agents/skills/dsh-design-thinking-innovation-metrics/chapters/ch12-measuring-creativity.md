# Chapter 12 — Measuring Creativity

*(printed pp. 197–210)*

## Core Idea

"The measurement of creativity is probably one of the most difficult metrics, and research in
this area has its limits. Creativity is not available at the push of a button, and it is even
less evident when we demand it."

The book's working definition: **"In the context of design thinking and innovation work,
creativity is an act of turning new and imaginative ideas into reality. Creativity is
characterized by perceiving the world in new ways, finding hidden patterns, making connections
between seemingly unrelated phenomena, and finding radically new solutions with an
interdisciplinary team for a customer/user problem. In design thinking, creativity consists of
thinking and doing."**

The honest limitation: **"As of today, there is no measurement tool that can feasibly measure the
full breadth and complexity of creativity, which leads to the fact that only different
characteristics can be measured."** Even combined tests are limited "because, for example, a weak
relation between divergent thinking and imagination exists."

The first decision: **"Knowing the reason for measuring creativity or the kind of creative work
one wants to evaluate is the first step in demystifying the creative space."**

## Frameworks Introduced

### The two historical streams
Since the 1950s two main streams of measuring creativity have evolved:
1. **Divergent thinking** — test-based research originating with **Guilford's (1950) theory of
   divergent thinking**; measuring the characteristics of divergent thinking gave rise to
   numerous tests in the 1970s and 1980s, e.g. the **Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking**.
2. **Imagination** — "everything related to the activity of visual imagination, which encompasses
   creating, interpreting, and transforming vivid mental representations."

### The Four Common Ways to Measure Creativity — and when to use each

| Model | What it measures | Choose it when… |
|---|---|---|
| **Taxonomy of Creative Design (Nilsson, 2011)** | How creative a *work* is | "When measuring the origin or influences of a work, taxonomy is often considered as a model" |
| **Guilford Model** | How creative a *person* is | "If individual team members are to be measured or compared to each other, Guilford's approach is a good choice" |
| **Requirements Model** | Creative work against a *program* | "For objective measurement of creative tasks, a coherent program should be included" |
| **Csikszentmihalyi's Model (1999)** | The *social value* of creative work | "For questions about the cultural value of a work, it is best to follow Csikszentmihalyi's model to study the response of the field" |

"Depending on the measurement method, the definition of creativity may vary. In some measurements
creativity refers to the **deviant production** (in what quantity creativity occurs or how diverse
it is). In other measurements creativity refers to the **novelty** of the design or prototype."

### Nilsson's Taxonomy of Creative Design — the five levels

| Level | Definition (the book's own wording) |
|---|---|
| **1. Imitation** | "The development of a similar or almost similar product as one in existence" |
| **2. Variation** | "The making of slight alterations to the existing object in a way that makes it look different despite being able to maintain the original object's identity" |
| **3. Combination** | "The mixing of two or more products to an extent that they can be described as either all or both" |
| **4. Transformation** | "The recreation of a product in a novel milieu to an extent that it acquires a number of the original product's attributes. Nevertheless, it cannot be referred to the original product" |
| **5. Innovative creation** | "The creation of a product that seems to have no single discernible quality of any existing idea or object" |

Measurement questions: *"How far does the creative work depart from previous works?"* and
*"How big a leap has the creator made in terms of content and form?"*

| Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|
| Evaluates creative works relative to other existing works by measuring their originality and influence | "It does not say anything about the value, relevance, or effectiveness of the work" |

### Guilford Model — the four (plus one) measures

| Measure | What it captures | The question |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluency** | The **volume** of responses | How many responses are there? |
| **Flexibility** | The **type** of responses | How many types of responses are there? |
| **Originality** | The **unusualness** of responses | How unusual are the responses? |
| **Elaboration** | The **detail** of responses | What is the level of detail of the responses? |
| **Connections** *(added in the warm-up variant)* | Linkage between symbols | What connected symbols have been created? |

| Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|
| "Assesses productivity output in a descriptive and quantifiable way" | "It does not indicate anything about the relevance or value of work in terms of its creative output" |

**Neuroscience extension:** "Today, Guilford model measurements can be conducted and validated by
examining the brain using imaging techniques such as **magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)** or
electrical activity monitoring methods like **electroencephalography (EEG)** that show how neurons
and circuits in the brain are firing. Other techniques, like **fractional anisotropy (FA)**, are
applied to measure the structure of white matter in the brain. White matter is mainly made up of
myelinated axons, long connections between neurons that carry brain signals."

### Requirements Model
"Evaluates creative work by using **criteria that are defined before the product is built**." In
design thinking, critical functions and experiences for a potential solution are defined during
the design challenge; the final prototype provides the basis for measurements such as the
appropriate number of functions, the multi-user capability, or the time the customer is engaged.
Harder evaluations — **emotional engagement or flow moments** — "seem to be subjective measures
[but] can actually be measured concretely with targeted questions and increasingly in combination
with new neurotechnologies."

| Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|
| Able to measure **relevance, efficiency, and value** against clearly defined requirements | "It works best when a solution is compared to **itself** and not to other solutions" |

Rule of thumb: "The higher the task is in the taxonomy, the greater the need to apply external
requirements to ensure that measurement is easy to perform."

### Csikszentmihalyi's System Model (1999)
Measures "the value of a work in the relationships between three important parties … the
**person**, the **domain**, and the **field**."
"The model **forbids calling a work creative, per se**. Much more important in the measurement is
the **approval of the field**, and in the creation of a work, the field is used as the main
determinant of whether the product can be sustained within the domain or whether it will fade
into oblivion."

| Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|
| "Assesses the relevance and value of a work by considering the **community context**" | "The model can be subjective … based on constantly changing parameters; therefore, the field tends to change over time… no absolute value can be represented, but an awareness at a particular time and in a defined context can be reflected" |

### The 5Ps — Creative Potential and Creative Output

| P | Definition the book gives |
|---|---|
| **Person** | Skills, talents, knowledge, and experience involved in creative work |
| **Process** | "The development path of a creative team, the steps and actions it takes to get the job done" |
| **Place** | "The environment in which the work is done … everything from access to material resources to high-level organizational policies and leadership to the culture and social norms of an organization" |
| **Product** | "The creative work itself — the new object, idea, or behavior that the organization hopes to commercialize" |
| **Persuasion** | Added because "an interdisciplinary team that wants to implement a creative idea must convince other teams, managers, executives, and even external stakeholders that what they want to do is novel and useful enough to merit support" |

Grouping:
- **Creative potential** = **people + process + place** (the elements that influence how creative
  the new product, idea, or behavior might be)
- **Creative output** = **product + persuasion** (the novelty and usefulness of the product, idea,
  or behavior)
- "Both are driven by the effectiveness of the persuasion needed to convince others that the
  effort of creative work is worth doing."

**The critical balance rule:**
> "When the focus is **only on creative potential**, the scope for implementing creative ideas is
> usually limited — opportunities are set aside due to lack of time and resources or the company's
> inability to deviate from standard EXPLOIT procedures. Similarly, measures that address **only
> creative performance** — activities aimed at creating the organizational space for creativity —
> without at the same time initiating measures into the creative potential needed to generate new
> and useful ideas, EXPLORE, are unlikely to succeed."

### From Single Metrics to Hierarchical Frameworks
The chapter's progression `SINGLE METRIC (1P) → 4Ps → FRAMEWORK 5Ps+`:
- Measuring the **creative person** (individuals and teams) based on skills, talents, knowledge,
  and experience involved in creative work
- Measuring creativity in **a combination of cognitive, conative, and emotional factors** which
  interact with the environment dynamically
- Measuring creativity based on **the interconnection of person, place, process, product, and
  persuasion**

## Key Concepts

- **The basic elements of creativity** are "the ability to **copy, transform, and combine**."
- **Creativity defined shortly:** "of having original ideas that have value."
- **Play:** "The most creative teams have the ability to operate in a childlike way of play and
  to explore ideas."
- **Creativity as a superpower:** "Creativity might be humans' highest cognitive function. It's
  everyone's superpower and it can be developed and refined, albeit with effort and practice."
- **Creativity is trainable:** "Creativity is a skill that can be developed and a process that can
  be managed. Creativity begins with a foundation of knowledge, learning a discipline, and
  mastering a way of thinking. Individuals and teams learn to be creative by experimenting,
  exploring, questioning assumptions, using imagination, and synthesizing information."
- **Divergent thinking is an essential capacity for creativity**, and value creation "starts with
  the problem for a significant time before even trying to solve it."

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating a single model as complete.** Every model in the chapter is presented with an
  explicit weakness.
- **Measuring only creative potential** (people/process/place) or **only creative performance** —
  both fail alone.
- **Measuring creativity retrospectively only.** "Often, we see the creativity of teams only in
  the retrospective."
- **Ignoring cultural variation.** "The canvas should be adapted to and supplemented for the
  respective cultural requirements, since, for example, **creative frustration or the handling of
  ambiguities differs depending on the culture, mindset, and team spirit.**"

## Reference Tables

### The 5Ps Creativity Canvas — every field and its questions

| Block | Fields and questions |
|---|---|
| **Persuasion** | **Networks & communities** — Who are the people who can support the creative pursuit? How do you create and maintain a fair and healthy relationship with these people and groups? · **Social environment** — What social environment is needed for creative work? To what extent can the environment be actively shaped? · **Collaboration** — What does the collaboration look like? Which stakeholders are to be involved in the process and when? How is collaboration with others during creative activity? |
| **Process** | **Problem** — Is there a problem worth solving, and if so, what exactly is the problem? Why should it be solved? · **Insights** — How is the emergence of insights supported and shared? How are interruptions and re-framing applied? · **Ideation** — Which thinking tools are applied to develop new approaches? Which techniques are appropriate, and how can they be trained? · **Exploration** — How radical should a potential solution be, and what influence should it have on a domain? To what extent do questions, thoughts, and design activities influence the domain? |
| **Person / Team** | **Curiosity** — What is the basis of curiosity and why? How can teams be curious more often and/or increase the chances of unforeseen situations? · **Purpose** — What are the real drivers and motivators, and why? What is the inner core that is being expressed? · **Knowledge** — What knowledge and skills are needed to achieve a particular creative goal? How can certain skills and abilities be built up? · **Reflection** — How can creative action be reflected upon and learned from? How can parts of the creative process be planned? |
| **Product** | **Success** — What does success look like at the project, team, and behavioral-change level? What outcome is good enough to achieve? · **Quality / quantity** — How is the quality of creative performance evaluated? How many functions and experiences from the requirements have been implemented? · **Usefulness** — How useful is the service, product, or experience from the perspective of the customer and other reviewers? How close is the emotional bond of the customer/user? · **Novelty** — How new is the product to the customer and the market? How radically different is the solution from existing solutions? |
| **Place** | **Mindset** — What are the characteristics and perspectives that promote or hinder creativity? How can attitudes and behavior change in the short, medium, and long term? · **Resources** — Which resources are needed, and which are available? Is it possible to replace lacking resources, perhaps with external support? · **Room** — What does the physical, hybrid, or virtual work environment look like to accomplish the tasks? What additional elements are needed to support the creative process? |

Canvas orientation: it "focuses on the **WHY** in relation to the person and the team, with
explorations of curiosity, purpose, knowledge, and reflection, and then goes into process, place,
persuasion and product," organised around WHAT? / WHY? / HOW? / WHERE? / WHO ELSE?

### The five taxonomy questions as a team assessment
| # | Question | Taxonomy level it detects |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the solution idea the same or nearly the same as something that already exists? | Imitation |
| 2 | Is it a slight modification of an existing product or service so that it is different but still retains the identity of the original solution? | Variation |
| 3 | Is it a mix of two or more functions and experiences so that it can be said to be both or all? | Combination |
| 4 | Is it a re-creation of something in a new context, so that it has some characteristics of the original solution, but can no longer be said to be the same? | Transformation |
| 5 | Does it have no recognizable characteristics of pre-existing solutions or ideas? | Innovative creation |

**Caveat the book attaches:** "It does not evaluate the level of difficulty, how far a potential
solution goes beyond what was there before, and some of the mechanisms for how it achieves these
gains. It is reasonable to say that this measurement … is **not applicable in measuring the
relevance, value, or effectiveness** of the solution."

## Worked Example

### The hiker's water design challenge — scoring team creativity with the taxonomy

**The design challenge as printed:** *"How might we (re)design a solution for hikers who depend on
fluids during a hike and want to transport the daily requirement of water easily and safely?"*
(labelled **THIRSTY** / *"How much water to carry?"*, drawn from *The Design Thinking Toolbox*,
pp. 187–195.)

The chapter scores four successive team outputs against Nilsson's taxonomy:

| # | What the team did | Performance classified as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "The team wants to initially learn more about the user and their problem through experimentation." | **Imitation** — "of something the team has seen before" |
| 2 | "The team creates a first solution based on insights, learning, and existing experience." **Solution: better grip and new design** | **Variation** — "staying with the core idea and tweaking it a little bit, which adds some novelty, but it very much remains of the solution of the existing alternatives" |
| 3 | *(next iteration)* | **Combination** — "of two ideas to improve the overall experience and functionality" |
| 4 | "The team leaves the existing frame to build something new to the market and new to the customer." | **Innovative creation** — "The solution appears to be an original creation. From all ideas, the **flying water** has the greatest novelty and could be classified as the most creative." |

This is the reproducible move: take the actual artifacts the team produced across the macro
cycle, classify each against the five taxonomy levels, and read the progression as the creativity
measurement.

### The 12-circles warm-up — Guilford scoring with the book's own results

**Procedure (exactly as printed):**
1. Create a sheet with **12 circles** and hand it out to the team.
2. Ask the team to create as many ideas as possible within the circles provided — **three
   minutes**.
3. Share all the sketches on a large wall and start assessing the creativity based on **fluency,
   flexibility, originality, elaboration, and connections**.

**The scored results the chapter prints:**

| Participant | What they drew | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Patrick** | The most drawings, but all faces | **Highest fluency** |
| **Peter** | The most *types* of responses, but fewer total responses than Patrick | **Highest flexibility** |
| **Marc** | Two wheels and a ball, "which have nice geometry!" | *"No prize, alas."* |
| **Lily** | Only two responses, but no one else drew a balloon or a bomb | **Highest originality** |
| **Rukaiya** | Only three faces, but with more detail than the others | **Highest elaboration** |
| **Michael** | Connected the elements and drew mainly physical products | **The ability to combine systems thinking and design thinking** |

**What the warm-up is and is not for:** it helps the team and facilitator "to obtain a more
psychometric evaluation of the team member · to mainly measure the productivity quotient · to
quickly generate quantifiable ways to evaluate the output · to measure output in a clear and
quantifiable way, **however without indications of comprehensively measuring real creativity.**"

## Key Takeaways

1. Decide *why* you are measuring creativity and *what kind* of creative work — that choice picks
   the model.
2. Work → taxonomy; person → Guilford; against a program → requirements model; social value →
   Csikszentmihalyi.
3. Every model has a named blind spot; use more than one and look for convergence.
4. The taxonomy's five levels (imitation → variation → combination → transformation → innovative
   creation) are the fastest reusable creativity scale.
5. Guilford's fluency / flexibility / originality / elaboration (+ connections) can be run as a
   three-minute, 12-circle warm-up.
6. Measure creative potential (person, process, place) *and* creative output (product,
   persuasion) — neither works alone.
7. Persuasion is a measurable dimension, not a soft extra.
