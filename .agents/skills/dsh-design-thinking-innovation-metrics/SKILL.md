## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. EXPLORE vs EXPLOIT — the split that governs every metric decision
The book's first move is always to ask which engine you are measuring. EXPLORE (the *jungle*) is
creativity, experimentation, and new capability under high uncertainty. EXPLOIT (the
*superhighway*) is efficiency, cost, output, and ROI under low uncertainty. They run on different
values and activities, so they need different metrics.
**Decision rule:** *"Metrics based on the one-size-fits-all pattern for EXPLORE and EXPLOIT often
do not achieve the desired goal."* The book's closing line repeats it: metrics that evaluate the
existing business usually have **no relevance** to innovation initiatives.

### 2. The four building blocks of the Innovation Measurement System (IMS)
1. **North Star metric(s)** — usually a *set*, defined at vision/strategy time
2. **Minimum viable set of exploration metrics** — before product/market fit
3. **Appropriate mix of relevant exploitation metrics** — after product/market fit
4. **Performance measurements (OKRs)** — cross-cutting alignment
**Decision rule:** apply the family whose *"best time to be applied"* matches where the initiative
sits on the Problem to Growth and Scale Framework.

### 3. The six levels for defining metrics
Individual → Team → Organization/Culture → Design Challenge/Project → Portfolio → Business
Ecosystems/Scale. Team-and-culture levels measure behavior, mindset, capability. Project-and-business
levels measure from tool-level activity up to EXPLORE/EXPLOIT portfolio balance.
**Decision rule:** choose the level deliberately before choosing the metric. The book explicitly
rejects a full stakeholder × function matrix as over-engineering.

### 4. Observation → Quantification → Measurement → Metric
A metric is *"a meaningful derivative of measure"* — a calculation between two or more measures
with defined business meaning, not a raw count. The book's own chain: 98 solution tests (quantification)
+ $4,000 testing expense (measurement) → **$20 average cost per new insight in one micro design
cycle** (metric).
**Decision rule:** if you have stopped at a count, you have not yet built a metric.

### 5. Vanity metrics, and the sanity check that kills them
Vanity metrics *"feel good, are great for marketing, and are easy to visualize"* but give no clear
indication of concrete action. The four classics to avoid: **number of ideas · number of patents ·
R&D spending · number of employees in R&D**.
**Decision rule (the fastest test in the book):** *"Ask what the team did differently if figures
went up, went down, or stayed the same. If there is no answer, put those metrics aside and look
for better ones."*

### 6. Minimum Viable Exploration Metrics (MVM)
*"MVMs inform business decisions by measuring means relative to ends. They are best applied before
product/market fit, with a strong focus on learning and improving."*
**Defaults:** start with **4–5** metrics. Express each as a simple team question ("Did the team
conduct at least five needfinding interviews this week? How many?"). Cycle: set up experiment →
measure → evaluate → adapt → operationalize. Expect attrition — some metrics have short lifespans
by design.

### 7. Kill metrics
*"Starting something isn't the hard part — killing an idea is."* Canonical form: **"If X hasn't
happened until [date], we'll shut it down."** Define them at the *start* of a Moonshot challenge,
before emotional investment. Metrics like insight velocity **separate the success of each idea
(ROI) from the success of the team** — a team can succeed by proving an initiative will fail.
**Decision rule:** *"Realizing and informing that an innovation initiative will not be successful
generates immediate ROI simply by stopping the project."*

### 8. Health metrics — the EXPLOIT alternative to classic KPIs
*"Health metrics describe a business's vital signs, much like physical vital signs."* A metric
qualifies only when **all five** hold: it is one of **5–7** critical viability measures; measured
**weekly**; the responsible team can **directly influence** it; it **links to the North Star**; and
it is measured against a **threshold, not a target** (baselined on the last 12 months).

### 9. The IMS Maturity Model (0 → 3)
**IMS 0 Experimental** (manual data collection, static information) → **IMS 1 Professionalized**
(key metrics operationalized, single-mode automation, dynamic information) → **IMS 2 Data Driven**
(advanced forecasting, hybrid model, multimodal automation, real-time) → **IMS 3 AI Driven**
(AI part of objective setting, dynamic multimodal automation).
**Decision rule:** most companies start at 0 or 1. *"Not every company is aiming to reach IMS 3."*
The IMS must fit aspiration level, culture, and business alignment.

### 10. Moonshot / Roofshot / Everest
| | Success criterion | Context | Mix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Moonshot** | 60–70% achievement | Exploring new market opportunities | 1 in 3 |
| **Roofshot** | 100% achievement | Exploiting existing opportunities | 2 in 3 |
| **Everest** | Something new to customer *and* market | Business ecosystem initiatives | Substitutes for Moonshot in fast-changing environments |
**Decision rule:** objectives are qualitative and outcome-oriented; key results are quantitative
with a start value and an end value, and **never** a solution, task, or classic KPI.

### 11. Align for impact, do not cascade
*"Traditionally, cascading is nothing more than cutting and pasting goals from the top to teams.
Aligning for impact means that teams and individuals consider where they can have the greatest
impact on priorities."* At least 50% of objectives bottom-up. **Do not agree individual OKRs in
EXPLORE.** Never link OKR achievement directly to bonuses — it invites gaming.

### 12. Leading paired with lagging
Leading indicators measure the activities needed to reach the goal and can be tracked ongoing;
lagging indicators confirm later-stage results. *"North Star, exploration, and exploitation metrics
might be leading or lagging, and very often work best when combined."*
**Decision rule:** name the lagging outcome you care about, then decompose it into the leading
steps you can move this week (macro cycle length → average process step length).

### 13. The hybrid model — design thinking + data analytics
*"While design thinking is still the main cornerstone to find out about the WHY, with data
analytics, decisions are based on what future customers really want. Together they ensure that
risky bets are not made based on instinct only, but also on evidence."*
**The step change:** at IMS 2, create **new metrics from the data** rather than defining or
adapting metrics based on assumptions.

### 14. Neurodesign — measuring the other 90%
Roughly 10% of the customer's decision layer is declarative ("what someone says") and correlates
weakly with actual purchase behavior; ~90% is subconscious. Neurodesign adds implicit data (EEG,
biometrics, face coding, eye tracking, hyperscanning) to design thinking's empathy and market
data's explicit signals for a more complete view.
**Decision rule:** single neuro tools each add resolution; only the **combined** toolbox produces
the step change — and neuroscience alone cannot read minds.

### 15. The golden rule
**Start applying the metric when it matters, and not too soon.**

## Scope & Limits

**Edition and source.** First edition, John Wiley & Sons, 2023. ISBN 9781119983651
(paperback), 9781119983668 (ePub), 9781119983804 (ePDF), 9781119983675 (print replica). Cover
design and illustrations by Rukaiya Karim. Preface by Ash Maurya. 388 pages.

**Position in a series.** The book is explicitly a complement to *The Design Thinking Playbook*
(overall context), *The Design Thinking Toolbox* (methods — "more than 500 methods and tools"),
and *Design Thinking for Business Growth* (business ecosystem design). It repeatedly defers to
those volumes for creativity techniques, prototype types, business ecosystem design methodology,
and scaling tools. This skill covers only what is in *this* book.

**What is covered.** Selecting, designing, and operationalizing metrics across the whole design
cycle: North Star, exploration, exploitation, health, creativity, and OKR measurement; the IMS
maturity ladder; the statistical and analytics toolkit needed to support it; and the forward look
into data-driven innovation, AI, and neurodesign.

**What is not covered.** The book deliberately keeps mathematics and theorems shallow ("covered in
as little depth as possible"); do not treat Chapter 14 as a statistics reference. Detailed
creativity techniques, prototype-type catalogues, and full business ecosystem design methodology
live in the companion volumes.

**What dates the material.** Written in 2022–2023 against an expectation of global recession and
new geopolitical challenges, and citing the WEF (2022) globalization scenarios. AI content
predates the generative-AI wave that followed publication: it treats "general AI" as a ~2020s
milestone and "advanced AI" as ~2040, and its named tools (Tableau, Power BI, OpenRefine, Data
Ladder, Grafana) reflect 2022 tooling. Metaverse workshops and neurotech are presented as emerging
practice. The Alphabet OKR figures are stated as of 2022 (140,000 employees). Named budget and
compensation rules of thumb (10% of revenues / 1% of profits to EXPLORE; up to 20% of exec comp on
EXPLORE metrics) are the author's practice-based heuristics, not benchmarks.

**Extraction quality.** The source is a heavily illustrated, hand-lettered book; the text layer is
complete but several graphical labels degraded in extraction. Where this affected content, the
chapter notes say so explicitly: (a) two of the seven North Star growth-strategy labels in
Chapter 9 are partly obscured; (b) the headings of recommendations 1 and 2 in the "General
recommendation to manage metrics" box in Chapter 8 did not extract, though their bullets did;
(c) the final bar label of the neurodesign impact chart in Chapter 19 is degraded. Nothing has
been invented to fill these gaps.

**Companion site.** The book directs readers to www.design-metrics.com for downloadable templates
of every canvas referenced here. Availability is not verified by this skill.
