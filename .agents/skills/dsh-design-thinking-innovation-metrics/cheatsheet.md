# Cheatsheet — Design Thinking and Innovation Metrics

Decision tables, thresholds, selection matrices, tells and smells, and process templates from
Michael Lewrick, *Design Thinking and Innovation Metrics* (Wiley, 2023).

## 1. The Metric-Selection Matrix

### 1a. Pick the metric family by phase and uncertainty

| Level of uncertainty | Position in the Problem to Growth and Scale Framework | Metric family to apply | Cause & effect focus | Typical metric types | Book examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| — (strategy setting) | Definition of vision, strategy, strategic choice | **North Star metric(s)** | Impact and actions | Behavior change, ratios, rates | Attentions, engagements, transactions, frequencies, productivity, reach |
| **High** | Design thinking, research; before problem/solution fit | **Minimum viable set of exploration metrics** | Learning, improving, effectiveness | Ratios/rates, new capabilities, new behavior, unit economies | Time-to-insight, budget-to-development, time-to-adaption, design thinking maturity level |
| **Medium** | Co-creation, lean start-up; toward product/market fit | **Exploration metrics tightening into exploitation** | Validation of viability and value proposition | Mixed | Cost from problem definition to product/market fit; # lead customers in MVP testing |
| **Low** | Business ecosystem design, agile customer development; after product/market fit or systems/actors fit | **Appropriate mix of relevant exploitation metrics** | Effectiveness and efficiency | Gross quantities and unit economies | Profitability, ROI, growth of users/customers |
| Any | Cross-cutting, all levels | **Performance measurements (OKRs)** | Alignment and prioritization | Leading indicators, outcome metrics | Objectives with 2–5 key results per objective |

### 1b. Pick by what you are trying to learn

| If the question is… | Use | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Are we working on a problem worth solving? | Team confidence level (Low / Not sure / High, 4 questions); # of critical and unknown assumptions identified | Ch 6 |
| Are we learning fast enough? | Customer interview velocity; insight velocity; time to insight; % change from unknown to known per iteration | Ch 10 |
| Should we keep going? | Kill metrics with explicit thresholds; probability of innovation success (Bayes + XYZ hypothesis) | Ch 10, Ch 6 |
| Is this idea any good? | Scoring on desirability / feasibility / viability; exploration grid (return × evidence) | Ch 6 |
| Did the experiment teach us anything? | Confidence level C = Insights × Evidence (1–9); love it / change it / leave it | Ch 6 |
| How creative is the *work*? | Nilsson's Taxonomy (imitation → variation → combination → transformation → innovative creation) | Ch 12 |
| How creative is the *person*? | Guilford model: fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration, connections | Ch 12 |
| Does the work meet a defined program? | Requirements model | Ch 12 |
| Does the field value the work? | Csikszentmihalyi's system model (person / domain / field) | Ch 12 |
| Is the team healthy? | Trust between teams and top management; conflicts resolved constructively; decision speed; learning agility; authenticity; presence | Ch 13 |
| Is the culture shifting? | % of employees in capability programs; # using free-time-to-experiment; % motivated by intrinsic motivation; C-level calendar time for EXPLORE | Ch 2, Ch 13 |
| Is the portfolio balanced? | Innovation portfolio balance (EXPLORE vs EXPLOIT); ratio between retire and explore; % core/adjacent/transformative; portfolio vitality index; risk-adjusted NPV | Ch 11, Ch 13 |
| Is the business viable right now? | 5–7 health metrics (weekly, threshold-based) | Ch 11 |
| Is the ecosystem working? | Ecosystem capital; engagement level of marquee actors; satisfaction of actors; contribution margin per transaction | Ch 6, Ch 13 |
| Will it scale? | Number of customer adoption in X-time vs market size; customer stickiness; unit cost per transaction vs IT investments to scale | Ch 6 |
| Did the capability programme work? | Capability development + project delivery + behavioral (three-part acceptance criteria); maturity level movement | Ch 4 |

### 1c. North Star vs Exploration vs Exploitation — the master table

| | **North Star metric(s)** | **Minimum viable exploration metrics** | **Appropriate mix of exploitation metrics** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Description** | Growth ambitions and directions | Inform business decisions by measuring means relative to ends | Quantify business performance for scaling, efficiency, cost savings |
| **Best applied** | Vision, strategy, strategic choice | Before product/market fit | After product/market fit or systems/actors fit |
| **Focus** | Impact and actions | Learning, improving, effectiveness | Effectiveness and efficiency |
| **Represents** | Company, organization | Company, organization, team/individual behavior | Performance of company and activities |
| **Types** | Behavior change, ratios, rates | Ratios/rates, new capabilities, new behavior, unit economies | Gross quantities and unit economies |

## 3. Tells and Smells — symptom → diagnosis → fix

| Symptom | Diagnosis | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Impressive innovation dashboards, no decisions change | Vanity/activity metrics ("miracle numbers") | Run the sanity check; classify the evidence; replace with actionable ratios |
| "How many new ideas did we come up with this year?" is a board question | Counting ideas as the innovation KPI | Kill the four classic vanity metrics: # ideas, # patents, R&D spend, R&D headcount |
| Teams generate thousands of ideas | You measured # of ideas created | Measure learning outcomes from experiments instead |
| Calendars end-to-end booked with workshops | You measured # of co-creation workshops performed | Measure customer interaction outcomes, not workshop volume |
| Teams look operationally busy but nothing lands | The North Star no longer fits; the S-curve inflection was missed | Redefine the North Star; start the second curve before the first peaks |
| Agile installed nine months ago and still no results | Design thinking assumed to be part of the agile toolbox; problem-space skills missing | Begin at the beginning: problem statement, empathy, PoV before ideation |
| Ideation consuming most of the calendar | Solution-space bias; <5% on the first two quarters | Redraw the Double Diamond asymmetrically; measure customer interactions and team confidence |
| Late changes and rework are the norm | Problem work skipped; cost of change now `$$$+` | Move effort left; track the ratio of time in problem definition vs implementation refinements |
| Solutions die at launch (too expensive, no acceptance, already exists) | Assumptions never validated with customers | Search for behavior, need, pains, gains, JTBD, use cases before building |
| Interviews only confirm what the team already believed | Structured interview guide | Use open-ended questions and a question map |
| The measurement system is inherited and untouched | Historically grown, EXPLOIT-shaped | Score against the 10 principles; fewer, modified metrics get accepted faster than new ones |
| Employees miss targets and get blamed | Efficiency measured where effectiveness was wanted | Fix the system of measurement, not the individuals |
| Metrics get gamed over time | Top-down measurement plus bonus linkage | Never link OKR achievement directly to bonuses; make objectives team-originated |
| Objectives read like to-do lists | Tactics smuggled into objectives | Golden rule #4: no tactics; key results are never tasks |
| OKR set is all Moonshots and nothing lands | Ambition mix wrong | One Moonshot to two Roofshots; substitute Everest in dynamic environments |
| Google's OKR system copied wholesale and stalling | Blueprint imported without culture fit | Fit the scoring system and cadence to culture and North Star; start with pilot teams |
| Teams struggle to explain the WHY of their work | No clear North Star | Define North Star; without it, autonomy produces chaos |
| Organization acts chaotically | High autonomy with low common alignment | Alignment/autonomy matrix — supply the North Star |
| Management monitors micro tasks | Low autonomy with high alignment | Shift control → trust; move decision rights to the team closest to the content |
| EXPLOIT portfolio crowded with cash cows kept alive | Portfolio overweight; sunk-cost defence | Evidence-based retire decisions: prove growth potential, strategic benefit, or technological viability do not exist |
| Ecosystem initiative measured on ROI and cash burn | Lagging indicators applied at initialization | Use ecosystem capital, MVE configuration quality, actor engagement instead |
| Absolute revenue numbers quoted at product/market fit | Unverifiable early predictions | Quote a *range* of possible revenues |
| An outstanding data scientist never talks to design teams | Shadow player in the ONA | Connect via knowledge brokers; the attrition and stagnation risk is real |
| Prototype feedback is positive but the launch flops | Declarative (≈10%) layer only; subconscious layer unmeasured | Add neuro measures: EEG, biometrics, face coding, eye tracking |
| AI brainstorm produces a feature list | Feature-first pitfall | Redirect to customer experience ideas; implement features later |
| Only specialists can see the analytics | Unequal access to advanced analytics | Democratize tools; misalignment and dissatisfaction follow inequality |
| Dashboard has dozens of metrics nobody reads | Dashboard fever | Give metrics expiration dates; put the eye-catching metric top-left |
| Chart uses 3D, unsorted pie slices, or a non-zero baseline | Misleading visualization | Column chart; sort descending; baseline at zero |

## 4. Process Templates

### 4a. The recommended procedure for starting to measure
1. **Explain the purpose and goals** — is the focus EXPLORE or EXPLOIT?
2. **Be clear about what is measured** — pick the level: individual, team, culture, project, portfolio, ecosystem
3. **Work with minimum viable exploration metrics before fixing anything** — measure, evaluate, operationalize
4. **Find alignment between business and teams** — vague OKRs without a North Star lead to wrong actions
5. **Measure at different levels** — even tool-level measurement yields first insights

### 4b. Five key questions before measuring anything
1. Why is it important to make this measurement, and what decision could be derived from it?
2. What consequences can be observed when the decisions are implemented?
3. How much knowledge about the problem exists and is known to the team?
4. When and where does a value or metric make a difference?
5. How much is the additional information worth and what impact will it create?

### 4c. The 10 principles for assessing an existing metric
Significance and importance · describes a whole phenomenon · parameters accessible and manageable ·
collection effort and value in healthy relationship · understandable and comprehensible · precise
with clear resulting measures · created in real time or timely · communicated transparently ·
meaningful and credible from different perspectives · factual and evidence based.

### 4d. Designing a minimum viable exploration metric — seven steps
1. State assumptions linking the activity to success
2. Check the vision/North Star and innovation strategy principles
3. Design potential metrics as simple team questions (leading indicators)
4. Start measuring — compare across teams
5. Evaluate actionable metrics over the entire design cycle
6. Discuss operationalization (aggregation window, early-warning value)
7. Derive questions, indicators, and actions from the results

### 4e. The data analysis + operationalization process
**I. Data analysis:** define the question → design experiment → set up experiment (build, clean,
merge) → conduct experiment → visualize and share.
**II. Operationalization:** implement (real-time analytics, metrics in decision and review
processes) → manage change (procedures and communication plan) → capture impact (measure impact on
innovation, business success, customer satisfaction) → **adaption**.

### 4f. The OKR cycle (quarterly rhythm)
Setting/(re)defining/aligning individual OKRs → team OKRs → making company OKRs transparent →
defining scoring system and retrospective → drafting temporary worksheet with company objectives →
measuring, discussing, (re)prioritizing → mid-quarter review → weekly check-ins → executing
missions and tasks. Anchored by North Star and strategic choice; ends in outcomes.

### 4g. The OKR toolkit six phases
`ENVISION` (strategic foresight; What if / Imagine that / How might we) → `FOCUS` (3–5 objectives,
ambition level) → `CREATE` (3–4 missions for 12 months) → `DEFINE` (success criteria then key
results) → `MOBILIZE` (skills, communication, funding) → `(RE)DEFINE` (measure, discuss,
reprioritize).

### 4h. The check-in — four steps
Current progress → predicted score and confidence level → roadblocks and constraints (what is
solvable, what must be accepted) → concrete adjustments, measurements, action plans (what, next
steps, who can help). Spend more time on low-confidence OKRs.

### 4i. Kill-metric setup
1. Define at the **start** of the challenge, before emotional investment
2. Use the form "If X hasn't happened until [date], we'll shut it down"
3. Cover: early customer feedback · willingness to pay · prototype cost vs budget · quality and
   quantity of critical functions · technology readiness
4. Give the persevere / pivot / kill decision to the team
5. Fund according to board risk tolerance, not stage gates

### 4j. Health Metrics Canvas columns
Describe metrics (why is it a health metric? how was it measured before?) · level of concern
(threshold) · source of data (and calculation) · previous average (defined period, e.g. 12 months)
· metric owner.

### 4k. Neurodesign project sequence
Empathy & ethnographic (*we know from memory… / we expect…*) → critical assumptions to be verified
→ select tools (hyperscanning, heart rate, eye tracking, EEG, biometrics, face coding,
self-report) → set up the experiment → capture, analyze, visualize → state limitations → report
results and onward use in ontologies, neural networks, and AI.

## 5. Quick Reference Cards

### The four building blocks of the IMS
1. North Star metric(s) · 2. Minimum viable set of exploration metrics ·
3. Appropriate set of exploitation metrics · 4. Performance measurements (OKRs)

### The six measurement levels
Individual · Team · Organization/Culture · Design Challenge/Project · Portfolio ·
Business Ecosystems (→ Transition to Scale)

### The seven North Star principles
Quantifiable · represents vision · aligned to strategy · leading indicator · actionable ·
easy to understand · no vanity metrics

### The five health-metric criteria
One of 5–7 critical viability measures · weekly · directly influenceable by the responsible team ·
linked to strategic priorities/North Star · threshold-based, not target-based

### The IMS maturity ladder
IMS 0 Experimental (manual, static) → IMS 1 Professionalized (single-mode automation, dynamic) →
IMS 2 Data Driven (multimodal automation, real-time, hybrid model) → IMS 3 AI Driven (dynamic
multimodal, AI in objective setting). *Not every company should target IMS 3.*

### The seven tips for building a measurement system
1. Hard to measure what hasn't happened — focus on team and capabilities
2. One size does not fit all — distinguish EXPLORE and EXPLOIT
3. Keep it simple — create acceptance
4. Leverage existing measurement systems — focus on relevant metrics
5. Don't be afraid to modify — love it, change it, or leave it
6. Focus on the customer with the metric — create actions
7. Don't let the wrong metrics stifle innovation — create transition into exploitation

### The golden rule
**Start applying the metric when it matters, and not too soon.**

### The closing rule
**Metrics used to evaluate the existing business (EXPLOIT) usually have no relevance to innovation
initiatives (EXPLORE).**
