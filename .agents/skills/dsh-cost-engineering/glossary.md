# Glossary — Cost Engineering (Chris Domanski, CRC Press, 2020)

Entries marked from the book's own Glossary (pp. 141–143) are reproduced in the author's wording; the rest are defined in the chapter text at the chapter noted.

**Activity-based costing (ABC)** — Method that identifies the activities a company performs, assigns all costs to those activities by causality, then assigns activity costs to the products or services they support using drivers; the most accurate allocation method (Ch 1, 5)
**Additive manufacturing (3D printing)** — Layering metal or plastic into part shape using heat; cost implications explicitly not covered by the book (Ch 4)
**Annual production hours** — Total number of hours in a year that machines are able to operate; a variable of the shift pattern/model (Glossary)
**Barrel plating** — Plating small parts by rotating a cage of parts through chemical baths; unsuitable for parts with critical features (Ch 4)
**Benchmarking / teardown** — Tearing down competitors' products and comparing them to your own on a costed, component-by-component basis (Ch 6)
**Best in class (BIC)** — An estimate scenario assuming the best available process/equipment rather than the supplier's actual condition (Ch 3)
**Bottleneck (gate station)** — The station in a manufacturing process that takes the longest time; parts cannot be produced faster than that station, and it usually determines the part's cycle time (Glossary, Ch 3)
**Burden** — The overhead cost (Glossary)
**Business case estimate** — Estimate over the full program life including cost escalation, cash flows, IRR and payback; contrasted with point-in-time estimating (Ch 2)
**Capacity utilization** — The percentage of a facility or its equipment that is used for production out of total available production time; e.g. running two of three shifts is 66.67% (Glossary, Ch 2)
**Capital allocation method** — Allocating overhead in proportion to machines' original acquisition cost (Ch 5)
**Cavitation** — Number of cavities per mold; the primary lever on process cost per part in casting, die casting and molding (Ch 2, 4)
**Change management control** — Disciplined evaluation, tracking and reporting of the cost impact of post-award design and process changes; supported by ERP or PLM (Ch 2)
**Clean sheet estimate** — Also "ground up"; every cost component estimated without prior historical cost assumptions (Glossary, Ch 1)
**Coil plating (reel-to-reel)** — Plating parts still attached to a coil by running them through several small baths; allows vertical control of which section is plated (Ch 4)
**Collect & compare** — The typical non-cost-based sourcing technique: gather competing quotes and take the lowest, without knowledge of underlying cost (Ch 3)
**Contribution margin** — Difference between price and all variable costs; used as an allocation base, the least accurate method (Ch 5)
**Corporate overhead** — See SG&A
**Cost accounting** — Accounting practice of setting up a system to track costs as they occur and tie them to areas or departments; not a cost engineering tool (Ch 1)
**Cost center** — Collection of resources that accumulates cost (factory, department, equipment center); objectives centered on improving cost (Glossary)
**Cost engineering** — Engineering a company's products to meet pre-defined cost requirements; sometimes called design to cost; the umbrella over the twelve methodologies (Ch 1)
**Cost estimating** — Estimating the cost of products being purchased and produced, ideally from the ground up (Ch 1)
**Cost modeling** — Deeper analysis anchored on P&L data that assigns cost by activity and driver and predicts the effect of structural change (e.g. utilization shifts) (Ch 1)
**Cost of capital** — Financing cost on investment; per Table 3.6, chargeable on investment but not on expenses, and at actual rather than internal hurdle rates (Ch 2, 3)
**Cost reduction workshop** — Time-boxed (3–5 day) cross-functional brainstorming event using teardown, functional analysis, TRIZ and VSM (Ch 6)
**Cumulative scrap** — Scrap accumulated through the process sequence, where each step's scrapped part carries the material and processing of all prior steps (Ch 2, 4)
**Custom assembly** — Product-specific assembly process that cannot be shared with another product type (Ch 4)
**Cycle time** — Time between manufacture of each part, or how fast parts come out at the end of the process — "drop to drop"; a variable of throughput (Glossary, Ch 2)
**De-gating** — Separating sprue, runners and risers from cast parts for re-melting (Ch 4)
**Depreciation** — Accounting device spreading an investment over years to smooth income; not an accurate measure of cost because past investment is sunk (Ch 2)
**Design to cost** — Alternative name for cost engineering (Ch 1)
**Die casting** — Forcing molten aluminium or magnesium under pressure into a die; presses 135–3,500 tons, drop-to-drop 35–65 s (Ch 4)
**Direct labor** — Workers who add value to the product during manufacturing, plus those who touch it without adding value (sorting, inspection) (Ch 2)
**DISAMATIC®** — Registered trademark of DISA Industries A/S; the automated sand casting system used as the chapter's example (Ch 4)
**Dross loss** — Oxidation loss skimmed off molten metal; the "soup froth" analogy (Ch 4)
**Drop-to-drop time** — Time between parts being removed from a machine after completion (Ch 4)
**EBIT** — Operating profit: profitability before interest and taxes; gross profit minus operating expenses (corporate overhead) (Ch 2)
**EBITDA** — EBIT also excluding depreciation and amortization, used as a cash flow proxy (Ch 2)
**ED&T (engineering, development & testing)** — Customer-specific R&D cost where engineering resources develop existing products for specific applications only (Glossary, Ch 3)
**Efficiency factor** — Percentage of total available production time in which machines are producing parts; the inverse of inefficiency (Glossary, Ch 2)
**Expendable packaging** — Packaging used once then thrown away or recycled, e.g. cardboard boxes (Glossary, Ch 2)
**Fixed cost** — Cost that does not vary in the short to medium term, e.g. buildings or executive management (Glossary)
**Flash** — See offal
**Forging** — Hammering a billet into a die cavity with multiple hits; cold for smaller parts, hot for larger; losses up to 30% (Ch 4)
**Fringe benefits** — Benefits afforded workers: health and life insurance, vacations, bonuses, cafeteria and transportation services (Glossary); in the connector example $1.50/hr on a $3.00/hr wage (Ch 2)
**Fully fringed labor rate** — Wage plus fringe benefits, e.g. $4.50/hr (Ch 2)
**Functional analysis** — VA/VE technique: identify every function a product performs, cost each, and find ways to deliver the same or better function at lower cost (Ch 1, 6)
**Gap closure process** — The nine-step cost-based negotiation flow from mandatory cost breakdown to agreed price (Ch 3)
**Grenzplankostenrechnung (GPK)** — German costing methodology for consistent, accurate assignment of managerial costs to a product or service (Glossary)
**Gross margin / gross profit** — Total sales revenue minus COGS (excluding corporate overhead), divided by revenue, as a percentage (Ch 2)
**Gross weight** — Part weight including all discarded material (runner, sprue, gate, offal); the weight that must be costed (Ch 2)
**Incoterm** — International commercial term defining carrier and insurance responsibilities in shipment; wall chart available from the ICC (Ch 2)
**In-circuit testing (ICT)** — Post-SMT test confirming all circuits in a finished PCBA are operational; machine ~$50–$150k (Ch 4)
**Indirect labor** — Workers supporting manufacturing without directly performing it: in-process material handlers, shift supervisors, machine and maintenance technicians, manufacturing and quality engineers (Ch 2)
**Inefficiency factor** — Percentage of total available production time in which machines are not producing parts (Glossary); 15% in the connector example (Ch 2)
**Insert molding / over-molding** — Molding in which metal components are inserted into the tool before molding, usually by robot (Ch 4)
**Inventory holding cost** — The financing cost of producing parts that cannot be sold for months; in the connector case $270.47/yr on $9,911 of inventory at 5% (Ch 3)
**IRR (internal rate of return)** — Return metric in the business case; 91%, 36% and 20% in the three connector scenarios (Ch 2)
**Just-in-time (JIT)** — Japanese system in which components are delivered only when needed for production (Glossary, Ch 6)
**Kaizen** — Japanese continuous improvement methodology, usually paired with target costing (Ch 1)
**Kanban** — Japanese system regulating component supply through an instruction card sent along the production line, to improve efficiency and reduce inventory (Glossary, Ch 6)
**Labor allocation method** — Allocating overhead as a fixed multiple of labor cost or operator count (Ch 5)
**Lean manufacturing** — Optimization focused on cycle time, OEE and inventory (Ch 6)
**Long-term agreement (LTA)** — Agreement securing future business with a supplier for a period in exchange for future price reductions, an upfront lump sum payment, or both (Glossary, Ch 2)
**Lump sum payment distortion** — Requiring suppliers to pay upfront to secure business; effectively a high-interest loan to the customer, baked back into future pricing (Ch 2)
**Machine rate** — Hourly rate per machine built from directly attributable costs (space, electricity, support, maintenance, depreciation) plus a residual plant overhead uplift (Ch 5)
**Management accounting** — Using accounting information to give management decision-making capability rather than a purely historical GAAP view; IMA's preferred tool is ABC (Ch 1)
**Manufacturing lot** — Batch of parts produced in one manufacturing run; the number of lots per year defines the number of setups (Glossary, Ch 2)
**Manufacturing overhead** — Factory cost not tied to a specific product: rent, maintenance, utilities, supplies, outside services, plant management (Ch 2)
**Margin** — Difference between price and cost of goods, e.g. $2 margin on a $12 price is 16.67%; calculated as X ÷ (1 − rate) (Glossary, Ch 2)
**Marginal costing** — Including only costs that vary with the decision in the decision analysis (Ch 1)
**Markup** — Amount added to cost of goods to cover overhead and profit, e.g. 20% markup on $10 gives a $12 price; calculated as X × (1 + rate); commonly confused with margin (Glossary, Ch 2)
**Material overhead** — Cost of warehousing and in-plant handling of material plus procurement staff (buyers, material planners) (Ch 2)
**Mock negotiation** — Training exercise splitting trainees into customer and supplier teams with private briefs and negotiation trackers (Ch 3)
**Motion study** — Supplier analysis determining, motion by motion, how long an operator takes to perform all required movements (Ch 3)
**Nesting** — Layout of parts in the working piece of a stamping to minimize offal (Ch 4)
**Net weight** — Weight of the finished part as specified in the engineering drawing (Ch 2)
**OEE (overall equipment effectiveness)** — Percentage of total available production time in which good parts are produced; unlike efficiency, it also discounts time spent making scrap (Glossary, Ch 2)
**Offal** — Raw material scrap discarded during manufacturing due to mismatch of incoming and outgoing shapes; also called flash in molding (Ch 2, 4)
**Payback** — Years to recover an investment; used instead of a flat profit percentage to price a project (Ch 2, 3)
**Payment terms** — Terms defining how quickly a supplier is paid, e.g. 90 days after receipt; longer terms raise piece price because suppliers carry higher borrowing cost (Glossary, Ch 3)
**Planned downtime** — Time machines are down for scheduled breaks such as lunches or maintenance (Glossary)
**Point in time estimate** — Estimate based on today's or past cost with no allowance for future fluctuation; the most common type (Ch 2)
**Powder metal (PM) sintering** — Press powder metal to shape, sinter in a furnace so granules melt together, then size/trim and test (Ch 4)
**PPAP** — Production test run information used to verify a supplier's declared process (Ch 3)
**Pre-agreed cost model** — A cost model simulating the supplier's cost structure, agreed by both sides in advance so estimates can be made without an RFQ (Ch 3)
**Processing time** — Time to process each part from beginning to end of the manufacturing process (Glossary)
**Productivity giveback** — Customer-required annual price reduction; drives suppliers to inflate first-year price to cover future reductions (Ch 2)
**Profit center** — Collection of resources that accumulates profit; objectives centered on improving profit (Glossary)
**Progressive die** — Stamping method for smaller parts: a coiled strip fed automatically through a press with multiple tool stations (Ch 4)
**Rack plating** — Plating larger parts hung on racks and dipped in baths; the most labor-intensive plating method (Ch 4)
**Regrind** — Discarded molding resin ground to pellet size and reused; otherwise resold at about 10% of original value (Ch 2, 4)
**Returnable packaging** — Packaging reused multiple times, e.g. plastic trays (Glossary, Ch 2)
**Reverse engineered P&L** — Rebuilding a supplier's plant P&L from plant-visit data (headcount, square footage, capital employed) to test quoted overhead rates (Ch 3)
**Rework** — Cost of repairing parts initially scrapped; correspondingly reduces scrap cost (Glossary)
**Run@rate** — Automotive assessment validating a supplier's ability to produce enough parts for production, usually before SOP (Glossary)
**Sand casting** — Pouring molten iron into a cavity formed in wet sand held in a box; grey or ductile iron differing by graphite content (Ch 4)
**Sandbagging** — Quoting higher than necessary cost to mitigate future risk (Ch 2, 7)
**SAVE International** — Professional organization fostering VA/VE methods across industries (Ch 1, 6)
**Scrap** — Material and parts discarded during manufacturing; ranges from ~0.5% (automated assembly) to ~30% (decorative painting) (Ch 2)
**Setup** — Cost of setting up tools and equipment for a production run; small per piece at high volume, dominant at low volume (Ch 2)
**SG&A (selling, general and administrative)** — Corporate overhead under US GAAP: sales and marketing, non-manufacturing finance/accounting/HR/IT, top management, and their buildings; R&D usually included under GAAP, often separate under IFRS (Ch 2)
**Shift pattern/model** — The set of production shifts, their length and timing through a production week, e.g. 3 shifts, 5 days (Glossary)
**Shot weight** — Total resin injected per molding cycle; gross weight per part × number of cavities (Ch 2)
**Should costing** — Synonym for cost estimating; "should" implies attainability but the accuracy limits are identical (Ch 1)
**Skilled labor** — Operators with advanced machine operating training, e.g. machinists on advanced machining centers (Glossary)
**SMT (surface mount technology)** — Placing electronic components on a PCB: solder paste stencilling, robotic pick-and-place, reflow oven, camera inspection (Ch 4)
**Speeds and feeds** — Machining tool rotational speed and the rate the tool is pushed through material; determines cycle time; calculation is out of the book's scope (Ch 4)
**Standard costing** — Cost accounting tool defining budgeted hourly costs per product and manufacturing step and tracking actual against them (Ch 1)
**Standard hour allocation** — Allocating cost per standard machine hour derived from the previous year's P&L (Ch 5)
**Stamping** — Forming metal in presses; transfer die for larger parts, progressive die for smaller (Ch 4)
**Startup cost** — Cost to launch production, e.g. hiring and training crews before SOP or financing equipment purchased in advance (Glossary)
**TAKT time** — Time between manufacture of each part needed to meet customer demand; often confused with cycle time, which can be lower (Glossary)
**Target cost** — Market price minus the desired profit margin; the cost a product cannot exceed (Ch 1)
**Target costing** — Setting cost targets for every component during development; regimented by Japanese carmakers and tied to kaizen (Ch 1)
**Theory of constraints** — Principle that sequenced stations are all charged at the bottleneck's cycle time; the book cites Goldratt's *The Goal* (Ch 3)
**Throughput** — Amount of parts producible in a given time, e.g. 300 parts per hour (Glossary)
**Tooling and fixtures** — Tools and holding fixtures required for manufacturing; three cost elements are design, raw material/purchased components, and tool/fixture manufacture; not in piece price (Ch 2)
**Total cost of ownership (TCO)** — All costs to a buyer over a product's total life: concept development, manufacturing, quality and warranty, disposal, servicing (Glossary)
**Transfer die** — Stamping method for larger parts: sheets loaded and unloaded across multiple presses by hand or robot (Ch 4)
**TRIZ** — Russian acronym for theory of the resolution of invention related tasks; Altshuller's 40 inventive principles derived from patent analysis (Ch 1, 6)
**Unplanned downtime** — Time machines are down for abrupt breaks such as breakdowns or power outages (Glossary)
**Unskilled labor** — Operators requiring no advanced equipment training (Glossary)
**Uptime** — Time a machine was actually running; the opposite of inefficiency (Ch 2)
**Value analysis (VA)** — Cost optimization activity after design freeze or production start (Ch 1, 7)
**Value engineering (VE)** — Cost optimization activity before design freeze or production start (Ch 1, 7)
**Value stream mapping (VSM)** — Map of material movement from customer order through raw material, processing and assembly to delivery, recording operators, cycle times, uptime, inventory and travel at each step (Ch 6)
**Variable cost** — Cost that varies in the short term with production volume, such as machine electricity or supplies (Glossary)
**Waterfall chart** — Graphical Pareto of the gaps between supplier and customer cost estimates by bucket (Ch 3)
**Walk away price** — The minimum acceptable price defined by the quote team, above which sales may negotiate (Ch 2)
**Yield** — Percentage of good parts out of total produced (Glossary)
