# Cheatsheet — Design Thinking for Business Growth (Lewrick, 2022)

## The pipeline, end to end

```
RESEARCH → DESIGN THINKING → LEAN START-UP → ECOSYSTEM DESIGN → IMPLEMENT & RUN → SCALE
 ~6 wks      ~6–10 wks         ~10–12 wks       ~12–16 wks         >16 wks         open end

Customer   → problem/solution → product/market → system/actors   →              → ecosystem/
needs        fit                fit              fit                              coevolution fit
             (final prototype)  (MVP)            (MVE)                            (black ocean)
```

Named outputs per phase: topic areas & capabilities → final + vision prototype, MVP requirements → MVP, WTP, validated assumptions → MVE, value streams, business model, actors → realization plan, product portfolio, scaled IT → expanded value proposition, actor innovation.

## Design lens selector

| Lens | Use when | Criteria | Canvas | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **#1 Design thinking** | Customer needs not yet understood or validated | Desirability + Viability + Feasibility 1 | Design thinking canvas (p. 188) | Final prototype, MVP requirements |
| **#2 Lean start-up** | Solution exists but is unvalidated in market | Desirability + Viability + Feasibility 2 | Lean start-up MVP canvas (p. 210) | MVP, MMF |
| **#3 Ecosystem design** | Value proposition validated, several actors needed | **Adaptability + Value enhanceability** + Feasibility 3 | Ecosystem design canvas (p. 246) | MVE |
| **#4 Scale** | MVE live, exponential growth is the goal | **Captivateability + Rhythmicability** + Feasibility 4 | Exponential growth and scale canvas (p. 272) | Exponential growth |

**Entry rule:** Do not start at #3 unless customer needs are identified *and* validated via MVP. Otherwise the team will treat the customer as one actor among many.

## Decision tables

### Is this actually a business ecosystem?

| Test | Ecosystem | Not an ecosystem |
|---|---|---|
| Opens up to the outside? | Yes | No — centrally controlled platform |
| Transparent about skills and values? | Yes | No — sourcing arrangement |
| Accepts new market roles? | Yes | No — value chain with SLAs |
| Needed for the overriding value proposition? | Yes | No — a normal partnership suffices |

### EXPLOIT or EXPLORE?

| Signal | Choose |
|---|---|
| Leverage existing capabilities, products, customer interfaces, markets | EXPLOIT (ceiling ≈ 2x growth) |
| Emerging needs, new behaviours, new markets, new roles/models | EXPLORE (black ocean potential) |
| Established company, incumbent structures, low change appetite | EXPLOIT, or EXPLORE via a greenfield unit |
| Ambition is exponential growth and ecosystem capital | EXPLORE |

### Which ocean?

| | Red | Blue | Black |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market | Existing | New | Completely new market areas |
| Competitors | Beat | Avoid | Make competition impossible (≤3 perceptible) |
| Demand | Existing | New | Previously unaddressed problems and needs |
| Requires | Efficiency or differentiation | Differentiation and low cost | Other actors, incl. competitors |

### Which role should we take?

| Situation | Role |
|---|---|
| We spotted the need and can fund prototypes/MVP | Initiator (often becomes orchestrator or actor) |
| We can coordinate, set rules, and govern growth | Orchestrator |
| We have capabilities/offerings to integrate; want to innovate within rules | Actor |
| We supply technology, software, infrastructure; no customer relationship | Supplier |
| We receive information or validate data in automated processes | Other player |

Rule of thumb: **5–6 roles suffice** for a definition; **3 roles** usually carry the strategic weight.

### Which network law applies?

| System type | Law | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Centralized business network | Sarnoff | V = n |
| Platform | Metcalfe | V = n² |
| Business ecosystem | Reed | V = 2ⁿ (subgroups scale faster) |

### Channel strategy maturity

| Stage | Definition | Prerequisite |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-channel | Different channels provided (e-mail, website, chat) | — |
| Omni-channel | Seamless integration, optimized through customer journeys | Multi-channel data |
| **Opti-channel** | Best possible interaction as part of the ecosystem journey; chat/voice bots, personalization, later emotional AI | Omni-channel data + AI/BDA/ML |

## Canvas field checklists

### Ecosystem strategy canvas (p. 106)
**Where to play?** — analysis of topic area and environment · customer needs/problem · customer interface, customer relationships
**How to configure?** — value proposition · capabilities (IT, data, infrastructure, AI) · MVPs, MMFs, backlog · organizational design, governance
**How to win?** — ecosystem vision · roles in the ecosystem · business model of the ecosystem · multidimensional view of the business models · growth and scale
**Band** — EXPLOIT (existing partnerships, initiatives, technologies, strategies) | EXPLORE (new ones)

### Design thinking canvas (p. 188)
research starting point and trends · vision (prototype) · requirements for MVP · problem statement · HMW question · critical items · final prototype · persona/user profile · exploration map · lean canvas
Phases: understand → observe → define point of view → find ideas → building prototypes → test

### Lean start-up MVP canvas (p. 210)
**Initial situation** — persona · top 3 problems/challenges · customer journey and use cases
**Plan** — vision and road map · top 3 features · MVP · build · costs and schedule
**Results** — conclusion/next steps (pivot?) · learn · measure

### Ecosystem design canvas (p. 246)
determine the needs of the users/customers · core value proposition · definition of the value streams · describe the actors · design/redesign · prototype, test and improve · analysis of advantages and disadvantages of each actor · multidimensional view of the business models

### Core value proposition canvas (p. 249)
Columns: Customers/Users | Orchestrator/Initiator | Actor/Role
Rows: needs · activities · benefits (centre: approach/solution) · competition/existing alternatives (centre: uniqueness) · value streams · value proposition (centre: core value proposition)

### Exponential growth and scale canvas (p. 272)
solving problems of many · building the customer base and community · expansion of the value proposition · scalable processes, IT, data analytics · leverage from ecosystem actors · ecosystem culture and network effects · leverage of digital, physical, hybrid touch points · optimized cost structure | expanded value streams

### Reflection canvas (p. 290)
**Top** — design lenses · digital fluency · ecosystem leadership · market opportunities
**Middle** — capabilities · mindset · lessons learned (initiative) · lessons learned (meta level) · think of possibilities · principles
**Bottom** — digital (enabler) technologies · big data analytics/AI/ML/DL · capital and assets · governance

## The four loops and ten steps of ecosystem design

| Loop | Step | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Virtuous design** | 1 | Formulation of the key value proposition |
| | 2 | Brainstorming to select and describe the actors |
| | 3 | Allocation of the actors on the ecosystem map |
| | 4 | Definition of the value streams and links between actors |
| | 5 | Elaboration of benefits and disadvantages for each actor |
| | 6 | Multidimensional view of the business models |
| | 7 | (Re)design of the business ecosystem |
| **Validation** | 8 | Looking at the collaboration between the actors |
| **Realization** | 9 | Formation of a team to implement the MVE |
| **Experiment** | 10 | Implementation of the final MVE |

**8 Golden Rules for an MVE:** combine design thinking and systems thinking · think transformation *and* evolution · find inspiration in biological ecosystems · think at all levels (customer, actor relationships, technology) · begin with benefits per actor and use value streams to show viability · accept the complexity of multiple business models and take the actors' perspective · start with the four loops · adapt the system if it doesn't work in its minimal version.

## Tells and smells

| Symptom | Diagnosis | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The customer appears as one actor among many on the map | Mindshift #1 not made | Return to lens #1; re-centre on customer needs (Ch 1, 10) |
| Ecosystem fields of action derived from competitor analysis | Mindshift #3 not made; linear strategy process | Start from "What does the customer want?"; use symbiosis framing (Ch 5) |
| Only your own business model has been designed | Mindshift #8 not made | Run the two-step multidimensional business modelling per actor (Ch 13) |
| Actors decline to join, or lose interest | No advantage or financial incentive | Complete the actor description template; check compatibility, motivation, market dominance (Ch 13) |
| Growth stalls at roughly 2x | EXPLOIT-only configuration | Add EXPLORE track, likely greenfield (Ch 7) |
| Every collaboration is called an "ecosystem" | Label inflation | Apply the three opening tests and the lens #2 transition question 1 (Ch 2, 12) |
| Governance modelled on partner management | Wrong mental model for orchestration | Rebuild as ecosystem governance; consider ecosystem unit → new venture path (Ch 8, 13) |
| Teams hurry and skip lens steps | Haste mistaken for velocity | Two steps backward; ask what would generate impact (Ch 8) |
| OKRs consistently 100% achieved | Objectives not ambitious enough | Reset to moonshot stretch; target 80% (Ch 8) |
| Initiative fails after a good design | Missing governance and scaling skills | Address before scale-up; 85% of failures are design, but implementation errors cluster at lens transitions (Ch 10, 13) |
| Similar ecosystem initiatives crowd one region/topic | No well-thought-out ecosystem design was applied | Run the design errors checklist (Ch 13) |
| Customers overwhelmed by channel choice | Multi-channel thinking | Move toward opti-channel: pick the best channel for them (Ch 14) |
| Cost programme slows growth | Positive costs cut alongside negative | Reclassify; preserve growth-enabling costs (Ch 14) |
| Retailers/actors migrate away from the platform | Tight orchestration, winner-takes-all, ownership demands on the customer interface | Loosen orchestration; share customer interfaces and data (Ch 16) |
| Design team cannot handle multidimensional models | Known capability gap the book names | Bring in expertise; do not skip — it is required for actor conversations (Ch 13) |

## The seven most common ecosystem design errors

1. Insufficient focus on new or changed customer needs
2. Weak core value proposition
3. Wrong configuration of the business ecosystem
4. Lack of governance structures and skills in orchestrating ecosystems
5. Wrong business model for the objectives of the ecosystem
6. Too little commitment in the multidimensional view of business models
7. Sluggish implementation and wrong leadership approaches in the scale-up phase

**Avoidance:** start with design thinking and a customer problem · realize initial MVPs and compelling value propositions · design through the loops up to an MVE · use governance that fits the system and adequate ecosystem leadership.

## Transition tables (what changes between lenses)

| | Final prototype → MVP | MVP → MVE | MVE → Implementation and growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Principle** | Test feasibility and desirability | Maximum learning, least effort | Maximum learning with the **smallest number of actors per role** → then leverage of network and lock-in effects for rapid, efficient growth |
| **Audience** | Stakeholders and decision makers | Initial (test) customers | Actors in the system + (test) customers |
| **Legacy** | Discarded after MVP requirements defined | First version of a marketable solution | First validation of a functioning ecosystem |
| **Risk** | Current risk low, future unknown | Moderate | Increased |
| **Feedback on** | Concept, idea, experience | Individual functions and experiences | Value streams, customer interfaces, data, algorithms |

**The five questions before leaving lens #2:**
1. Is a business ecosystem needed for the overriding value proposition, or does a normal partnership suffice?
2. What is the experience, function, or feature (MMF) to start the minimum viable variant?
3. What skills are needed by the other actors? What does your own company bring?
4. For which persona/target group is the first function and experience?
5. Have potential partners been involved? Should they be part of co-creation? Are there other actors who should join as early as the design phase?

## Governance path options (MVE onward)

`Ecosystem unit → Vertical/company branch → Subsidiary/affiliate → New venture → Participation of several actors in the new venture`

Chain: ecosystem strategy (right to play, digital requirements, design lenses) → operating model (governance, configuration, team of teams, backlog functions, orchestrating actors, skills and mindset) → result (performance KPIs, speed of growth, ecosystem evolution, expanded value proposition, growing ecosystem capital, opening up and transparency).

## Five success factors in dealing with business ecosystems

1. Ecosystem awareness — see yourself through other actors' eyes
2. Understanding systemic possibilities — imagine productive behaviours for yourself and the whole
3. Governance of ecosystems — work on and in the system, integrate partners, benefit all actors
4. Sustainable ecosystem intelligence — long-term systems and design thinking, agile advancement strategies
5. Leadership with business ecosystem design — integrate systems design into culture, break existing rules consciously (black ocean)
