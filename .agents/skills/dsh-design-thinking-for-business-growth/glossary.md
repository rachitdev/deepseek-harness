# Glossary — Design Thinking for Business Growth (Lewrick, 2022)

**Acceptance criteria** — Testable conditions attached to a user story, identified before implementation, with a clear pass/fail result and both functional and nonfunctional elements; normally verified by the product owner (Ch 12)

**Actor** — A role in a business ecosystem that has skills or offerings integrated into the value proposition, renders service or partial services, innovates within the principles and rules, and connects parts of its activities over the course of the project (Ch 3, 7, 13)

**Adaptability** — Solution maturity criterion of the ecosystem design lens: focusing on the needs of the actors and their ability to create a value proposition together (Ch 10, 13)

**Ansoff matrix (adapted)** — Portfolio tool for MVPs in potential ecosystems, with market penetration, product development, market development, and diversification; new markets × new offers is the potential black ocean quadrant (Ch 12)

**Black ocean strategy** — Configuring an ecosystem so that no other system or company can compete with the same or similar value proposition; ambition is no more than three perceptible competitors (Ch 1, 2, 6, 7)

**Blue ocean** — Creating new markets, avoiding competitors, tapping new demand (Ch 2, 7)

**Brand messaging pillars** — The stories told to the various actors in the system to win them over so they participate in the ecosystem and support the value proposition (Ch 13)

**Build – measure – learn** — Iterative lean start-up feedback loop applied until the product or service reaches desired market maturity; also adapted for MVE preparation (Ch 12, 13)

**Business ecosystem** — A system that goes beyond traditional customer-supplier relationships, including all actors who may have a direct or indirect impact on the strategy, sales, products, or capabilities, delivering a unique value proposition to a shared customer (Ch 3)

**Business ecosystem design** — The discipline situated at the interface between innovation, knowledge/information, and transaction/data ecosystems, using them as design building blocks (Ch 3, 13)

**Captivateability** — Solution maturity criterion of the scale lens: focus on increasing frequency of customer interaction, share of wallet, and retention/bond to the system (Ch 10, 14)

**Centralized business network** — Medium-complexity system of the platform economy, characterized by network effects, scaling, and APIs, usually resting on existing power relations and familiar business models (Ch 3)

**Choice cascade** — Lafley/Martin "playing to win" structure adapted here as winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, core capabilities, management systems, plus how to configure (Ch 7)

**Coevolution** — Steady change dynamics in an ecosystem, based on data, skills/capabilities, and other resources of the participating actors; mindshift #4 (Ch 1, 3, 14)

**Co-competition (coopetition)** — Collaboration that does not exclude competition among actors in specific areas; Hubject and Ant Financial are named cases (Ch 3, 16)

**Co-creation** — Joint exploitation of resources and mutual fertilization of ideas across actors; five forms: conduct research, open, integrative, collaborative, reflective (Ch 9, 13)

**Cooperation/industry matrix** — Inventory tool plotting existing partnerships, cooperations, and suppliers by focus/diversification and own industry/cross-industry (Ch 6, 13)

**Cooperatives** — Opening-up type: companies relying on collaboration with others from the onset to define a shared system (Ch 9)

**Core value proposition** — Concise explanation of the functional and emotional benefits offered by the ecosystem as a whole; "like a contract between the customer and all actors" (Ch 13)

**Critical items diagram** — Design thinking tool producing eight consolidated crucial elements (four experiences, four functions per member, one future-facing), which become MVP definition and later MVE backlog (Ch 4, 11)

**Customer** — The center of the roles grid: procures service, interacts on a recurring basis, shares behavior and transaction data, gets direct/indirect added value (Ch 3)

**Decentralized business ecosystem** — High-complexity system with an orchestrator, meeting changed customer needs, requiring new capabilities in design, orchestration, and governance (Ch 3)

**Design lens** — One of four framings — design thinking, lean start-up, ecosystem design, scale — each with key questions, a canvas, and a tool set; transitions between them are fluid (Ch 10)

**Design principles** — Team guidelines developed on a pyramid (more project-specific higher up), reduced by vote to a maximum of three per section and nine total (Ch 5)

**Design thinking canvas** — Lens #1 canvas covering research/trends, vision prototype, MVP requirements, problem statement, HMW question, critical items, final prototype, persona, exploration map, lean canvas (Ch 11)

**Desirability** — Solution maturity criterion of the design thinking and lean start-up lenses: focus on and validation of customers and their needs (Ch 10, 11)

**Discrete choice analysis** — Willingness-to-pay method based on actual purchase data or on asking customers for preference among alternatives with different attribute sets (Ch 12)

**Driver tree** — Innovation accounting device for early simulations of sales figures (Ch 12)

**Ecosystem capital** — The wealth of relationships between actors; monetized from the structures and connections to the respective actors; the fifth capital type in the book's configuration charts (Ch 1, 3, 13, 16)

**Ecosystem configuration grid** — Tool with dimensions as rows and manifestations as cells, used to define a specific ecosystem's configuration (Ch 7)

**Ecosystem design canvas** — Lens #3 canvas: customer needs, core value proposition, value streams, actors, design/redesign, prototype-test-improve, advantages/disadvantages per actor, multidimensional business models (Ch 13)

**Ecosystem journey** — The radically newly designed customer experience chain integrating the services of several companies; maps all connections and interactions in an ecosystem, not just company touch points (Ch 2, 13)

**Ecosystem leadership** — The networking of all actors involved in value creation; based on initiate and orchestrate rather than command and control (Ch 8)

**Ecosystem map** — Placement of actors in concentric circles by closeness to the core value proposition; two or three circles suffice for initial considerations (Ch 13)

**Ecosystem play and win configuration framework** — Three-panel framework defining market opportunity, capabilities/roles/governance, and procedure/tactics/strategy (Ch 7)

**Ecosystem strategy canvas** — The documentation canvas for design lens output, structured as where to play / how to configure / how to win, with an EXPLOIT–EXPLORE band (Ch 5)

**Emotional AI** — Systems that learn to recognize human emotions by analyzing eye movements, facial expressions, and tone of voice; named as a future opti-channel element (Ch 14, 16)

**Exploration map** — Tool documenting all experiments and prototypes on experience and function axes, recording expected vs actual outcomes (Ch 11)

**Exploratives** — Opening-up type: companies that experiment with various ecosystem approaches and seek active parts in other ecosystems (Ch 9)

**EXPLOIT** — Efficiency and growth; low uncertainty; steady returns; linear, projectable processes; management focused on organization and planning (Ch 2, 7)

**EXPLORE** — Exploration of new things and radicalness; high uncertainty; venture-style expectations; fast iterative procedure with fast learning cycles (Ch 2, 7)

**Extreme users** — Users who exceed the normal use limits of a product, service, or system; a source of insight into unaddressed market areas (Ch 11)

**Feasibility** — The criterion running through all four lenses, in four escalating forms: use of enabler technologies → testing/validating components → specifying components and interfaces → building out, professionalizing, and leveraging (Ch 10)

**Feedback capture grid** — Four-quadrant reflection tool: I like / I wish / questions / ideas (Ch 15)

**Final MVE** — The minimal implementation of a system that will be gradually expanded and scaled; proof that ecosystem design has yielded a viable, value-generating system (Ch 13)

**Final prototype** — The artifact concluding problem solving in lens #1; elegant and simple; contains critical items elements; discarded after MVP requirements are defined (Ch 11)

**Focused challenger** — Named strategic manifestation: an established company initiating a digital ecosystem to challenge the market and serve a particular segment with targeted products (Ch 7)

**Focused partner network** — Lowest-complexity system, based on value chains and SLAs; explicitly not a real business ecosystem (Ch 3)

**Greenfield approach** — Building an ecosystem on a new technology platform as a separate company or unit, far from traditional corporate structures and mindsets (Ch 7)

**Groan zone** — The transition from the divergent phase of problem exploration to the convergent phase of problem solution; the vision prototype helps a team cross it (Ch 11)

**Growth loop** — Trigger ↔ action ↔ new customer ↔ output; requires an external action familiarizing a potential customer with the value proposition (Ch 14)

**HMW question** — "How might we..." question derived from a point of view; a substantiation of the problem statement and the launch of the ideate phase (Ch 11)

**Hooked model** — Nir Eyal's trigger ↔ action ↔ reward ↔ investment loop, aimed at establishing a habit (Ch 14)

**Hub-and-spoke** — Customer relationship manifestation: individual service model with specific contacts for particular target groups, issues, or transaction types (Ch 7)

**Hybrid world** — Combination of physical and digital interaction; especially important where trust is a key element of the offer (Ch 14)

**Initiative/industry matrix** — Inventory tool plotting all company initiatives by isolated-to-networked and own-industry-to-cross-industry (Ch 6)

**Initiator** — Role that recognizes customer needs and market opportunity, starts initialization, provides budget for prototypes and MVP, and often switches to orchestrator or becomes an actor (Ch 3, 7, 13)

**Innovation accounting** — Objective measurement that the right conclusions are drawn from MVP experiments; uses acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, referral questions (Ch 12)

**Innovation ecosystem** — Ecosystem type developing new products, services, or processes through co-creation; often clustered (FinTech Singapore, IT security Tel Aviv, Crypto Valley, Silicon Valley); ambition is differentiation and technology leadership (Ch 3)

**Innovation team matrix** — Placement of innovation teams by technological versus market complexity, determining whether to move technology knowledge, market knowledge, or rotate the workforce (Ch 8)

**Interview for empathy** — Tool examining the problem from the user's point of view, using a question map and journey stages to understand the context in which a user acts (Ch 11)

**Intertidal zone** — Metaphor for where co-creation happens: joint exploitation of resources and mutual fertilization of ideas (Ch 3)

**Invincible companies** — Osterwalder's term for organizations that constantly reinvent themselves, balancing EXPLORE and EXPLOIT (Ch 2)

**Jobs to be done** — What the customer wants to perform; a field of the persona / user profile canvas (Ch 4, 11)

**Killer function / killer experience** — The MMF chosen for greatest customer benefit — the one that brings the ecosystem to life, engenders customer enthusiasm, and convinces potential actors (Ch 12)

**Knowledge and information ecosystem** — Ecosystem type exploiting decentralized knowledge and information resources; ambition is intellectual property and negotiation power (Ch 3)

**Lead users** — Users who act as innovators, satisfying requirements not yet known to the mass market (Ch 11)

**Lean canvas** — Maurya's canvas, preferred here over the business model canvas for the MVP transition because it is less inside-oriented; iterate fields I–V first (Ch 11)

**Lean start-up MVP canvas** — Lens #2 canvas: initial situation (persona, top 3 problems, customer journey and use cases), plan (vision and road map, top 3 features, MVP, build, costs and schedule), results (conclusion/next steps, learn, measure) (Ch 12)

**Life cycle approach** — Named strategic manifestation and portfolio option: services and products focused on customer needs across life phases, "cradle to grave" (Ch 7)

**Metcalfe's law** — V = n²; the growth law of many platforms (Ch 14)

**Mindshift** — One of ten paradigm shifts underpinning design thinking for business growth (Ch 1)

**Minimum marketable feature (MMF)** — The feature and part of a value proposition with greatest customer benefit, used to establish, test, and gradually improve the MVE (Ch 12, 13)

**Minimum viable ecosystem (MVE)** — Prototype of an entire system: an actor for each role so the system can be tested; achieves maximum learning with the smallest number of actors per role (Ch 4, 8, 12, 13)

**Minimum viable product (MVP)** — Tests individual experiences and functions with potential customers to make value tangible; more high-resolution than design thinking prototypes; also the persuasion artifact for potential actors (Ch 11, 12)

**Multidimensional view of the business models** — Designing not only the ecosystem's own model but how every actor makes money and what additional models each can stack; mindshift #8 (Ch 1, 2, 3, 13)

**Multi-channel** — Provision of different channels (e-mail, website, chat), driven by increasingly digital customer behavior (Ch 14)

**Mysterious (the)** — Opening-up type: companies keeping strategy at top management level and developing prototypes behind locked doors until implementation (Ch 9)

**North star** — The communicated target image, cascaded through target image, MVEs, MVPs, and prototypes with their respective fits (Ch 8, 15)

**Objectives and key results (OKRs)** — Short-cycle goal framework used across teams and ecosystem partners; max five objectives per quarter with four key results; 80% attainment is outstanding (Ch 8)

**Omni-channel** — Seamless integration of different channels, optimized through customer journeys (Ch 14)

**Opti-channel** — Provision of the best possible customer interaction as part of an ecosystem journey, with personalized interaction and chat/voice bots (Ch 2, 7, 14)

**Orchestrator** — Role coordinating activities, defining principles, rules, and framework conditions for growth, and realizing sustainable governance (Ch 3, 7, 13)

**Other players** — Role often receiving information or serving as an oracle for the validation of data in automated processes; usually no direct customer interface (Ch 3)

**Persona / user profile canvas** — Eight-area canvas: description, jobs to be done, use cases, pains, gains, sketch, influencers, trends (Ch 11)

**PESTLE** — Political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental analysis, adapted here with ecosystem-specific questions (Ch 6)

**Pivot** — A radically modified version of the entire MVP or part of it; nine named types including zoom-out, zoom-in, platform, channel, value capture, customer need, customer segment, business architecture, engine of growth, technology (Ch 12)

**Point-to-point** — Customer relationship manifestation: targeted personal interaction for specific issues by subject matter experts (Ch 7)

**Positive and negative costs** — Costs that facilitate future exponential growth (preserve) versus costs to be reduced by strategic cost-cutting (Ch 14)

**Problem 2 Growth and Scale framework** — Customer needs → problem/solution fit → product/market fit → system/actors fit → ecosystem/coevolution fit (Ch 10)

**Problem statement** — One-sentence formulation of the problem, in the form "How might we redesign (what) (for whom) so that (need) is satisfied?" (Ch 11)

**Progression curve** — Foresight tool grasping micro-, macro-, and megatrends at three levels: basic technologies, innovations accelerators, future scenarios (Ch 11)

**Red ocean** — Competition in the existing market; beating competitors; exploiting existing demand (Ch 2, 7)

**Reed's law** — V = 2ⁿ; the growth law of successful business ecosystems, where subgroups scale faster in value (Ch 14)

**Reflection canvas** — Canvas covering design lenses, digital fluency, ecosystem leadership, market opportunities, capabilities, mindset, lessons learned (initiative and meta), possibilities, principles, enabler technologies, big data/AI/ML/DL, capital and assets, governance (Ch 15)

**Retrospective sailboat** — Retrospective with island (goal/vision), wind (accelerating factors), anchor (inhibitory factors), cliffs (environmental factors beyond the team's power) (Ch 15)

**Rhythmicability** — Solution maturity criterion of the scale lens: exploitation of network effects and scale effects (Ch 10, 14)

**Role playing** — Tool in which team members take on actor roles (max 12 in the first phase) around a written value proposition, grouping by importance and relation (Ch 13)

**Sarnoff's law** — V = n; the growth law of centralized business networks (Ch 14)

**Seekers** — Opening-up type: companies with a clear idea where market opportunities lie, actively seeking actors to build ecosystems or contribute capabilities (Ch 9)

**Sesame score** — Credit ranking used by almost 70% of people in China to gain access to personalized financial products in Ant's ecosystem (Ch 16)

**Solution maturity** — Position in the design lens sequence, measured by the criteria that apply to each lens (Ch 10)

**Super app** — Ant Financial's self-description: an app subsuming a multitude of functions across merchant resources, financial services, life services, and social platforms (Ch 16)

**Supplier** — Role delivering technology, components, software, integration layers, infrastructure, and connectivity; has no customer relationship (Ch 3)

**Sweet spot** — The intersection holding potential for new business models, value propositions, and ecosystems (Ch 2)

**Team of teams** — Decentralized organizational design shifting from scalable efficiency to scalable adaptability, on five values including confidence, transparency, personal relationships, empowered execution, and a collective mindset (Ch 8)

**Topic areas map** — Visualization of ecosystems in a region or topic, including participating firms and who acts as initiator or orchestrator (Ch 6)

**Transaction and data ecosystem** — Ecosystem type built on technology platforms coordinating offerings, interfaces, and technologies, combining customer data for a synchronized 720° view; ambition is network effects and scaling (Ch 3)

**Trusted life cycle player** — Named strategic manifestation: greenfield, digital-first ecosystem built around a customer or service life cycle with neutral expertise (Ch 7)

**T-shaped** — Team member profile combining breadth of knowledge (horizontal) with specialist know-how (vertical) (Ch 8)

**Usability test** — User-centered validation of function, experience, or feature; recorded in a four-quadrant matrix of customer behavior and attitude, qualitative and quantitative (Ch 12)

**User story** — Role/persona + wish/goal + measurable benefit, in the form "As <role>, I would like to <goal> so that <benefit>" (Ch 12)

**Value enhanceability** — Solution maturity criterion of the ecosystem design lens: shaping sustainable value streams and benefits for all actors in the system (Ch 10, 13)

**Value proposition canvas** — Osterwalder's canvas used for the customer side of the core value proposition work (Ch 13)

**Value streams** — Flows between actors in the form of money, information, data, innovation/IP, or intangible assets; can be positive or negative (e.g. transfer of risk) (Ch 3, 13)

**Viability** — Solution maturity criterion of the design thinking and lean start-up lenses: creation and validation of an innovative, sustainable business model and the value of the offering (Ch 10, 11, 12)

**Virtuous design loop** — Steps 1–7 of the ecosystem design procedural model, from core value proposition through to (re)design of the ecosystem (Ch 13)

**Vision prototype** — First concept attempting to solve all identified needs and problems, with a distant time horizon; helps the team cross the groan zone (Ch 11)

**VUCA** — Volatility, uncertainty, complexity, ambiguity — answered respectively by vision/purpose, understanding/networks, clarity/ability and experiments, agility/transparency (Ch 8, 9)

**Willingness-to-pay (WTP) analysis** — Pricing validation via discrete choice or conjoint analysis, producing a market demand curve and price elasticity (Ch 12)

**720-degree view** — The holistic, cross-actor view of the customer that ecosystems assemble from data provided by individual actors (Ch 2, 3, 16)

**10x** — Shorthand for exponential growth; levers greater than 5, 8, and 10 of the current value (Ch 14)
