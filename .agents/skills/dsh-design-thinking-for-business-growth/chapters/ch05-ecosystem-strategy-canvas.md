# Chapter 5 — Ecosystem Strategy and the Strategy Canvas

*Pages 99–109 ("Ecosystem Strategy", "Strategic Options", "Strategy Documentation", "Ecosystem Strategy Canvas")*

## Core Idea

An ecosystem strategy cannot be produced by the classical strategy process. Corporate strategy over the last 70 years — from "plan and execute" through blue ocean and value migration — shares a **linear development** built on ambition plus comprehensive market and competitor analyses, from which business units derive detailed implementation plans. Applied to a business ecosystem strategy, this process quickly reaches its limits: speed, coevolution, and framework conditions are more dynamic, and experience shows these factors make traditional plans superfluous.

What replaces it: an experimental procedure in which the strategy process acts as an **enabler** — promoting co-creation, providing funds for experiments, and shaping the environment together with other actors. The **ecosystem strategy canvas** is the documentation instrument that captures what the four design lenses produce and lets the ecosystem be communicated and evaluated as a strategic option.

## Frameworks Introduced

**The crucial question.** Since every starting point differs, the main question should be:

> **"What does the customer want?"**
> - What are the deep-seated needs and wishes of the customers?
> - Which customer problem can be solved?
> - Which tasks does the customer want to complete?

**The three-part strategic design question set** — the canvas's column logic:

*Value proposition (spans the canvas):* What products, services, or experiences are offered? Who is the target customer? How do customers benefit? What makes the offer unique?

|---|---|---|
| Which customer problem is solved? Which topic area is covered by the ecosystem? | Which role is to be assumed in the business ecosystem? | What is the vision of the business ecosystem? |
| What reach (regional, national, international) does the target ecosystem have? | What capabilities are needed regarding IT, data, infrastructure, AI, and APIs? | What is the business model of the ecosystem? |

## Key Concepts

- **Awareness of ecosystems as a strategic option.** The decision-maker questions are: *What capabilities, products, and customer interfaces does the company have and how can they be used outside the known industry boundaries and structures?* In short: **"Who are we, and what role can the company assume in an ecosystem play?"**
- **Why it is easier now.** Cloud computing brings together interactive platforms and solves customer problems holistically; big data analytics, AI, and distributed ledger technologies allow end-to-end automation and mass customization; a collective capability for easy access to external resources has established itself; traditional industry boundaries are disappearing, fusing previously unrelated industries and offers.
- **Opportunities usually exceed risks** — but it takes investment, good governance structures, and a new mindset to succeed on such a growth path.
- **The ecosystem play competes with other strategic options.** Applying the Problem-to-Growth-and-Scale framework yields the content for the ecosystem play and win configuration framework; the goal is combining well-known strategy tools with iterative approaches. For pros in ecosystem design, it is often enough to work with the design lenses alone, since their goal is a superior system beyond familiar ideas of addressable markets.
- **Preliminary analysis has value but no primacy.** Actual analyses, roughly drawing up topics, and PESTLE do not replace the first design lens. Research is a *complementary* element allowing comprehensive understanding of the problem and validation of unclear assumptions.
- **The canvas is dynamic.** Its primary task is documenting ongoing activities arising from the design lenses. Each iteration in each lens brings new insights into customer needs, functions, business models, and value streams, so the canvas is fleshed out and developed evolutionarily. Even after realization, elements change — hence the emphasis on not losing sight of the larger target image.

## Mental Models

- **The strategy process as enabler, not as plan.** Its jobs are: promote co-creation, provide funds for experiments, shape the environment together with the other actors.
- **When to open up.** Depending on the problem statement, decide whether a first prototype should already be developed with potential partners, whether opening up is possible only upon defining MVP requirements, or whether other actors should be involved even later with the initial MVP. In actual company practice, only very select partners are involved in the first phases; **opening is mostly done after the MVP**, since higher maturity gives decision makers and design teams more certainty about the potential solution.
- **Vision prototypes seed strategy.** Initial high-resolution prototypes from iterative and agile approaches often form the basis of an ecosystem. The vision prototype aims at the imaginative power of the design team to realize a visionary version of a service, product, or business model *before* MVP requirements are defined.

## Anti-patterns

- Deriving ecosystem fields of action from competitor analyses. Success usually fails to materialize because customer needs were not taken into account, nor was a jointly supported value proposition worked out with other actors.
- Applying the traditional process-and-planning cycle to ecosystem strategy definition.
- Treating analysis alone as sufficient: "The pure analysis, without the iterative exploration of customer needs, tactics, and strategies, will not lead to success."
- Losing the larger target image of the ecosystem after realization begins.

## Worked Example

**Running the design principles tool** (pp. 108–109) — the chapter's concrete, filled-in procedure.

*What it is for.* Design principles give ecosystem design teams guidelines for a specific design in the form of clear statements. Especially where collaboration crosses company boundaries (co-creation), the principles should be developed together and accepted as a cross-company team.

*What they achieve.* The principles help the team to:
- put a clear focus on a specific mindset or the requirements for the ecosystem or topic area **as early as at the beginning of the project**;
- provide a uniform understanding of the task so everybody is on the same page;
- provide guidance so decisions can be made **faster**;
- define general characteristics that should be treated with higher priority;
- develop a guideline ensuring future ecosystem projects are created on the same overarching principles.

*The four-step procedure, with its thresholds:*

1. Invite all participants to write design principles on Post-its and put them in the "**basket**."
2. As soon as the basket is full, sort the principles onto a **pyramid**, dividing them into **three groups**. Sorting rule: **the higher up on the pyramid, the more project-specific the principle**; general design principles for business ecosystems sit at the **bottom** of the pyramid.
3. Once assignment is complete, conduct a vote — e.g. with glue dots. The aim is to reduce to a **maximum of three principles per section, i.e. a maximum of nine per pyramid**.
4. Put the selected and adopted principles in a place where the team is often confronted with them and to which it has quick access.

*Where it fits.* The book recommends preparing and establishing the design principles as a team **together with the project sponsors at an early stage** — before the teams start activities in the lenses (this point is repeated in the ecosystem leadership section, p. 146). Principles can be worked out for a specific initiative or defined in general terms for ecosystem projects.

## Key Takeaways

- Ecosystem strategy is documented, not planned: the strategy canvas is a living record of design lens output, not a front-loaded deliverable.
- "What does the customer want?" is the entry question for strategy work, not a design thinking nicety.
- Where to play / how to configure / how to win is the canvas's structure and the spine of Chapters 6 and 7.
- The EXPLOIT/EXPLORE band forces every strategic statement to declare whether it leverages the known or explores the unknown.
- Design principles: max three per pyramid section, max nine total, agreed cross-company, before lens work starts.
