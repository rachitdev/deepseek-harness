# Chapter 16 — Examples Worldwide and Outlook

*Pages 297–327 ("Examples of Design Thinking for Business Growth", "A Final Word")*

## Core Idea

Thirteen ecosystem and growth initiatives, presented with a common structure: a description, a "factors of success" list, and a capital-configuration chart using a fixed five-part key — **tangible capital · financial capital · human capital · intellectual capital · ecosystem capital**. The set spans global orchestrators (Tencent, Alibaba, Amazon), transformed incumbents (Ping An, DBS), and national or regional initiatives (Klara, Cardossier, Green Class, Hubject, YOUR NOW).

The framing claim: "By 2030, more than 1/3 of global sales will be generated in ecosystems. Experts assume that this evolution of business ecosystems will go so far that **more than 100 different value chains will be consolidated** in them."

## Frameworks Introduced

**The regional snapshot, 2020:**

| Region | Position |
|---|---|
| **Europe** | Among the top 50 companies in Europe, "there are only a few initiatives up to now that have realized a 100% digital business model, platform business model, or business ecosystem." Globally, Europe covers a mere **5% of the market capitalization** in currently dominant models of the platform economy |
| **United States** | Among the top 50 companies, many initiatives with hybrid or 100% digital business models. "Apple, Google, and Amazon take advantage of the opportunity for exponential growth. Market shares of established companies are declining" |
| **Asia** | Many initiatives with hybrid or, increasingly, 100% digital business models. "China has currently nearly **100 digital platforms** with corresponding business ecosystems, with Alibaba leading the field" |

Baidu, Alibaba, and Tencent together "already accounted for about **30% of China's GDP** [in 2020], which is more than **3 trillion euros**, and this figure is rising."

**Retail ecosystem mindsets — a 2×2 on orchestration and complexity:**

| | Loose orchestration | Tight orchestration |
|---|---|---|
| **High complexity** | Fosters fragmented competition | Leads to a winner-takes-all mentality |
| **Low complexity** | Creates a turbulent environment | Encourages collaboration |

Evolution of mindsets and value propositions in retail:

| Walmart | Amazon | Shopify | Open Bazaar | Future? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Product-centered** | **Digital product ecosystem** | **Prosumer ecosystem** | **Cooperative ecosystem** | ? |
| Competing through efficiency | Leverage the ecosystem for exponential growth | Empowering customers and actors to "connect, share, and own" | Fairness and transparency with customer involvement | Well-being instead of possessions? Helping everyone to prosper? Sustainable products and lifestyles? |

The book's observation: "Several million retailers have now 'moved' from Amazon to Shopify, since the winner-takes-all mentality with rigid rules of a centralized platform and the ownership and data demands of the customer interface are no longer appropriate for the times to many retailers." Shopify COO Harley Finkelstein is quoted: *"The future of commerce needs to be owned by all of us — partners, merchants, service providers, tech enablers and shoppers. The masses, not the few."*

## Key Concepts

- **Open ecosystem strategy** (Ant Financial). "Ant's business ecosystem strategy purposefully relies on co-competition. The open system is made available to other financial institutions... This opening allows Ant to realize entirely new value streams that increasingly generate more revenue than traditional financial services." Ant "became independent of conventional commissions and transactions and banks on higher margins in technology delivery."
- **Recurring interaction as design goal** (Tencent, Ping An). Tencent's customer needs "consist mainly of recurring interactions, e.g. chatting (= constant interaction), ordering a taxi (= daily), coordinating doctor's appointments (= monthly)."
- **Stakes to secure data.** "In well-considered strategic decisions, Tencent takes a financial stake in other actors in the system, especially when these actors generate important data about customers that will help in the future to create a 720 degree view of each and every customer or user."
- **Becoming invisible** (DBS). "While DBS has developed customer journeys and improved existing touch points in the past, today the bank is focused on becoming invisible to the customer. The bank disappears in the consciousness, but the services from its unique value proposition bind the bank with the customer many times over."
- **Coopetition as explicit strategy** (Hubject). "Hubject deliberately pushes 'coopetition' for the mobility market across four industries: automotive, technology, energy suppliers, and telecommunications providers."
- **Culture as a growth tool, not a rulebook** (WeWork). "The ecosystem orchestrator and initiator does not treat The We Company corporate culture as a list of rules set in stone. Rather, the culture is used as a tool to help the ecosystem grow, change, and evolve as needed."

## Anti-patterns

- Europe's position is presented as the cautionary case: a mere 5% of platform-economy market capitalization and few top-50 companies with digital or ecosystem business models.
- Winner-takes-all orchestration: the book links Amazon's tight orchestration to the retailer migration to Shopify.
- Thinking only in customer/supplier relationships: "In many industries, decision makers still think in terms of customer/supplier relationships and venture at most a first small step toward participation in an industry marketplace or similar initiatives."

**Tencent / WeChat — figures and success factors.**
WeChat: over **1.2 billion active users**; over **one million mini-programs** with **400 million active users every day** (as of 2020); governance of **1.5 million third-party software developers** and **60,000 service providers**. Beyond the original chat function, WeChat offers payment services (WeChat Pay), online-to-offline (O2O) services (meitun.com), and integrated online shopping (JD.com).

*Tencent factors of success:* a simple mission as compass for decisions — "Improving people's quality of life through digital offers"; founder-managed company; diversified portfolio and constant expansion of the value proposition; innovation according to six principles — **agility, openness, customer first, speed, resilience, and evolution**; active shaping of business ecosystems.

*COVID-19 digression — the QR code economy.* More than **100 mini-programs** related to COVID-19 were integrated. "Tencent health codes" were downloaded by **1 billion people** and applied more than **9 billion times** during the first wave in China (January to May 2020), with over **26 billion users** visiting the corresponding mini-programs. Over the same period, more than **10 billion yuan** in WeChat salary vouchers were announced or distributed by local governments. Health-related mini-programs saw user numbers rise by **347%**; more than **1,000 hospitals** processed **130,000 health inquiries** via WeChat Work functions. "WeChat Work multi-person conferencing tool" was used **220 million times** to work from home January–May 2020; **one-fifth of the schools in China** used WeChat Work to reach a total of **50 million parents and their children**. The QR code "creates a digital connection between people, things, and places"; the networked system's basis is the official WeChat user account, mini-programs, WeChat Pay, and WeChat Work.

**Alibaba — the two transitions.** Mobile penetration of goods purchasing was below **15% in 2010**; today **more than 90%** of goods are purchased via mobile phones. The second big transition came in **2014**: Tencent launched WeChat's "Red Envelope"; Alibaba responded with Alipay in time for Chinese New Year. Both allow sending small amounts of money as red Chinese envelopes (*hongbao*). "This killer function radically changed the way customers use the option of the virtual transfer of small amounts of money."

*Alibaba factors of success:* unique business model; unconventional profit model; reliable evaluation model of the actors; support services for greater customer satisfaction; pronounced sensitivity to new market opportunities; new transaction models (e.g. C2B and O2O); holistic business ecosystem concept.

**Payment black oceans.** In mobile payment, China leads all other regions, with around **47% of smartphone owners using a digital wallet**. "WeChat and Ant dominate with their black ocean strategy." Plans exist in China to connect the QR code payment ecosystem to a **universal code**. AI now also allows payment by face scanner: in early 2020 there were over **110 million Chinese users** paying this way in supermarkets, grocery stores, shopping centers, and restaurants.

**Ping An factors of success:** focus on a business ecosystem that goes beyond the sale of insurance policies; focus on customer needs and customer experience chains; use of existing and new data for product and service innovations; investment in artificial intelligence and process automation; active roles as ecosystem initiator and orchestrator with a focus on monetizing data and new services.
Named sub-offerings developed with partners: **"Ping An Good Doctor," "Autohome," "Ping Haofang."** Up to **30 million customers** visit the Autohome platform for the purchase and sale of cars — simultaneously potential customers for financing and insurance products.

**DBS Bank.** Value proposition: **"LIVE MORE, BANK LESS."** The **"DBS Co-Creation Program"** comprises **65 partners** from sectors including finance, health care, retail, and automotive. "The respective ecosystem design leaders at DBS come from a wide range of industries; many from technology companies that have already asserted themselves in an ecosystem play. **Traditional bankers and industry experts are not to be found among them.**"

*DBS "Transformation Wheel":* (1) automate everything; (2) develop high-performing teams; (3) organize for success; (4) shift from "project" to "platform"; (5) design of cutting-edge systems.

*Five core capabilities of the digital business model:*

| Capability | Content | Aim |
|---|---|---|
| **Acquire** | Customer acquisition through expanded distribution, moving away from physical branches and account managers to digital marketing channels and social media | Increased customer acquisition; lower acquisition costs |
| **Transact** | Eliminate paper and physical forms in favour of e-forms, e-statements, digital account opening, one-click order processing, end-to-end automations | Eliminate paper, realize instant processing; reduce costs |
| **Engage** | Data-driven interaction — customized reports, statements, needs-based customer engagement | Increase lock-in based on needs; realize cross-selling through contextual marketing; increase revenue per customer |
| **Ecosystems** | Initialization and participation in ecosystems to deliver comprehensive services; rapid connectivity with various APIs and responses to changing customer needs | "Pipes to platforms" |
| **Data** | — | "Be insights driven" |

*New prioritization of KPIs at DBS:* the traditional 50% (shareholders, customers, employees) plus 50% strategy (geography, regulation, society, enablers) is restructured into **50% traditional KPIs + 20% "making banking joyful" + 30% strategy**. The "making banking joyful" block covers acceleration of digitization and happy customers and employees through "customer experiences"; the strategy block covers value capture through digitization. "The KPIs represent the core of digital transformation and account for **20% of the total scorecard value**." Piyush Gupta, CEO: *"The winner over the next 10 to 12 years will be people who have been able to build nimbleness, flexibility, adaptability, and responsiveness into their way of working."* And on scale: "DBS operates like a start-up. The difference, however, is that it has **over 26,000 employees**."

**Amazon.** Digital journey began in **1995** by digitizing existing processes (selling books online), then digitizing content (e-books, Kindle format). Its evolution reads: *digitization of processes → digitization of contents → multidimensional view of the business models → expansion of the value proposition*. Approximately **60% of Amazon's revenue comes from the intermediary business**. Amazon's **40-plus subsidiaries** include **PillPack**, which ships individually labeled packs of medicine (labeled with date and time to take), launching "**PillPack by Amazon Pharmacy**," a new business ecosystem. Amazon Prime customers "generally spend twice as much on other Amazon offers as regular customers."

*Amazon factors of success:* innovation and value proposition design as two key elements in developing new value propositions; strong anchor effects through Amazon Prime (solidifies loyalty to online shopping); leverage of proven business models such as subscription, marketplace, pyramid model, or on-demand; gradual evolution from digitization of physical processes to a holistic ecosystem.

*Amazon's ecosystem areas* (43 subsidiaries): innovation + start-up enablement; health care + retail; books + publications; virtual community + content; entertainment; e-sports; IoT and AI devices; voice; infrastructure (IoT + software); food logistics; digital payments + finance; digital advertising; digital education.

**YOUR NOW (Daimler + BMW).** The joint venture orchestrates **five pillars**: **REACH NOW** (multimodal), **CHARGE NOW** (charging), **FREE NOW** (ride hailing), **PARK NOW** (parking), **SHARE NOW** (car sharing). Current customer base around **60 million active users**; the five companies want to scale globally from key regions of Europe and America.
*Factors of success:* exploit strategy based on existing products to expand market space by using start-up companies and initializing an ecosystem; value proposition based on the basic need simply to go from A to B; design of new end-to-end offerings within the ecosystem; step-by-step expansion into new areas and portfolio elements; two strong, established, prestigious brands as a branding element.
Harald Krüger, chairman of the Board of Management of BMW AG: *"We want to connect more means of transport, reach even more people in additional cities and thus improve the quality of life in metropolitan areas."*

**Hubject.** A business and transaction ecosystem with over **760 actors in 43 countries**, building a nationwide retail network for electric vehicles since **2012**; originally launched as a joint initiative of **Bosch, EnBW, Siemens, RWE, Daimler, and BMW**. Core value proposition of the intercharge network: "*Accelerating the emobility ecosystem through business services and a customer-focused approach*" — for charging station operators, traction power providers, energy suppliers, fleet operators, car sharing companies, service card providers, and car manufacturers. In **2020**, over **250,000 charging points** on four continents were connected to the open platform. Digital interaction via QR codes, RFID cards, NFC, or plug-charge solutions; billing similar to roaming on the mobile network.
*Factors of success:* participation in a new growth market of e-mobility; core value proposition with multiple added values for actors and end customers; simplification and automation of processes and payment procedures; fast scaling by way of simple interfaces and powerful IT; open system with conscious decision for "coopetition"; strong growth of ecosystem capital through network effects.

**Green Class.** Launched **2016** by Swiss Railways (SBB) with BMW, "initially as a design thinking project that designed a minimum viable ecosystem in short sprints in **less than six months** in the co-creation mode." The offer: a public transport subscription plus an electric vehicle of your choice, with all customer services, road tax stickers, tire changes, taxes, and insurance included; additional services bookable (parking, charging on the road, car sharing, bike sharing, cab services). Target segment: customers in urban agglomerations or the countryside commuting mainly to big cities, multimodal, with a more pronounced desire for sustainable solutions; "in most cases, customers do more than five stretches of road a day."
*Factors of success:* personalized mobility concepts; any route and means of transport you like; strong focus on the trend of sustainability; answers to changing customer needs; application of new enabler technologies for interface-free integration of different mobility solutions.

**Klara.** A Swiss business ecosystem taking over tasks from SMEs since **2016**, part of the AXON Group; orchestrates banks, insurance companies, and trustees, with over **100 specialists** in payroll, fiduciary services, marketing, and IT development. Value proposition: "*more than just taking the administrative burden off people's backs — Klara makes your office easy*." Focus on micro-enterprises (KLARA Business) and increasingly private households (Klara Home). "Klara uses design thinking, data ideation, lean start-up, and business and data ecosystem design for the further development of the value proposition."
*Factors of success:* design of offers according to customer needs; easy onboarding with the ability to remove inhibitions for micro-businesses through coaching and setup services; evolutionary expansion of the offer and use of new technologies; end-to-end processes and digital interfaces to key partners of small businesses in Switzerland; application of different business models from freemium to subscription and pay-per-use.

**Cardossier.** Launched as an **association in Switzerland in 2019**; the association is initiator and orchestrator, developing a blockchain-based solution to map the life cycle of vehicles digitally. Members include **AdNovum, the University of Zurich, the Lucerne School of Computer Science**, and industry partners **AMAG and AMAG Leasing, AXA Insurance, Mobility, Audatex, auto-i-dat AG, AutoScout24, PostFinance, and the Swiss Leasing Association**; the Swiss Federal Roads Office FEDRO and cantonal road traffic offices are also part of the ecosystem. **Eleven million data sets** were provided by auto-i-dat AG. Value proposition: "Bringing transparency to the ecosystem around vehicles and establishing trust for all trading partners."
*Factors of success:* digitization of physical assets using blockchain technology; increasing efficiency in an ecosystem with many actors and little transparency; database for new products (on-demand insurance, pay as you drive, parametric insurance, peer-to-peer insurance); more transparency and data security for private customers; automated processes (facilitated claims management, contracting, document exchange, forgery protection); ecosystem with seamless customer experience based on trustworthy technology.
*Why blockchain:* "With blockchain technology, you can perform trustworthy transactions without intermediaries. Distributed ledger technology (DLT)... ensures that transactions are registered in many places and, once verified, are virtually impossible to change." The complete life cycle of a vehicle — kilometers driven, workshop visits, vehicle drivers, even location data — can be recorded. "The ecosystem provides trust."

## Worked Example

**Ant Financial** — the chapter's deepest single case (pp. 304–306).

*Why it repays study.* "Analyzing the sub-business ecosystem Ant is especially rewarding since data play a decisive role in this system. The business ecosystem has **petabytes of data** at its disposal, which was provided by customers and users for the most part **free of charge**. Other platforms such as Google or Facebook are well stocked with user data but they don't get near the dimensions Ant is aiming for. **No other tech giant has the same reach and depth of data from mobile payments and the associated offline services as Ant Financial.**"

*Why the data is valuable.* "It provides information on user behavior all the way up to so-called **live events (marriage, children, inheritance, etc.)** with the greatest potential for the sale of products." From this, Ant designs, structures, and offers tailor-made financial services from mortgages to asset management and insurance policies — realizing "the ideas of comprehensive-financing service providers envisioned in the 1990s."

*The timeline (the book's own milestone table):*

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 2004 | **Alipay founded.** At the time, China had no more than 30 million credit card users |
| 2007 | **>50 million payment users** |
| 2011 | **Payment license** — Alipay receives a payment license in China and introduces payment by bar code to develop the offline market |
| 2013 | **Yu'e Bao founded** — new offerings from Alipay in the area of wealth management and launch of the Wave Payment technology |
| 2014 | **Ant Financial founded** — Alipay is renamed Ant Financial; founding of the financial information platform Zhao Cai Bao |
| 2016 | **Series B funding** — €4.5 billion (Series A financing in 2015). The Mybank app begins providing financial services to SMEs and individuals |
| 2018 | **$14 billion, $150 billion valuation** — the largest individual financing by a privately owned company, valued at €150 billion. Launch of AlipayHK and GCash for cross-border money transfers |
| 2020 | **IPO** — preparations for the IPO of Ant Financial |

*Beyond distribution of financing offers.* "Using AI, new premium models for policyholders are developed, for instance; at the same time, claim assessments are done in a flash with the aid of intelligent image recognition and structured loss data." Traditional passwords have been replaced for the most part by **biometric data** — identification on the basis of face, retina, and fingerprint scans. "In this way, **Ant Insurance has won over 400 million policyholders** in the life, non-life, and other insurance products."

*The open ecosystem strategy in detail.* Ant purposefully relies on **co-competition** — the open system is made available to other financial institutions, enabling other financial actors to use the system design and technology to address new customer needs. This opening realizes entirely new value streams that increasingly generate more revenue than traditional financial services, putting **technology services for banks and insurance companies at the center of its growth strategy**. As of 2020, Ant had a capital market valuation of **more than €150 billion**. Alipay and related organizations have around **1.2 billion active users, 900 million of which are living in China**.
Example of the actor value proposition: wealth management companies can offer pension funds on **Ant Fortune**; as actors, they also access data-based analyses of target customers and can publish AI-based services such as automated e-brochures for their segments. "Ant is a prime example of the fantastic potential of open business ecosystems, in which fintech is both initiator and actor in the ecosystem and the platform provided."

*The "super app" and its data mechanics.* Ant sees itself as a super app subsuming a multitude of functions, spanning **one million merchant resources, financial services, life services, and social platforms**. Data enables suppliers to access desired customer groups on the Ant marketing platform; promotions via coupons; quick, simple, cost-effective payment options; and learning about customers and behavior. Push notifications are sent through **geolocation** as soon as customers are in a region, city, or store. For tourists shopping abroad, the app has an **automatic tax refund function**. Special shopping experiences can be shared via social sharing.
Credit mechanics: "As soon as the transaction data from Alibaba's Wallet is available, Ant can offer suitable financial products that match the customers' risk profiles. These products help to check creditworthiness in real time and to provide small loans at the POS, even offering money market funds. At this point, **almost 70% of the people in China use the 'sesame score' as a credit ranking** to gain access to personalized financial products in return."

*Globalization.* "In the longer term, Ant focuses on building out its presence globally for scaling. The aim is that customers will have the opportunity in the future to shop and transfer funds throughout the world." Joseph Tsai, CEO, Ant: *"If you have a network of partners all sitting on the same technology stack, interoperability is not a problem. In the future, somebody using the Philippine version of Alipay can come to Hong Kong and shop at any store that accepts Alipay. That's the vision."*

*The book's verdict.* "Ant Financial is a good example of how a business ecosystem can grow exponentially with cutting-edge technology and ecosystem leadership. External factors such as COVID-19 caused customer needs to change once more... So it comes as no surprise that other ideas for expanding the value proposition have been moving toward **personalized medicine** since 2020."

## Outlook — what the closing section adds

- **Where to start.** "Those who want to build an ecosystem with design thinking for business growth should first ask themselves **in which areas of the customer's life they want to satisfy needs** and, in the second step, **how other actors can help to cover this value proposition** in the best possible way."
- **The book's stated main aim.** "To replace the horizontal and vertical structures that are entrenched in the cognitive repertoire of many business leaders and managers with a new way of thinking about business models, ecosystems, and growth."
- **Ecosystem capital again.** "In the world of digital ecosystems, companies can grow quickly through a clever combination of data, software, and appropriate ecosystem configurations. The connections between the actors not only generate new intellectual capital but, above all, **ecosystem capital!** Hybrid models of physical and digital interaction currently appear particularly attractive. The best examples are the ecosystem orchestrators Amazon and Alibaba."
- **Trends named as accelerants.** Generation Z (share of the population >25%), 100% of whom already own smartphones and take digital interaction for granted; new technologies allowing analysis and sophisticated influence of behavior; **emotional AI**, where systems learn to recognize human emotions by analyzing eye movements, facial expressions, and tone of voice; **human augmentation** with emulated virtual reality — e.g. "an avatar acts as a digital advisor that can simultaneously maintain eye contact with thousands of customers while adjusting its tone of voice and choice of words based on data about each customer's preferences"; and new market spaces in **work-life integration**, where boundaries between designing work, leisure, and lifelong learning blur.
- **Three prerequisites, restated.** Opening to the outside; transparency about skills and values; willingness to change and acceptance of new market roles. Plus: include the innovation ecosystem and the data, information, and knowledge ecosystems in the design of a business ecosystem.
- **Closing words** by **Prof. Larry Leifer** (Stanford University; founding director, Stanford Center for Design Research; founding director, Hasso Plattner Design Thinking Research Program at Stanford): business ecosystem design "is a hot research topic in my LAB at the Stanford Center for Design Research (CDR)... we are currently gathering the evidence on what has worked, what has not worked, and what the root causes are for both." On the book: it "explicitly address[es] WHERE, WHEN, HOW, and WHY some ecosystems thrive and others simply wither over time," and "it will take a few years until this mindset has arrived in all universities, companies, and with the respective decision makers so the fruits can be harvested."

## Key Takeaways

- Thirteen cases, one comparison frame: description, factors of success, and a capital configuration reading tangible → financial → human → intellectual → **ecosystem** capital.
- Ant Financial is the deepest case: petabytes of freely-given data, an open co-competition strategy, and technology delivery replacing commissions as the margin source.
- Regional asymmetry is stark on the book's 2020 numbers: Europe at ~5% of platform-economy market capitalization; Baidu/Alibaba/Tencent at ~30% of China's GDP.
- Small and national initiatives work: Green Class designed an MVE in under six months of sprints; Cardossier and Klara are national-scale ecosystems with named member lists.
- The retail comparison (Walmart → Amazon → Shopify → Open Bazaar) is the book's argument that tight orchestration invites migration.
- The outlook's practical instruction: start from which *area of the customer's life* you want to serve, then ask which actors are needed.
