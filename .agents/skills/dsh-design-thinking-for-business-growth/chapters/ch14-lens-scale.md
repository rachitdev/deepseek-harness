# Chapter 14 — Lens 4: Scale

*Pages 270–286 ("Lens #4: Scale")*

## Core Idea

"To think exponentially means ensuring growth (e.g. **doubling of the customer base every six months**). However, it also includes deliberations about how one's own business model could be disrupted to attain greater leverage from existing components, capabilities, and network connections."

Scaling is the book's hardest phase — "the scaling of the business ecosystem is one of the greatest challenges." Its distinctive demand is a change of *magnitude* in the questions asked: "Most business models are linear and designed to boost profit or to reduce costs, e.g. by 15% within one year. To scale successfully, you must have the courage to think exponentially, i.e. to deal with **levers greater than 5, 8, and 10** of the current value. The term '**10x**' is often used for exponential growth."

After the MVE is implemented, ecosystems need different amounts of time to scale — months or many years — and dependencies may exist that the ecosystem can affect only to a limited extent but that are necessary for scaling.

**The three network laws:**

| Law | Formula | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Sarnoff's law** | V = n | Centralized business networks — value grows proportionally to network size |
| **Metcalfe's law** | V = n² | Many platforms — value proportional to the square of network size |
| **Reed's law** | V = 2ⁿ | Successful business ecosystems — able to grow in proportion to network size, but groups form that scale quicker in value than others, on the basis of different interrelationships among them |

**Growth loop + hook model.** The procedural model consists firstly of the "**hooked model**" established by Nir Eyal:

`Trigger ↔ Action ↔ Reward ↔ Investment`

| Step | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | An internal or external nudge that prompts the user to act |
| **Action** | The customer performs the desired action in terms of the product, service, or experience |
| **Reward** | The customer need is satisfied; at the same time the desire to engage repeatedly is increased |
| **Investment** | Consists of two parts — anticipation of future rewards, and reasons for the customer to continue to engage with offers from the ecosystem |

This is mirrored in the **growth loop**, with trigger and actions similar in both, then:

`New customer ↔ Output`

| Step | Definition |
|---|---|
| **New customer** | Customer engages with the output and makes a decision to try out the product, service, or experience |
| **Output** | External result that is perceived outside the ecosystem and current customer basis |

The distinction: "With regard to actions, the growth loop requires an **external** action that familiarizes a potential customer/user with the value proposition; by contrast, the hook model aims at establishing a **habit**."

**Multi-channel → omni-channel → opti-channel evolution:**

| Multi-channel | Omni-channel | Opti-channel |
|---|---|---|
| Provision of different channels (e-mail, website, chat) | Seamless integration of different channels | Provision of the best possible customer interaction as part of an **ecosystem journey** |
| Driven by the Internet and increasingly digital customer behavior | Optimization of the channels through customer journeys | Use of chat and voice bots; personalized interaction with the customer; future use of **emotional AI** |

"Companies that have already collected data through omni-channel activities can individualize their customer interaction with new technologies (AI, big data analytics, machine learning) and offer an opti-channel that meets the needs and preferences of the customers." And: "Offer the customers the **best** channel for their needs instead of giving them a choice via multi-channel, which would overwhelm them."

## Key Concepts

- **Four types of need that scalable offerings cover:** functional, emotional, life-changing, and those with a greater social impact. "The more elements a value proposition contains, the greater customer loyalty and the greater the exponential growth of an ecosystem."
- **Solve the problem of many — the four-part procedure:** (1) **Commitment** — which needs ensure the product, service, function, or experience is used on a recurring/daily basis? (2) **Community** — how is added value generated for the community, and how does the customer community generate added value for the ecosystem? (3) **Data** — what data about customers is available, and how can data for the algorithm and automated processes that create value be collected? (4) **Implementation and promotion** — is the offer so simple that children and seniors understand it?
- **Leverage, innovate, expand.** The orchestrator's control pattern for value proposition expansion. Its procedure: (1) leverage first proposition; (2) innovate by exploring; (3) expand proposition. When observing new customer behaviors or needs that seem recurring, "they must be tested quickly and iteratively for an expanded offering so they become a part of the value proposition." At this point the cycle goes back to the first phase of design thinking, lean start-up, and (re)design of the ecosystem.
- **Freedom at the limits of the rules.** "The job of the orchestrators is to provide the necessary freedom so that actors can become active even at the limits of the rules."
- **Positive vs negative costs.** "Cost management should absolutely not be allowed to encumber the growth of a business ecosystem. It is important for the growth of a business ecosystem initiative that '**positive**' costs be preserved, i.e. costs that facilitate future exponential growth, while '**negative**' costs must be reduced by strategic cost-cutting measures."
- **From network effects to coevolution.** The named shift for the scale lens. A transformation from a centralized digital platform toward an ecosystem supports the chances for exponential growth. "Coevolution as a steady change dynamics in the business ecosystem is based on data and skills/capabilities as well as other resources of the participating actors."
- **Governance as playbook.** "Governance consists more of a playbook for exponential growth than of rigid rules. The individual management tasks are carried out in a multifunctional way." Responsibilities are clearly defined, "but roles and job names are constantly changing."
- **Digital maturity precedes exponential growth.** "Before exponential growth becomes possible, digital maturity must be achieved, which allows interacting as a business ecosystem in a hybrid and digital world."

## Mental Models

- **The heartbeat.** Doubling the rate of delivery of new products, services, and experiences to many customers — while keeping the focus on minimum viability. (First stated as design principle 5 in Ch 1.)
- **Trust needs hybrid.** "The combination of physical and digital interaction is especially important for offers in which trust is a key element."
- **Data as lubricant.** "In many ecosystems, data is the lubricant that allows for the development of data-driven new products, services, and functions and experiences."
- **Ecosystem culture requires autonomy.** "An exponential growth path is possible if the employees and teams are given the autonomy to do what they have to in the open culture that a business ecosystem requires so as to make quick and targeted decisions."

## Anti-patterns

- Applying linear business model targets (e.g. +15% in a year) to an ecosystem play.
- Letting cost management encumber growth by cutting "positive" costs.
- Overwhelming customers with channel choice instead of selecting the best channel for their needs.
- Scaling without governance and skills — named in Ch 10 as why "many ecosystem initiatives lack the governance and skills needed for the fast scaling of a solution."
- Red tape blocking team problem-solving: teams "must be able to solve problems on their own and avoid red tape."

**Ecosystem culture based on team of teams** — four elements:
- **Flexible organizational structures** with clear roles and responsibilities.
- **New formats for meetings and a collaboration** that is geared to acting instead of overanalyzing everything.
- **Greater autonomy for teams and individual employees** — employees must be able to solve problems on their own and avoid red tape.
- **Unique decision-making process** that continuously advances the growing ecosystem and the organizational structure.

Collaboration platforms named as supporting spontaneous collaboration and network emergence in physical, hybrid, and virtual spaces: Teams, Zoom, Mural.

**Scalable processes, IT, data analytics — the trade-off pairs.** The tool is presented as a set of tensions to balance while scaling:

| Left (growth-facing) | Right (efficiency-facing) |
|---|---|
| Customer experience | Process automation |
| Updated offer | Cost to serve |
| New offers | Efficiency |
| New revenue streams | Data quality |
| Digital culture | Regulatory compliance |
| Future skills | Process upgrading |

The supporting capability stack: digital capabilities and culture of innovation · focus on future technologies · digital leadership · collaboration and partnering · technology components · technology as differentiator.

What the focus on process automation, IT, and data analytics helps the team do: understand digital technologies and evaluate their impact on the ecosystem; build the capability of using these technologies and configure them to help scale IT, processes, and infrastructure; establish clearly defined governance and processes for involving other companies, start-ups, and technology firms; introduce comprehensible performance measurement and decisions for selecting partners and technology components.

**Leverage of touch points — the four-step procedure:**
1. Gather information from the physical world to create a digital record of physical interaction and of the value chain where it makes sense.
2. Offer the customers the best channel for their needs instead of giving them a choice via multi-channel.
3. Machines communicate with one another to exchange information, enabling advanced analysis, visualization, and use of real-time data from various sources for customization and data-driven innovation.
4. Application of algorithms and automation to enable decisions and actions related to interactions with customers in the digital and physical world.

The hybrid world model: **physical world** (gradual digitization of processes and interactions) ↔ **digital world** (analyze and use) → **hybrid world** (e.g. use of opti-channel strategies for top-notch customer experience) → generate actions.

**Coevolution view — the three-step procedure** (tool 7):
1. Identification of new market opportunity, customer problems, or inefficiencies.
2. Transformation and key issues: **Coevolution** — What is developing? Who makes a contribution to it? **Mechanism** — How does it develop? **Strategy/governance** — How should the evolution be orchestrated?
3. Realization and step-by-step implementation with the inclusion of integrated resources. Continuous exchange of data, information, and guiding principles for the realization of innovation and new ways of accessing markets and customers.

What the coevolution view achieves: not having to build required resources and skills yourself but developing them incrementally in the ecosystem with the right actors; distributing the burden of capital-intensive projects and asset purchases across more shoulders; establishing a general change of behavior — searching for possibilities of realizing the value proposition in a business ecosystem instead of trying to build all capabilities yourself.

**Transition from linear growth to exponential growth** — standard procedure vs advanced practices:

| Dimension | Standard procedure (linear) | Advanced practices in scaling ecosystems (exponential) |
|---|---|---|
| **Digital as a strategy** | Appreciation of technology as an enabler for implementing an ecosystem strategy; development of expertise that triggers differentiation and competitive advantage | The orchestrator points out clear strategic goals and aspirations, followed by initiatives, to become a digital ecosystem; possesses the skills to develop and implement digital initiatives in coordination with other actors |
| **Focus on future technologies** | Analysis of existing and emerging technologies for use in the ecosystem; ability to test new technologies early and check for innovation, cost reduction, and growth potential | Ability to understand digital technologies and evaluate their impact on the ecosystem; capability of using these technologies and configuring them to help scale IT, processes, and infrastructure |
| **Digital leadership** | Transversal embedding of technological expertise beyond core processes; clear assignment of responsibilities for technology decisions to an ecosystem team | Formation of cross-function squads and chapters for technology and innovation; special innovation labs for research and use of digital technologies in close collaboration with other actors |
| **Digital capabilities and culture of innovation** | Focus on building employee skills in the digital area; gathering innovative ideas within the organization and through open innovation | Close collaboration with selected start-up ecosystems; hackathons for problem statements in data analytics, AI, or other technologies; motivating environment for employees and stakeholders to tackle new challenges and use enabler technologies intelligently |
| **Collaboration and partnering** | Awareness that new players operate disruptively as a start-up or business ecosystem | Clearly defined governance and processes for the involvement of other companies, start-ups, and technology firms |
| **Technology components** | Focus on collaboration with well-known technology providers to extend with digitization layers and specific APIs as interfaces in marketplaces | Comprehensible performance measurement and decisions for the selection of partners and technology components |
| **Technology as a differentiator** | Use of technology to set oneself apart from competitors; building first trustworthy alliances | Ability to master technologies and benchmark them; differentiation geared to outstanding innovations and customer experiences |

**Expanding value streams — options named:** expansion toward value streams arising from a separate big data ecosystem; aggregating data and making it available anonymously on marketplaces for data; mass customization; providing third-party data for a fee (in compliance with applicable laws and regulations). "Actors who are financially involved in the business ecosystem often have privileged access to the data, while other actors have limited access or need to purchase access rights. Successful initiators of business ecosystems also acquire actors or else they have a majority stake in companies to get access to important data from customers and transactions."

## Worked Example

**Amazon and Waze — the chapter's two scaling cases, plus the housing ecosystem expansion.**

**Amazon: automation, then a new business from the infrastructure.**
"Technology helps to analyze routine activities/offerings in a business ecosystem on a continuous basis and to automate them so as to disrupt the traditional value chains (= continuous redesign of business ecosystems). Amazon, for instance, has been using robots for quite a while to stock and take out products and is getting closer to have its goods commercially delivered by drones. Likewise, Amazon recognized early on that a strategic option to offer cloud computing services to business customers might become a key source of earnings. **Amazon Web Services (AWS)**, which offers access to computing infrastructure, was launched in **2006**. Today, the service contributes **60% to Amazon's growth**."

The mechanism the book is illustrating: an internal scaling capability (infrastructure built for the ecosystem's own needs) becomes an *expanded value stream* sold to a new customer class — exactly the "can technology components, algorithms, or the entire infrastructure be used for additional B2C and B2B services?" feasibility question.

**Waze: user-generated content plus external network effects.**
"Content generated by customers and boosted by external network effects helps scaling. Successful business ecosystems use this strategy as a motor for customer acquisition. The **'Waze' traffic app**, for instance, connects **GPS data with real-time traffic input from customers**; **rudimentary gamification** is used to make the process more entertaining and interesting. In addition, information is provided about physical offers at **gas stations, restaurants, and DIY/hardware stores along the route**. In North America, Waze is one of the most popular apps for commuters."

The mechanism: the hybrid touch point tool in action — physical world information (gas stations, restaurants, stores) digitally recorded and delivered into the digital journey, with customer contribution as the growth loop's *output* that recruits the next customer.

**The "Renting, Buying, Housing" ecosystem: expansion of the value proposition.**
The chapter's illustrated example of *leverage, innovate, expand* uses the same housing ecosystem introduced as an MVE in Ch 13. Its expansion legend distinguishes three moves:

| Move | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Expansion into new topic areas** | — |
| **Expansion with more actors who have the same skills/roles/offers** | e.g. an additional real estate portal or bank |
| **New actors and roles in the system** | — |
| **Identification of further customer needs** for existing, supplementary, and new value propositions | e.g. contracts, moving, rating |

The rule the example encodes: "When expanding the value proposition, it is important to ensure that the relevant actors can make their contribution to the innovation of the business ecosystem. They must be given the opportunity to adapt their role to the dynamic changes as well."

**Matternet and Mercedes-Benz: leverage of different actors.**
"Many exponential business models are based on an unusual mix of different actors in the business ecosystem. In most cases, different types of companies from different industries collaborate so as to realize a value proposition together and benefit from the integrated value. The drone company **Matternet** and **Mercedes-Benz** are good examples of this. These enterprises have bundled their forces to design an integrated delivery solution in an ecosystem concept that is intended to change and make easier the way in which goods are procured and delivered."

**The cost discipline that must hold across all of it.** From the canvas's bottom band: "How can the cost of user acquisition be kept lower than that of the lifetime value generated?" — CAC < LTV as the standing constraint on every growth mechanism above.

**Note on data-driven models and COVID-19.** "Data-driven business models combine insights from traditional and digital services and offers from the business ecosystem. The proportion of such business models is steadily on the rise. In 2020, the increase was quite noticeable. Driven by COVID-19 and lockdown measures, virtual meeting rooms were increasingly used and augmented by further services. There has also been an increase in digital twins for further optimization or to reduce costs in research and development."

## Key Takeaways

- Exponential thinking means levers of 5, 8, 10x — not percentage improvements; a concrete benchmark given is doubling the customer base every six months.
- Reed's law (V = 2ⁿ) is the ecosystem's growth law; Metcalfe's (n²) is the platform's; Sarnoff's (n) is the centralized network's.
- Hook model builds habit; growth loop recruits externally — both run on the same trigger and action.
- Opti-channel, not omni-channel, is the ecosystem's channel target: choose the best channel for the customer rather than offering all of them.
- Preserve "positive" costs that enable future growth; cut only "negative" costs.
- The four need types (functional, emotional, life-changing, greater social impact) predict loyalty and growth by how many the proposition covers.
- Amazon's AWS (2006 → 60% of growth) is the book's clearest case of infrastructure becoming an expanded value stream.
