# Report 1 — The ROI of AI in Customer Experience

*Google Cloud + National Research Group. Subtitle: "How AI agents are helping boost engagement and delight customers." ~34 pages. Source file: `google_cloud_roi_of_ai_customer-experience.pdf`.*

## Core Idea

Generative AI in customer experience has crossed from "does it work?" to "how do we scale it?" The report's central claim is that a self-identified cohort called **agentic AI early adopters** — organizations allocating **at least 50% of their future AI budget to AI agents** and already running agents across operations — outperforms the general population on every ROI measure the survey tracks. The prescription is therefore not "adopt AI" but "adopt agents, fund them as a dedicated line item, sponsor them at C-level, and instrument the returns."

The headline number: **88% of agentic AI early adopters are seeing positive ROI on at least one gen AI use case** (n=460), versus **74% across all organizations** (n=3,466).

## Survey Methodology

Precision matters here because the report footnotes every figure against a different base.

- **Fielded by:** Google Cloud and National Research Group (NRG), a commissioned research partner.
- **Fieldwork window:** April 18 – June 3, 2025.
- **Total sample:** 3,466 senior leaders of global enterprises.
- **Firmographic screens:** over 100 full-time employees; over $10M annual revenue.
- **Seniority quotas:** 940 CEO/CIO; 1,097 CFO/CMO/CTO; 768 CISO/CDO/CSO/COO/Director of Digital Strategy/VP of IT; 661 IT Director/Head of Innovation/Director of Customer Experience or Service/Marketing Director.
- **Priority industries:** media and entertainment, retail and CPG, financial services, manufacturing, healthcare and life sciences, telecommunications, public sector.
- **Country sample:** US 1,047; Brazil 351; Mexico 350; Canada 200; France 179; Nordics 177; UK 175; Germany 175; Australia 118; South Korea 118; India 117; Japan 117; Indonesia 116; Taiwan 116; plus a MEA aggregate (UAE 40, Turkey 35, Saudi Arabia 18, South Africa 7, Qatar 7, Kuwait 3).
- **Year-over-year comparisons** are against the 2024 wave, where total global n=1,539. Any "vs. 2024" figure in this report is comparing a 1,539-base to a 3,466-base.

## Frameworks Introduced

### 1. The survey's definition of an AI agent
"AI agents are specialized LLMs that have specific roles, context, and objectives to independently plan, reason, and perform tasks with access to data function call APIs and can interact with other AI agents if needed. These can be pre-built or in-house built agents." Every adoption percentage in the report is measured against respondents holding *this* definition — not a looser "chatbot" definition.

### 2. Levels of AI agent maturity
| Level | Name | Examples given |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Simple tasks | Chatbots, information retrieval, image generation |
| Level 2 | AI agent applications | Customer service AI agents, creative agents |
| Level 3 | Multi-agent workflows | Agentic workflows, agent orchestration |

### 3. The agentic AI early adopter definition
Two conjoined criteria: **(a)** at least 50% of future AI budget allocated to AI agents, and **(b)** agents already deeply embedded across operations. This is a budget-and-deployment definition, not an attitudinal one — which is why it correlates with outcomes.

### 4. Five proven areas where AI is delivering ROI in CX
1. Faster financial returns
2. Happier customers
3. More productive human agents
4. Improved CX beyond the contact center
5. Data-driven intelligence

### 5. The "human plus machine" super agent strategy
The report's named deployment pattern: AI-augmented "super agents" that give pre-purchase guidance, empower reps post-purchase, and "transform the contact center from a cost center into a high-value, problem-solving hub." Agents absorb repetitive tasks; humans keep empathy and critical-thinking work; junior staff ramp faster via real-time information, sentiment analysis, and personalized recommendations.

### 6. The ROI AI agent checklist (the report's closing prescription)
- Find your executive champions (cultivate C-suite sponsorship to advocate, clear roadblocks, align to results).
- Demonstrate value to secure AI budget — build the business case for AI as its own investment.
- Start with the biggest wins: agents that automate repeatable tasks with clear ROI.
- Build trust in AI from day one: first get the data house in order with data governance and enterprise security; second, always keep a human in the loop.
- Give agents the tools to be useful: secure, governed access to internal enterprise systems such as CRM or Drive.
- Create your AI rulebook now, not later — enterprise-wide guidelines to secure data, protect IP, ensure compliance.
- Invest in talent and internal AI education. "The most successful companies don't just buy technology, they build skills."

## Key Concepts

**Adoption is mainstream, not experimental.** 63% of executives report their organization is using gen AI for CX and field service functions (n=3,466). 52% of executives whose organizations use gen AI have adopted AI agents in production. 54% report using gen AI for digital commerce and enhanced experiences across mobile, kiosk, and web (n=3,466).

**Agents are simultaneously internal and external.** 52% of executives whose organizations use gen AI for CX and field service report those use cases are *both* internal and customer-facing (n=2,190). 49% of executives at organizations with agents in production report they are used for customer service and experience (n=1,814).

**Agentic search is the second front.** Beyond support, the report positions natural-language agentic search as the transformation of digital discovery: intent comprehension replacing keyword matching, guiding customers "from initial inspiration to completed purchase."

**Engagement is the biggest measured CX effect.** Of executives reporting improved CX (n=2,177), 83% cite increased user engagement — engagement score, traffic, click-through rate, time on site. 75% of that same base report improved user satisfaction or NPS.

**Improvement is real but modest in magnitude.** Among executives who report improved CX and were asked to size it (n=2,133), 51% put the improvement at 6–10% — down slightly from 53% in 2024 (n=912). The improvement is broad, not dramatic.

**Spending is rising and shifting from other budgets.** Mean AI share of total annual IT spend is 26% overall (n=3,315) versus 39% for early adopters (n=459). 77% report gen AI spend has increased as technology costs fall (question not fielded in 2024). 58% are allocating net new budget without reducing other budgets (61% in 2024); 48% are reallocating non-AI budget (44% in 2024) — i.e., net new funding is down and cannibalization is up year over year.

**Sponsorship is the strongest correlate of ROI.** 78% of executives whose organization has comprehensive C-level sponsorship and a clear corporate vision report ROI now on at least one gen AI use case (n=1,269). Strong alignment between gen AI adoption and C-suite sponsorship grew from 69% (2024) to 73% (2025), where "strong" means a 4 or 5 on the question "How is gen AI adoption connected to your organization's business goals?", with 5 defined as "Comprehensive C-level sponsorship and clear corporate vision for gen AI objectives."

**The blockers are foundational, not model-related.** Top three factors when considering LLM providers: data privacy and security 37%, integration with existing systems 28%, cost 27% (n=3,466). Over 1 in 3 name data privacy and security. The report frames the fix as a modern integrated data strategy with governance and security from the start.

## Mental Models

- **Budget allocation as the leading indicator.** The early adopter cohort is defined by where money is committed, not by stated enthusiasm. If you want to predict which organizations will report ROI next year, look at the percentage of future AI budget earmarked for agents.
- **Cost center to problem-solving hub.** The contact center is the report's recurring metaphor: automation of routine work is only the entry price; the value is re-pointing the freed human capacity at high-value interactions.
- **From catalog to concierge.** Websites and mobile apps are reframed from "simple catalogs" into "intelligent concierges" — search bar to cart as a guided journey rather than a query-and-filter exercise.
- **ROI is not only financial.** Trimble's Eric Lambert: "Leaders need to first decide what ROI means. It goes beyond financial returns. We have to ask if it's making people more efficient and building towards business objectives."
- **Every industry adopts for its own reason.** The same technology, different priority: financial services optimizes for secure personalized interactions; retail and CPG for inquiry volume and shopping journeys.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating AI as a line item inside an existing IT budget.** Early adopters carve out dedicated budget; the report ties funding structure directly to consistent ROI.
- **Buying technology without building skills.** Named explicitly in the checklist. Upskilling ranks third among investment priorities (40%).
- **Deploying agents before governance.** The checklist orders it: data governance and enterprise security *first*, human-in-the-loop *second*, then tooling. Deutsche Bank's Christoph Rabenseifner: "While everyone believes in their value, deploying AI agents while covering enterprise security, compliance, and other requirements is still tremendously difficult."
- **Starving agents of tool access.** "For an AI agent to do the work, it needs access to your internal enterprise systems, like your CRM or Drive." An agent without governed system access cannot produce the measured outcomes.
- **Spreading effort across equally-weighted pilots.** "Not all AI projects are created equal. Focus your energy on building AI agents that can automate repeatable tasks to deliver clear ROI."
- **Writing the AI rulebook after scaling.** "Create your AI rulebook now, not later."

## Worked Example

The report's four named, numbered customer outcomes — reproduce these verbatim when citing evidence.

**Toolstation (UK tools and building materials supplier), Vertex AI Search for Commerce.** Deployed to understand and serve trade and DIY customers. Result: **a 5.5% increase in search-based revenue and a 10% lift in click-through rates.** Rob Contreras, Head of Innovation: "Collaborating with Google Cloud has been about more than just implementing new technology; it's also been strategic to fundamentally understand customer intent. Vertex AI Search for Commerce allows us to deliver a truly intelligent experience that not only drives significant commercial results but also builds deeper trust and loyalty with our customers."

**Commerzbank, Customer Engagement Suite.** Built "Bene," a specialized chatbot. Result: **handles over 2 million chats and successfully resolves 70% of all inquiries.** Oliver Dörler, Chief Data and AI Officer: "At Commerzbank we prioritize AI use cases that promise the greatest ROI, enabling us to unlock significant cost savings."

**Mercari (Japan's largest online marketplace), contact center overhaul with Google AI.** Building an "AI-driven CS" experience. Result: **projected 500% ROI by reducing customer service representative workload by at least 20%.** Note this is a projection, not a realized figure — the report says "is projected to yield."

**Mr. Cooper (largest US mortgage supervisor), Google AI inside its AgentIQ tool.** Result: **average agent handle time improved by 3.53% across 500,000 monthly calls, unlocking 28,000 hours annually.** Shikha Chadha, Senior Principal, Product Management, AI Products: "By improving average agent handle time by 3.53% across 500,000 monthly calls, we unlocked 28,000 hours annually, boosting efficiency at scale."

**The arithmetic worth noticing.** Mr. Cooper is the one case where the numbers can be checked against each other. 3.53% of the annual handle-time base yields 28,000 hours, implying roughly 793,000 baseline agent hours per year across 6 million annual calls — about 7.9 minutes average handle time per call. The figure is internally consistent, which is why it is the strongest single data point in the report: a small percentage improvement applied to large volume, not a large percentage on a small pilot. This is the shape of the ROI argument the whole report is making.

**Third-party corroboration cited.** Forrester's Total Economic Impact study of Google Cloud Customer Engagement Suite with Google AI (2025): **207% ROI over three years with a payback period of under six months.** Forrester's TEI studies are commissioned by the vendor whose product they evaluate; treat this as vendor-sponsored analyst research, not independent benchmarking.

## Key Takeaways

1. Quote the base, not just the percentage. Nearly every headline number in this report has a different denominator, and several are filtered to respondents who already reported success.
2. 88% vs. 74% ROI is the report's core comparison, and the early adopter cohort is defined by budget commitment (>=50% of future AI budget to agents) plus deep deployment.
3. Agent deployment depth is the sharpest differentiator: 82% of early adopters run more than 10 agents in production versus 39% overall.
4. The measured CX gain is broad but modest — 51% of those reporting improvement size it at 6–10%.
5. Engagement metrics (83%) move before satisfaction/NPS (75%); pick engagement as the leading indicator.
6. Funding is shifting from net-new budget (58%, down from 61%) toward reallocation of non-AI budget (48%, up from 44%).
7. C-level sponsorship correlates with ROI (78% vs. 72%), and the correlation is stable across both survey waves.
8. Data privacy and security (37%) outranks cost (27%) as an LLM provider selection criterion — a governance-first market.
9. Industry variation is real but bounded: 42% to 57% across seven industries for the same CX agent use case.
10. Named case studies cluster on operational efficiency (Mr. Cooper, Mercari, Commerzbank) rather than revenue, with Toolstation the main revenue-side proof point.
