## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The two nested loops.** The **micro cycle** is Understand → Observe → Define point of view → Ideate → Prototype → Test → Reflect, run many times. The **macro cycle** is the seven-step arc of a whole project: initial brainstorming (20 min) → critical functionalities (1–2 h, 10–20 functions) → benchmarks from other industries (90 min) → dark horse (50 min) → funky prototype (1 h) → **vision prototype** (~2 h, tested with at least three users) → concretization (functional/MVP → finished → final → implementation plan). *Process awareness* means knowing which loop you are in and which mindset applies right now.

**"WHY? expands, HOW? narrows."** The most portable move in the book. Asking why repeatedly widens the creative framework until you reach wicked problems; asking how scales it down to a buildable mechanism. Use it to place the design challenge at the rung that leaves the comfort zone but still admits a prototype. If the scope still feels wrong, **start anyway** — iteration one corrects it.

**The groan zone.** The transition from divergence to convergence, and the single hardest judgment call in the discipline. Divergence is infinite and cheap; the skill is the timed exit. Two devices make the exit objective: fix the **critical functionalities early**, then match the mass of ideas against them; and **outsource the vote** to people outside the group, who have not fallen in love with the idea.

**Three problem types, three processes.** *Well-defined* (one solution), *ill-defined* (many solutions — the normal case in design thinking), *wicked* (the problem is the search for the problem; solution understanding changes problem understanding — co-evolution). Linear analysis fails on the third; the countermeasures are the "How might we…?" question and the why technique.

**Needs are verbs.** State needs as WHAT the user wants to achieve, keeping solution-oriented HOW out. Surface your assumptions, plot them on *incidental ↔ decisive* × *knowing ↔ ignorant*, and test the decisive/ignorant ones first. Skipping needfinding skips three steps at once, because Understand, Observe and synthesis cannot be cleanly separated.

**The persona is the starting point, not the goal.** Design thinking begins with a persona who has a need — never with a target. Thin personas produce Prince Charles when you meant Ozzy Osbourne, so dig one level deeper and **validate the sketch against reality**. Jobs-to-be-done ("When ___, I want to ___, so I can ___") replaces attribute-tinkering; the milkshake is hired for two opposite jobs, so it needs two opposite specs.

**Prototype = disprovable assumption.** LOVE IT — CHANGE IT — LEAVE IT. *Never fall in love with your prototype.* Build it only good enough to make the basic features comprehensible, always on the basis of **what is to be tested**, and drive maturity only as far as the goal requires. Decisions rest on the intersection of **human desirability, economic feasibility, technical implementability** — always starting from the human being.

**Testing is learning, not selling.** Do not explain the prototype early; do not ask suggestive questions; answer questions with questions. Test several variants so feedback can be comparative, in the real context, with neutral people, in pairs (moderator / actor / observer). Run **many qualitative tests with no more than five people each**, and pilot with one person before the series. Fail fast, fail often.

**Interdisciplinary ≠ multidisciplinary.** On a multidisciplinary team each expert advocates their specialty and the solution is a compromise. On an interdisciplinary team everybody owns the whole. Compose from **T-shaped** people (depth plus empathy and interface expertise) developing toward **Pi-shaped**, on a **U-shaped** team that returns to equilibrium after disruption — because safety and security mean productivity.

**Facilitation owns process, never content.** The facilitator stays neutral in substance, is resource-oriented rather than deficit-oriented, and holds the group through emergence. Build the room with **ARE IN** — Authority, Resources, Expertise, Information, Need. Test of success: *if everything remains the same afterward, the facilitation has failed*.

**Method without organizational fitness is a racing bike with a beer belly.** Design thinking is only as effective as the organization's capacity to implement the result evenly. Silos plus process improvement make the problem worse; measure customer centricity across a representative cross-section, develop measures *with* employees, and close the loop (measure → options → implement → measure).

**Combine mindsets deliberately.** *Systems thinking* (white box, solution space, gradual refinement, feedback loops rather than cause/effect chains) and *design thinking* (black box, problem space, many fast iterations) are both double diamonds and complement each other on wicked problems. *Strategic foresight* adds the long horizon — shape the future rather than extrapolate it (WYFIWYG: "what you foresee is what you get"). *Lean start-up* carries it forward: customer/problem fit → problem/solution fit → product/market fit, with risk reduced experiment by experiment.

## Scope & Limits
- Covers the 2018 Wiley edition. This skill contains **19 of the book's 25 sections**: Part 1 (1.1–1.10, the micro cycle), Part 2 (2.1–2.7, the mindset and the organization), and the first two sections of Part 3 (3.1 systems thinking, 3.2 lean business model thinking).
- **Not covered here**: the remaining Part 3 sections on business ecosystem design, bringing it home / implementation and stakeholder mapping, design criteria in the digital paradigm, kick-starting digital transformation, AI and personalized customer experience, and design thinking with data analytics. Chapters occasionally reference those topics by book chapter number; those cross-links point to the nearest covered chapter instead.
- The companion volume *Design Thinking Toolbox* holds the step-by-step mechanics of many tools named here; chapters flag it as **dsh-design-thinking-toolbox**. Tool mechanics are summarized, not reproduced in full.
- Technology references date the material: blockchain, chatbots, the Gartner Hype Cycle, Facebook's ten-year road map and the named startups reflect 2017–2018. The method content is not time-sensitive; the trend lists and company examples are.
- The book is deliberately non-prescriptive — "design thinking is not a structured process." Treat every procedure here as an indication, not a recipe: *love it, change it, or leave it.*
