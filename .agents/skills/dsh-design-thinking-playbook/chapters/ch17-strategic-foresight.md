# Chapter 17 (Book §2.7) — Why Strategic Foresight Becomes a Key Capability

## Core Idea
"Peter, Priya, Lilly, Jonny, Linda, and Marc have one thing in common. **They are all on
Facebook.**" The chapter opens on Facebook as the exemplar of long-horizon thinking:
"Facebook has grown into the largest social network in less than a decade… The great success
of Facebook is based on **long-term thinking, in particular on a strategy planning with a
perspective that goes beyond five years**" — the **10-year road map**. "As soon as the
strategy process is completed, Zuckerberg begins to **break down the strategy into small and
implementable sub-projects (missions)** for his teams."

Zuckerberg, quoted: **"We go mission-first, then focus on the pieces we need and go deep on
them, and be committed to them."**

**Strategic foresight** is defined as "an approach that **focuses on shaping the desired
future**. Strategic foresight consists of **a mindset and a methodology in tune with it**.
The mindset is characterized by **a belief in the future, which we can shape ourselves by
hunting for new market opportunities.** The methodology includes various tools and
techniques that lend a hand in steering the teams systematically and purposefully in the
right direction."

## Frameworks Introduced

**The Strategic Foresight Framework** — "developed at the **Stanford Center for Design
Research** and has evolved into **four schools** represented by Drucker, Schwartz, Jouvenel,
and Arnold. Of course, the Framework is **embedded in design thinking**." (See Reference
Tables.)

**The Foresight Framework — five stages.** "The Foresight Framework has such a simple
structure that **anybody can go through the five stages from beginning to end**. The first
three phases are dedicated to the question of how we best deal with a new or hitherto
unknown problem statement. We begin with **the famous white sheet of paper** and try to
capture the future. The problem statement can be directed inward or outward. **In extreme
cases, it can even mean that we redefine the future of an entire enterprise.**"

| Stage | The book's one-line brief |
|---|---|
| **1. PERSPECTIVE** | "Look into the mirror of the past to be able to shape the future actively." |
| **2. OPPORTUNITY** | "Identify current growth opportunities and extrapolate them by understanding changed customer needs of the future." |
| **3. SOLUTION** | "Find the right way to discover innovations that are specific to your industry, customers, organizational form, and team skills." |
| **4. TEAM** | "Identify leadership and develop talents that are needed to transform ideas into an innovation." |
| **5. VISION** | "Develop a clear vision, so the teams will be able to survive on their own. Provide assistance with clear, forward-looking actions." |

"**Every phase has used three tools** that are very useful when processing the respective
questions."

**WYFIWYG** — "A few years ago, the wisdom of **WYFIWYG** took hold at Stanford: **'What you
foresee is what you get.'** Thus we found out that **expectations have an impact on what
will happen in the future. What we foresee and intuit also influences the result!**"
(Modelled on WYSIWYG, "popular in the late 1960s… It means we accept as given what we see
before us.")

**The double diamond with a vision in the center.** "When we superimpose selected tools and
methods onto the phases of **research, conceptual design, and implementation**, we get a
'double diamond' with **a vision in the center**." (See Reference Tables for the tool bands.)

**The strategic foresight toolbox** — progression curves, Janus cones, change paths, white
spot analysis, buddy checks, crowd clovers, generational arcs, real theater, vision
statement, pathfinder (see Reference Tables). The instruction: **"Build your own toolbox!"**

## Key Concepts
- **The foresight mindset.** "A mindset consists of **established attitudes, values, and
  opinions**, which are currently brought to day-to-day work… With a 'foresight mindset,' we
  are convinced **we have the future in our own hands**. This future is translated step by
  step into reality with targeted activities. In a company, a foresight mindset can form the
  basis for **launching a new business area or developing an innovative product**."
- **Not futurology.** "This approach is very different from that of **futurologists**.
  Futurologists usually claim that they are able to map the future through scenarios and
  trend analyses. **They base their forecasts on past data or current trends, which they
  extrapolate into the future.** The model for strategic foresight discussed here… **combines
  a long-term perspective with the well-known tools of strategic planning and design
  thinking.** This combination enables teams to tackle **short-term fields of action that are
  aligned to medium-term and long-term market opportunities.**"
- **Mood affects capability.** "Positive moods influence the team. By creating a positive
  attitude toward future market opportunities, we are better prepared for realizing them:
  *We can do better! · We are more effective! · We have a different approach to the solution
  of a problem!*"
- **The three rumors about radical innovation** the book debunks:
  - "Success is due to **the genius of the company founder and serial entrepreneur** (Apple
    & Tesla)"
  - "**Unlimited resources** are the decisive factor (Google & Facebook)"
  - "It's just **pure luck** (Twitter & Snapchat)"
- **The real ingredient.** "**Ultimately, a clear vision is the only ingredient that
  determines success.** What spoils success are **prevailing mindsets and management
  paradigms based on fear.** Fear is easy to stir up, such as with negative financial
  forecasts about missed targets, cost savings, and the announcements of staff layoffs.
  **The good news is: There are other ways!**"
- **"Disruption dread."** "The Foresight Framework was developed to protect teams from a
  'disruption dread': **the growing fear that others might overtake you.** The distinguishing
  feature of the Foresight Framework is **a positive vision** of the future, the products,
  the services, and the vision of a company."
- **The Playbook for Strategic Foresight and Innovation.** Tools documented from 50 years of
  Stanford / Silicon Valley practice, "available free of charge to all innovation fans at
  **www.innovation.io**." Companies that "have established a new mindset in their
  organizations with the aid of these tools": **Deutsche Bank, Volvo Construction Equipment,
  Samsung Electronics**, and other global businesses.
- **Why tools, specifically.** "Studies conducted at Stanford have proven that people find
  such tools helpful for **arranging and structuring their thoughts, especially when it comes
  to knowledge-intensive and complex issues.** Strategic foresight is based on **abstract
  ideas with a high level of uncertainty** in the early stages of development. The tools help
  in the analysis of **what is already known and what is not.** The future can also be made
  **palpable and graspable** this way."
- **The trend surface.** The chapter's own trend map places **business intelligence, global
  futures and strategic foresight** around a core of **trends** — *technology, environmental,
  demographics, economy* — at *international / global* scale.

## Mental Models
- **Understand the past to shape the future.** "In the initial phase ('Perspective'), the
  focus is on **understanding the past**. This reflection helps us to comprehend what has
  happened up to the present day. Once a team has understood the past, **it is easier to
  understand possible options for the future.** The question of **why** these options are
  rated high especially can yield the decisive insight when they are implemented later.
  **Often project teams immediately address the solution.**"
- **Unfulfilled needs point at the ready-to-change customers.** "When we recognize
  unfulfilled customer needs, we have already come closer to **customer groups who are most
  ready for changes**, and we automatically address the question of **how these customers
  might benefit from an innovation.**"
- **Value is comparative.** "The value of the solution option that was developed **can be
  better assessed when compared to other solution options and workarounds.**"
- **Vision is what buys stakeholder lifeblood.** "**Only with a clear vision** will the
  various stakeholders support the idea, **invest their lifeblood in it**, and ultimately
  contribute to **scaling** the idea."
- **The last two phases are the slow ones.** "The latter two phases help to enshrine
  strategic foresight throughout an organization. **This process takes time because
  organizations do not learn overnight.**"
- **Foresight covers design thinking's blind spot in time.** "Strategic foresight **embeds
  design thinking in an infinite product and service continuum**, which comes to life
  iteratively in our future-proof concepts. Thus it helps to develop **a long-term
  perspective and robust ideas**… Through **early detection**, strategic foresight helps to
  deal with **the high speed of change on the outside and the frequently prevailing internal
  inertia of the organization.**"
- **Micro trend → macro trend → mega trend.** The chapter's own worked progression (see
  Worked Example).

## Anti-patterns
- Jumping straight to the solution and skipping the Perspective phase.
- Extrapolating past data and calling it foresight.
- Management paradigms based on fear — negative financial forecasts, cost savings, layoff
  announcements — as a motivator.
- The "secret recipe" search: "Countless books have been written on the gloomy future of
  companies that don't succeed in deciphering the secret recipe."
- The excuse: "**Often companies have no clear idea as to where the journey should lead.
  Their excuse is that other companies don't have a clear strategy and vision either and
  that it is still too early to formulate a clear digitization strategy anyway.**"
- Waiting for a weak signal to become undeniable before acting on it.

## Worked Example — Combining foresight and design thinking to shape a vision of future mobility

The chapter's own application example, reproduced with its causal chain intact.

**The behavioural premise.** "The **feeling of ownership** — which is currently still a
strong feature (e.g. possession of cars) but will very likely change due to new mobility
concepts — can be depicted in **a scenario about future mobility** (through **personas,
needfinding**, etc.)."

**The enabling condition.** "However, this transition **becomes a reality only when the
infrastructure of cities is geared to the new concepts**, offering the user the best possible
experience."

**How the data drives the design.** "On the basis of **movement data (big data/analytics)**,
it is possible to determine:
- the **optimal locations for concepts in terms of Car2go and Bike2Go**, for **bus stops and
  point-to-point connections**;
- **zones and driving lanes** for **private mobility service providers** (e.g. **Uber, Lyft,
  Didi Kuaidi**) and **public mobility services** (bus, train, tram)."

**The physical consequences.** "**Large parking lots are turned into green areas**; the old
parking spaces along the streets become **the new waiting lines for autonomous vehicles.** By
**actively making possibilities for parking scarce in the cities** and optimizing a range of
offers of limitless mobility, **the quality of life in the cities will increase in the medium
term.**"

**The system it plugs into.** "Thus **smart mobility will be a relevant pillar of smart city
initiatives**, which are connected to the respective mobility concepts through **sensors,
smart video surveillance, and video analytics** with the goal of **controlling such complex
systems**."

**The trend maturation the example demonstrates:**

| Trend | Status |
|---|---|
| **"Access over ownership"** | "Provided only **a weak trend signal** only a short while ago"; has "now developed into **a macro trend** that was picked up by many companies and affects numerous areas" |
| **"Decentralization of everything"** | "An emerging **micro trend** — as can be seen in the first **blockchain initiatives or free-floating parking spaces**" |
| **Smart cities** | "**From Singapore to Berlin, everybody is talking about the mega trend of smart cities.**" |

This is the framework's own logic in miniature: *Perspective* on ownership as an inherited
norm; *Opportunity* in the changed customer need; *Solution* prototyped as re-zoned street
space; and a *Vision* — livable cities — clear enough for stakeholders to invest in.

## Key Takeaways
- Apply strategic foresight as **the ability to plan and shape the desired future**.
- **Understand the past in order to be able to shape the future.**
- Recognize and extrapolate the **changes in customer needs**.
- Take a **positive view of the future** and use tools to make it become a reality.
- Work with **interdisciplinary teams** to achieve transversal dissemination of the mindset.
- **Develop a clear vision so that everybody on the team pulls in the same direction.**
- Define clear-cut steps so the vision can be implemented in a targeted manner.
- Use the tools described in the **"Playbook for Strategic Foresight and Innovation."**
- Develop a **combined mindset from design thinking and strategic foresight** for the digital
  vision.
