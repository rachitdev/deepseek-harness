# Chapter 12 (Book §2.2) — What Are the Benefits of Interdisciplinary Teams?

## Core Idea
Peter "emphasizes two dimensions: The team members must have **in-depth technical knowledge
as well as a broad general knowledge**." The reason is iteration speed: "the most important
thing is that we learn and iterate at a fast pace. This, in turn, only works when the
questions are asked — and challenged — as early as possible and the things developed so far
are **looked at from a different perspective**."

The chapter's sharpest distinction is between two kinds of mixed team:

> "On **interdisciplinary** teams, ideas are produced on a collective basis. In the end,
> **everybody feels responsible for the overall solution**… In comparison with
> **multidisciplinary** teams, they have the advantage in that, at the end, everybody
> stands behind the commonly created product or service — a factor of success that
> multidisciplinary teams cannot afford. **On a multidisciplinary team, every member is an
> expert who advocates his or her specialization. The solution is often a compromise.**"

And a note on how good experts actually behave: "Frequently, it so happens that the expert
begins his or her own work way before the actual topic is discussed and **poses critical
questions instead of simply tying in to their area of expertise**. As a consequence, the
things that were developed so far assume a new quality because they are suddenly considered
using a holistic approach."

## Frameworks Introduced

**The T profile** (concept developed by **Dorothy Leonard-Barton**)
- **Vertical bar — depth of expertise.** "The respective specialist skills that someone has
  acquired in his training and that are required for the individual steps in the design
  thinking process and the implementation project. A psychologist, for example, brings
  along experience and methodological knowledge to the 'Understand' phase."
- **Horizontal bar — breadth of knowledge**, defined by two characteristics:
  1. **Empathy** — "able to take up somebody else's perspective while looking past his
     own."
  2. **Ability to collaborate / interface expertise** — "open; interested in other
     perspectives and topics; and curious about other people, environments, and
     disciplines."
- The payoff rule: "The better the understanding of the way others think and work, the
  faster and greater the common progress and success in the design thinking process."

**The Pi profile.** "The profile of an **adaptive employee** who develops further in
addition to his specialization." Not only able to steep himself in a colleague's discipline
and understand it, "he also has the ability to respond to challenges of everyday working
life and educate himself accordingly. In this way, he can assume **multiple roles**, which
usually are linked to one another in terms of content: for example, **business analyst and
UX designer**, or **software developer and support employee**." One leg is depth in one's
own core competency; the other can be **depth of expertise in leadership**.

**The U-shaped team.** "The name can be inferred from the analogy of established systems,
which, **after a disruption, return to an equilibrium and are more stable than they were
before**. Systems that break at the first sign of friction are the opposite."
U-shaped teams "help one another, are there for one another, even if one member has a bad
hair day and is not able to deliver his usual performance; these are teams in which **you
can make a mistake without having to be afraid of losing your job**." The economic claim:
**"safety and security mean productivity."**

**Six simple rules for a successful interdisciplinary team** (see Reference Tables).

**Whole Brain® model (HBDI®)** — four physiological brain structures, not two halves.

**"Connect 2 Value" (Lewrick & Link)** — three levels and six principles (see Worked
Example).

## Key Concepts
- **The I profile is the easy hire.** "It is probably easier to find people with specialist
  skills for the individual process steps — corresponding to an **I profile** — than those
  who have both forms of knowledge."
- **The interview tell for collaboration ability.** "A good sign for the ability to
  collaborate, from the horizontal bar, is when people during the interview **talk not only
  about themselves but also emphasize what they have learned from others** and how valuable
  the collaboration was for the common project."
- **Have candidates draw their own T profile.** "The way somebody fills out the profile
  yields a lot of information on his way of thinking and expressing himself. At the same
  time, it shows how somebody **interprets the requirements for collaboration** and
  presents himself in this respect."
- **The design thinking boot camp** as a recruiting instrument. Two purposes: (1) "a quick
  and easy way for candidates to experience design thinking and its individual steps in
  practice and judge for themselves if they want to collaborate in this way"; (2) "those who
  assemble the team quickly get an idea of the **specialist and social skills** of future
  team members."
- **Mix hierarchies too.** "Putting teams together with people not only from various
  disciplines and departments, but also **from different hierarchical levels**, has proven
  to be of particular help." By-product: "the new interdisciplinary approach will spread
  faster and transversally throughout the whole company."
- **Interdisciplinarity has a cost.** "The complexity of the collaboration increases with
  this approach compared to an individual way of working, without iterative agreements. The
  complexity can be reduced using a few simple rules, on which the entire team should agree
  from the outset."
- **The two-halves-of-the-brain myth.** "The myth that the left hemisphere of the brain is
  responsible for analytical thinking and the right hemisphere for holistic thinking is
  widespread. But because our brain is anything but well organized, **such a simple division
  into two halves is quite wrong.** Tendencies can indeed be identified" — number
  understanding, spatial thinking and face recognition more in the right half; recognizing
  details and capturing small time intervals more in the left. As a **quick** workshop
  heuristic the book still uses the two-halves model to sort participants roughly into
  **"analytical/systematic" versus "intuitive/iterative."**
- **Lilly's target company shape:** "a team of **15 to 25 employees**, in which technical
  skills are represented **multiple times**" — enabling several parallel projects and
  constant reshuffling of teams.
- **Lilly's motto:** "People who feel safe, secure, and comfortable, who are supported and
  appreciated — **with all their rough edges, warts and all** — are highly motivated to
  deliver a great performance."

## Mental Models
- **Compromise is the failure mode of multidisciplinary teams.** Interdisciplinarity is
  judged by shared ownership of the whole, not by representation of each specialty.
- **Swap thinking preferences deliberately.** "It can be quite valuable to swap the
  thinking preferences on the team; individual tasks are assigned to the respective people
  in the relevant phases during the design process. Ultimately, better solutions are
  achieved this way. **It also helps us when communicating ideas and concepts to decision
  makers** and telling the story of new products and services."
- **One strong member per quadrant.** "Teams are most effective when they have **one team
  member each with a strong manifestation of one of the quadrants** in the metaphorical
  model."
- **Team composition is stage-dependent.** Profiles can be placed **on an S curve** across
  a company's growth phases; the profile mix a start-up needs is not the mix a scaling
  company needs.
- **Development path: T → Pi, on a U base.** "The principles of T-shaped people developing
  into Pi-shaped people should always be borne in mind — **on the basis of a U-shaped
  team**."
- **Networking beyond the team decides team success.** "The personal relationships of and
  networking between individual people **beyond the teams** must be seen as decisive in the
  success of teams."

## Anti-patterns
- Assembling experts who each defend their specialization — you get a compromise, not a
  solution.
- Consulting an expert only after the concept is fixed, so the critical questions arrive
  too late to change its quality.
- Recruiting only I profiles because they are easier to find.
- Trusting the left-brain/right-brain split as if it were physiology.
- A team with an unbalanced quadrant mix and no plan to fill the gaps — Marc's start-up is
  the book's own example of exactly this.
- A "stylish" high-performance team without psychological safety: a non-U-shaped team
  breaks at the first friction.

## Reference Tables

**Six simple rules for a successful interdisciplinary team**

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| **1** | "The team has a **common vision** that must be fulfilled as a team. In the best case scenario, it will be an answer to the **'How might we…' question**." |
| **2** | "Every step in the design thinking process is **led by the respective expert** (vertical bar in the T profile) on the team, who suggests a clear direction and tried-and-tested methods while offering support in the implementation." |
| **3** | "The team has adopted **common values**. They have been developed together and are **visible by everybody at all times**. The brainstorming rules, for example, are a good basis." |
| **4** | "There is an **atmosphere of trust** in which everybody has respect for and accepts the experience of the next person — at least when the role of expert is taken on." |
| **5** | "Only those who know the expectations and to what extent they can be met can become better. **The more comprehensible the feedback of the team is, the more specific** will be the way in which the entire team and its individual members become better and ultimately act in common." |
| **6** | "**Shared common processes and quality standards** are determined, so that everybody always knows the procedure and the necessary requirements for the desired result and can orient themselves to them." |

**Whole Brain® / HBDI® — the four-quadrant metaphorical model**

| Mode | Styles of thinking the book ascribes | Label used |
|---|---|---|
| **Cerebral** (upper) | Cognitive and intellectual | — |
| **Limbic** (lower) | Structured and emotional | — |
| **Upper right** | Integration, conceptualization, creative, innovative | **"Innovators"** |
| **Lower right** | Teaching, writing, communication, interpersonal | **"Humanitarians"** |

The model "breaks down our brain into four physiological brain structures… not only the
left and right mode but also… the cerebral and limbic mode."

**The T → Pi → team transformation — two vital steps**
1. **Identify gaps on the team and the development potentials of individual employees.**
   Company management and team leaders identify gaps and potentials and "discuss personal
   interests and associated development paths with the employees."
2. **Draw up a training schedule** to close gaps and promote employees — "attuned to both
   corporate and personal goals and **verifiable by means of defined milestones**."

## Worked Example — Marc's start-up team and the "Connect 2 Value" framework

**The team as cast.** "Marc and his team are already quite well set up for their start-up:
**Innovators such as Marc**, **women with a business sense like Beatrice**, **makers and
shakers such as Vadim**, and **team members like Stephan and Alex**, who actively control
the business development." The book names the archetypes on the quadrant map: *The innovator
(Marc) · The maker and shaker (Vadim) · The salesman (Alex) · The strategist (Stephan)*.

**Marc's own profile.** "Marc's HBDI® profile is characterized by a focus on the **yellow
and blue quadrants**." His vision: "a great vision for the health care system of the
future, in which **patients decide autonomously which information goes into their
electronic patient record**. In a first step, he focuses on a few functions of his idea that
he will implement **on the blockchain** and with the relevant actors in the ecosystem."

**The diagnosed gap.** Placing the HBDI® profiles on an **S curve** across the growth
phase: "Rational decisions must be made and measures defined for the next scaling step and
for the transformation of the company from a start-up into a mid-sized enterprise. Above
all, team members with strong **organizational ('green')** and **interpersonal ('red')**
characteristics are necessary for this. **Both are currently underrepresented on Marc's
start-up team.**" That is the explicit hiring brief for his next expansion.

**The "Connect 2 Value" framework (Lewrick & Link) — three levels**
- Connect **knowledge** to value
- Connect **talent** to value
- Connect **systems** to value

"It combines the design thinking mindset with the core aspects of a human-centered
approach, co-creation, and value creation as well as with **strategic foresight** for the
definition of a clear vision for the enterprise… and ensures that **top talents are deployed
in those places where they can create the greatest value**." Its structure runs **People →
Teams of teams → Ecosystems**, with **Execution** underneath and the tagline **"Make it
happen… fast and agile!"**

**How Marc lives the six principles**

| # | Principle | What Marc actually does |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **People: Attract the right talents** | "Marc does not just consider the skills, the T profile, and the HBDI® model but also **invites potential candidates directly to his workshops** to find out whether they generate a positive energy and mood on the team and live the design thinking mindset." |
| **2** | **Realize team and network effects** | Composition of teams and goal-oriented collaboration, internally and with external partners. "Marc sees **the human beings behind the companies** in the business ecosystem as team members, too." |
| **3** | **Shape for growth & scale** | "From the outset, Marc co-designs the business ecosystem. He creates a **win-win situation for all actors**. He uses technologies and platforms for scaling, which also make the realization of **data-driven innovations and business models** possible." |
| **4** | **Create the magic** | "For Marc, leadership means to **bring the magic to the team and make possible the impossible** for them. It includes the creation and communication of a business vision, which helps to encourage the teams to fulfill their mission and act in an entrepreneurial way." |
| **5** | **Create a new mindset** | "Positive energy is the elixir and driver of outstanding achievements and motivation. An **open feedback culture** allows the top talents to contribute their skills optimally. Ideas and concepts are implemented quickly, and **failure is part of a positive learning effect**." |
| **6** | **Make it happen** | "Marc implements the concepts — fast and agile… **assigns the top talents and resources to those activities that generate the greatest value**. He consciously uses external skills and platforms of actors in the ecosystem for the realization." |

**And the teams of teams.** "Using the teams of teams concept, the respective skills in
larger organizations can be utilized for all design challenges. This will pay off once
Marc's company grows and **individual squads work on different functionalities** for
patients and for involving other actors in the ecosystem." (Developed further in book
Ch. 3.4.)

## Key Takeaways
- Put together interdisciplinary teams of **T-shaped and Pi-shaped** members.
- Let team members draw up their own T-shaped profiles **and present them to one another**.
- Develop a common vision of collaboration; adopt common values and rules.
- Create an atmosphere of trust and respect.
- Involve all disciplines; ensure technically relevant perspectives and
  personality-related views are represented **in equal measure**.
- Visualize different thinking preferences (e.g. with HBDI®) to improve mutual
  understanding.
- Exploit team heterogeneity deliberately to promote creativity.
- Identify weaknesses on the team; define measures and develop collaboration skills step by
  step.
- Use the **"Connect 2 Value"** framework to implement projects — fast and agile.
