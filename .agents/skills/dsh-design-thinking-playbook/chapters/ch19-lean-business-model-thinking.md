# Chapter 19 (Book §3.2) — How to Apply Lean Business Model Thinking

## Core Idea
"In a dynamic environment, there is little time to plan activities over a long period. **The
lean start-up mindset is therefore the best for the continuation of design thinking
activities.** Like design thinking, it focuses on **short iteration cycles and the
consideration of customer feedback**. At the end, the product life cycle and the development
of the business model should be designed in such a way that **few costs are incurred**."

The chapter's central artifact is **the lean canvas by A. Maurya**, "which can be expanded
very well by other design thinking methods, such as **the customer profile** and **the
experiment reports** by **A. Osterwalder**."

**"The canvas is like the blueprint of an architect. It outlines the key factors, which
ultimately cover the most important areas of a company: problem, solution, customers, value
propositions, and financial viability."**

The tension it manages is stated visually as **TOO FAT / TOO THIN / LEAN** — the business
model must carry enough to be viable and no more.

## Frameworks Introduced

**The lean canvas — its geometry.** "The **unique selling proposition (USP) constitutes the
core of the canvas** that either **solves a specific problem of the customer** (e.g.
sovereignty over patient data) or **meets a specific need** (e.g. design thinking consultancy
adapted to Asian business practices)."
- **Right side** — "the sales channels and revenue are entered alongside the customer
  segments (**divided into target segment and early adopters**)."
- **Left side** — "concentrates on **rational reasons**. The focus is on **the problem
  statement, your own solution, and the existing alternatives, as well as on the cost
  structure.**"
- **Supplements** — the **customer profile** helps us understand the needs of the customer
  better; the **experiment reports** "document our approach and show **the progress per
  iteration**."

**The 12-field development sequence** — the order in which to fill the canvas (see Worked
Example), plus a **viability check (reflection)** loop.

**Six entry points into the canvas for digital offers** (see Reference Tables).

**Three business-model archetypes: circular value creation · servitization · frugality**
(see Reference Tables).

**NABC — need / approach / benefits / competition** — "another simple tool for drawing up a
value proposition. **If you are able to answer the key questions from the four areas
adequately, the value proposition is usually clear.** Unlike the customer profile, **the NABC
analysis takes the competition in due consideration, so it allows for an additional focus on
uniqueness.**" The mapping back to the canvas: *needs and benefits derive from the customer
profile · approach corresponds to the solution · competition is in line with the existing
alternatives.*

**Service design thinking → the customer journey and the service blueprint.**

**The Problem to Growth & Scale framework** — six steps (see Reference Tables).

**Lean management** — the expansion after initial scaling.

## Key Concepts
- **The fit ladder.** "With design thinking, we achieve a **customer/problem fit** — that is,
  our understanding of the customer and the problem has the necessary depth. Using lean
  start-up, we initially create a **problem/solution fit**; then we refine it into a
  **product/market fit**. Over the course of the process, **we reduce the risk step by step
  through experiments; at the same time, we heighten the value of the project.** From the
  simple prototype, we develop a **minimum viable product (MVP)**, expand it with each
  iteration, and test it with the customer… **An MVE helps to test the desired ecosystem
  scenario.**"
- **The short concept — the X-for-Y analogy.** "The value proposition we communicate should
  be formulated as concisely as possible **in one brief sentence**… by way of an analogy,
  such as **'Sailcom' (car sharing for boats)** or **'WatchAdvisor' (TripAdvisor for
  watches)**." The canvas field itself gives *"YouTube = Flickr for video."* Lilly's own
  attempts — **"Book and Fly for Design Thinking"** and **"Last Minute Problem Solving"** —
  are recorded as *not yet good enough*: "she is not really happy with her ideas."
- **Servitization.** "**Customer needs are fulfilled without the customer having to own the
  physical product.**" Examples: **Rolls-Royce's power-by-the-hour model** in aircraft
  engines; **pay-per-use** in software; textile subscriptions — "socks or baby clothing can
  now be ordered with a monthly subscription"; **Vigga**, "a model that enables mothers to
  **lease organic baby clothing**."
- **Circular value creation.** "The basic idea is to **generate value from a waste
  product**. **Filippa K** takes back the worn clothing of their customers and **sells it in
  their own secondhand shops**."
- **Frugality / resource efficiency.** **YR** "makes it possible for customers to create
  their own personal T-shirts, which are then **produced on demand**. This way, **there won't
  be any overproduction of designs**, which are later hard to sell on the market. Consumers
  also have **a greater emotional bond** to the product and the brand."
- **Value excellence.** "The quality and **operational excellence** in the service used to be
  in the foreground. Then, more and more importance was put on **experience**. Today and in
  the future, **value** will take center stage. **Value excellence is achieved through a
  strong customer centricity and close, proactive collaboration with customers.**"
- **Customer development (Steve Blank).** "Customers must be **developed and won over during
  the development phase** in order to increase the probability of a scaling business in the
  end."
- **Good business design, defined.** "Characterized by the fact that **an obvious market
  opportunity is identified and transformed into a scaling business.**"

## Mental Models
- **The canvas is a hypothesis set, not a plan.** Everything in it is testable, and the
  experiment reports run alongside it as the evidence log.
- **Take your time.** "**A good business model rarely emerges within a few hours; valuable
  insights are gained over the course of time.**"
- **Build variants, then select.** "Create different business model and lean canvas variants
  of your idea and **select the most promising.**"
- **Don't split business design from product development.** "In the next phases, business
  design and product development are frequently done separately; from our experience, it is
  profitable to **create the development paths for the customers, the business, and the
  product in close collaboration.**"
- **Two levers at scale.** "Ultimately, there are two levers: **optimizing structures and
  processes, and scaling the excellence of teams.** This requires at least **T-shaped people,
  positive energy, and leadership.**"
- **Don't follow a blueprint at scale.** "Check the mindset and skills in your organization
  and **don't just follow a blueprint.**"

## Anti-patterns
- Racing the canvas to completion in a workshop afternoon.
- Deriving a value proposition without checking the competition — the gap NABC exists to
  close.
- A value proposition that aims at too many tasks, pains and gains instead of solving a few
  extremely well.
- Building business design and product development in separate tracks.
- Scaling structures before checking whether the organization's mindset and skills can carry
  them.
- Designing a service without mapping the invisible provider activities — the blueprint
  layer where the failures hide.

|---|---|
| **Problem** | "Where are the main problems the business must resolve? Describe **the three biggest problems of our customers**" |
| **Existing alternatives** | "How were the problems solved up to now?" |
| **Solution** | "Describe a solution for **every problem**" |
| **Key metrics** | "What measurable figures show whether the solution works?" |
| **Unique value proposition** | "What is the value we provide the customer? Formulate **a simple, clear message, which explains why the solution is different and noteworthy**" — as *profit generator*, *problem solver*, and "support/procedure for the performance of tasks in specific use cases, which are important to the customer" |
| **Short concept** | "**X for Y analogy** — is there a simple analogy? (e.g. YouTube = Flickr for video)" |
| **Unfair advantage** | "Something that makes it difficult for the others to copy the solution" |
| **Channels** | "Through which channels do our customer segments want to be reached?" |
| **Customer segments** | "List the target and user groups. **For whom do we create value? Who are your most important customers?** Use the persona" |
| **Early adopter** | "Describe the characteristics of **the ideal customer**. Use the persona for this" |

| **Revenue streams** | "List the sources of income" |

**Companion — the customer profile:** *Name · persona description ·* **Jobs-to-be-done ·
Pains · Use cases · Gains** — filled in for both the target segment and the early adopter.

**Companion — the experiment reports:** repeating *Experiment N / Learnings N* pairs, each
with **Step 1: Hypothesis** ("We believe that…"), **Step 2: Test** ("To verify this, we
will…"), **Step 3: Metrics** ("And measure…"), **Step 4: Criteria** ("We are on the right
track if…"), plus documentation of the test (e.g. photographs) and "We have learned the
following:" The framing question above the whole strip: **"What is the best way to get to
the goal?"**

**Six entry points into the canvas for digital offers**

| | Entry point | Logic | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Via **the problem** (or oftentimes the solution) | "The classic entry point… then the value proposition is inferred via the customers (by means of the customer profile)" | — |
| **B** | Via **the solution** | "We look for **physical objects that have not yet been digitized** and create the same customer application digitally instead of with a physical object" | **iPod/CD → streaming services**, or **3D printers for food** |
| **C** | Via **missing existing alternatives** | "At present, almost everything is being digitized… We look for things for which **there is no digital alternative yet**" | **Fiat money → cryptocurrencies** |
| **D** | Via **the creation of an unfair advantage** | "Protection against one's own business model being copied… such as by **building up an entire business ecosystem**" | **WeChat** (Ch. 20) |
| **E** | Via **digital instead of physical channels** | "Digital access, **directly or through a partner**, can be the entry point" | **Amazon services** |
| **F** | Via **existing business models** | "Business models that have **not yet been implemented in one's own environment or language area**. Existing solutions are copied and adapted to the new digital context" | **Online retailers for eyeglasses** |

**Three business-model archetypes**

| | Circular value creation | Servitization | Frugality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Idea** | "Create value from waste — close the cycle" | "Create value for customers without having them own the product (physical good)" | "Promote the effective use of resources" |
| **Focus** | "Recycling and resource efficiency are in the focus" | "All-inclusive carefree packages, pay-per-use models, etc." | "Solutions that actively lower consumption and production" |
| **Example** | **Filippa K** operates secondhand shops | **Rolls-Royce: power by the hour** | **YR: on-demand production of T-shirts and shoes** |

**NABC — the key questions**

| Need | Approach (Solution) | Benefits | Competition (Existing alternatives) |
|---|---|---|---|
| What customers do we address? (internal/external) | What is the approach to a solution or the performance promise? | What are the benefits for the customer? | What alternatives exist today and in the future? |
| What is the main customer need? | What is the product, service, or process proposal? | What are qualitative and quantitative benefits for the customer? | What is the risk? |
| What does the customer have problems with? | How will the product or service be developed and launched on the market? | **How can I communicate them in the form of storytelling?** | How were the problems solved up to now? |
| Where are opportunities for improvement? Where does our opportunity lie? What are the main problems? | **How will we earn money with it? (business model)** · What technological drivers affect our business model? | | |

**Ten key factors of success to check when validating a value proposition**
1. Embedding in a great business model.
2. Focus on what is important for **most** customers.
3. Focus on topics for which the customer is **willing to spend a lot of money**.
4. Focus on **unresolved** problems.
5. **Aims at only a few tasks, pains, and gains but solves them extremely well.**
6. Alongside functional task fulfillment, takes into account the **emotional and social**
   components of the tasks.
7. Alignment to **the customer's measurement of success**.
8. Differentiation from the competition.
9. **Better than the competition in at least one dimension.**
10. **Difficult to copy.**

**The service blueprint — levels and build sequence**

Levels differentiated: *activities the customer goes through · visible activities of the
provider with respect to the customer · invisible activities of the provider that are
directly connected but not visible to the customer · supporting activities and systems of the
provider or his partners.* **"The customer interaction line separates customer activities
from provider activities."**

| Step | Post-it colour | Questions |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Actions and activities of the customer on the timeline** | large and small circles for the respective activity | "What are the main actions? What happens in this step?" |
| **2. Customer interactions** | **green** | "What are the touch points with the customer?" |
| **3. Visible activities of the provider** | **blue** | "Who are the actors? Who is involved all together? What are the actions of the provider?" |
| **4. Invisible activities** | — | "What are the activities that are invisible to the customer?" |
| **5. Supporting activities and systems** | **orange** | "What supports the whole thing (software, platform, processes)?" |
| **6. Evaluation** | **red** | "What is critical? Where are errors possible? Where are risks and vulnerabilities?" |

"Afterward, we can infer **ideas for improvements, new processes, or entire service
innovations**. The service blueprint also helps us **in interviews/tests with
customers/users** to capture the situation and obtain feedback." "The blueprint can be
**iteratively improved and adapted. The iterations also help identify the weak points in the
process.**"

**The Problem to Growth & Scale framework — six steps**

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| **1. Design thinking** | Determine your potential users, customers, and stakeholders · identify the real customer needs · **find solutions that are as elegant as they are simple** · use systems thinking and data analytics |
| **2. [Research]** | Understand the problem and the situation **holistically** · take advantage of market research instruments · validate and supplement your findings |
| **3. Co-creation** | Win more customers, users, and **lead users** and retain them · get the necessary help from the outside · work on teams **across departmental and organizational boundaries** · develop **MVP/MVEs** and build up trust in partners and customers |
| **4. Lean start-up** *(Test & validate)* | Develop your offer further **with little capital** · structure the solution step by step · improve and validate your business model with **fast iterations** · **clarify the biggest uncertainties with experiments** |
| **5. Business ecosystem design, agile product and customer development** *(Launch & execute)* | "**Shift your activities from problem solving and search for a solution to finding the right business model with business ecosystem design**" · develop product and business model further with agility (e.g. **Scrum**) · **think in variants when developing business models** · "**multidimensional consideration of the business models of all actors in the ecosystem becomes a success factor**" |
| **6. Scale** *(Growth)* | Prepare the organization for growth and scaling · establish **scalable processes, structures, and platforms** · check the mindset and skills in your organization and **don't just follow a blueprint** · "bring the entire organization one step forward and break new ground" |

**Lean management — the nine principles to add after initial scaling**
*We focus on our own strengths · we optimize our business processes constantly · we bank on
continuous improvement · we live internal customer orientation · we rely on the
responsibility of the teams for the fulfillment of their mission · we act in decentralized
and customer-oriented organizational structures · we offer the best support in the leadership
of our employees · we exercise open and direct communication · we conserve resources and
avoid waste.*

Typical tools: **value stream design, continuous improvement process (CIP), 5S, TPM, kanban,
and Makigami.** Levers it opens: "fixed costs can be reduced by **outsourcing**, or hierarchy
levels can be reconsidered by **the expansion of competencies**." And a design-thinking
crossover: "it is of great value to **make the process tangible, to shift process steps
physically and scrutinize them.**"

## Worked Example — Building the lean canvas in order, for Lilly's and Marc's start-ups

**The two USPs at stake.** "For the start-ups of Lilly and Marc, it is crucial to define a
unique selling point. **Lilly** is faced with the challenge of having to define **what
exactly differentiates her start-up from the countless other consultancy firms on the
market**. **Marc** must find features that **convince patients to manage their 'patient
record' on his solution.** Furthermore, the value proposition is designed to **provide a
solid basis in the long run for monetizing the collected data.**"

Marc's USP therefore *solves a specific problem* — **sovereignty over patient data**.
Lilly's *meets a specific need* — **design thinking consultancy adapted to Asian business
practices**.

**The build order.** "**Typical start at 1. or 4.**"

| # | Field | Question | Methods the book prescribes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Problem** | "What is the (main) problem?" | Problem interview · stakeholder map · **6WH** |
| **2** | **Customer segments** | "What do the customer profiles look like?" | Customer segmentation |
| **3** | **Unique value proposition** | "What is our value proposition / what is our unique selling proposition (USP)?" | **Value proposition design · NABC** |
| **4** | **Solution** *(→ MVP/MVE)* | "What is the idea/solution?" | Creativity techniques · **solution interview** · analogies/benchmarking |
| **5** | **Existing alternatives** | "How has the problem been solved up to now?" | Interview & observation · competition analysis · best practices |
| **6** | **Channels** | "How can we reach our customers?" | Cf. marketing distribution. **Think of: direct, indirect + automated; analog + digital; B2C, B2B, and B2B2C** |
| **7** | **Unfair advantage** | "What will make it difficult for others to copy the solution?" | **Think of: ecosystem design, black ocean; SWOT analysis, team analysis** |
| **8** | **Key metrics** | "What key metrics do we need to manage activities?" | KPI · **pirate metrics** · A/B testing |
| **9** | **Revenue streams** | "What is the potential revenue? How will we earn money?" | Solution interview · pricing · customer analysis |
| **10** | **Cost structure** | "What are the biggest costs?" | Cost structure analysis · fixed + variable costs · **make or buy partnerships** |
| **11** | **Short concept** | "Is there a simple analogy?" | Business model analogies · creativity techniques |
| **12** | **Experiment reports** | "**Where does the greatest risk lie? How can we test assumptions/hypotheses?**" | Prototyping + test · experiments |

**The viability check (reflection)** closes the loop: *"Are we satisfied with the viability of
our business model?"* — with the follow-ups **"Can we test our business model? Can we further
improve the business model at the moment? Are there other/better variants?"**, using business
model analogies. **NO → back to fields 1 to 11. YES → continue.**

**Applying entry point D to Marc.** Marc's **unfair advantage** is exactly the book's fourth
digital entry point: protection against copying "by building up an entire business
ecosystem," with **WeChat** cited as the model — which is why his path in the Problem to
Growth & Scale framework runs through step 5, business ecosystem design, and why an **MVE**
rather than an MVP is what tests his scenario.

**A worked service-design case — Sanitas and Swissmom.** "The Swiss health insurer
**Sanitas** integrates the **Swissmom portal** in their service portfolio. Thus a customer
interaction chain is created with expectant mothers, **from the initial wish for a child, to
pregnancy, to giving birth, up to dealing with toddlers.** The actual product of insurance
protection for newborns is **embedded in the service components**. In addition, mothers are
given access to **a community and a further range of offers for childcare.**" The insurance
product stops being the offer and becomes one node in an experience chain.

## Key Takeaways
- Start with design thinking to capture customer needs and get to the **problem/solution
  fit**.
- Use the lean canvas to **summarize the findings from design thinking**.
- Create different business model and lean canvas variants, and select the most promising.
- **Take your time** — a good business model rarely emerges within a few hours.
- Determine a very good unique selling point and value proposition; use a variety of tools
  and experiments (customer profile, NABC).
- **Reduce the risk systematically through experiments** and adapt the lean canvas.
- Modify your business model later "when the market requires it or the customers desire it."
- Combine design thinking, research, co-creation and lean start-up **even at established
  companies** for innovation projects.
- Consider lean management approaches **early** to optimize efficiency and effectiveness at a
  high level of scaling.
