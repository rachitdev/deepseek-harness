# Glossary — Quick and Easy Music Theory (Lloyd Steiner, 2009)

Definitions follow the book's own wording where it gives one; chapter numbers point to
where the term is introduced or first used substantively.

**Alphabet Check** — Steiner's procedure of writing the letters A–G out from the root, numbering them 1–5 (or 1–7), and reading off the slots the chord needs; it fixes the letters before any accidental is chosen (Ch 7)
**Ascending** — playing a series of notes that go up in pitch (Ch 22)
**Augmented Chord** — dissonant three-note chord, formula 1 (4) 3 (4) 5; "think of a haunted house" (Ch 6, 9)
**Authentic Cadence** — V → I; the most commonly used ending in history (Ch 28)
**Amen Cadence** — alternate name for the plagal cadence, from its use in hymns (Ch 28)
**Bass** — the lowest sounding instrument; the only one that determines a chord's inversion (Ch 27)
**Burning Question / Answer / Reason** — Steiner's recurring device for resolving enharmonic-spelling disputes (Ch 1, 4, 10)
**Cadence** — a series of chords that imply an ending to the ear (Ch 28)
**Chord** — any combination of 3 or more different notes played at the same time (Ch 6)
**Chord Abbreviation** — letter-plus-suffix notation for a chord (Gm, F7, Bb+, Dbº) that needs no key statement (Ch 24)
**Chord Progression** — a series of chords, played one right after the other (Ch 6)
**Consonant** — pleasing to the ear (Ch 6)
**Deceptive Cadence** — V → vi; a fake ending that fools an audience expecting V → I (Ch 28)
**Descending** — playing a series of notes that go down in pitch (Ch 22)
**Diatonic Triads** — chords that naturally occur in a given scale (Ch 18)
**Diminished Chord** — dissonant three-note chord, formula 1 (3) 3 (3) 5; "think of zombies coming after you" (Ch 6, 10)
**Diminished Seventh** — 1 (3) 3 (3) 5 (3) 7 (Ch 11)
**Dissonant** — not pleasing to the ear (Ch 6)
**Dominant** — degree 5 of a scale, and the chord built on it; the tension chord (Ch 18, 20)
**Dominant Seventh** — 1 (4) 3 (3) 5 (3) 7; a major chord plus a minor seventh; written with a bare 7 (Ch 11)
**Double Flat (bb)** — lowers a letter two half steps; required in chords like Gb minor (Bbb) (Ch 8, 11)
**Double Sharp (x)** — raises a letter two half steps; required in chords like B augmented (Fx) (Ch 9, 11)
**Enharmonic Spelling** — the same note spelled in different ways; F# = Gb, Eb = D#, B# = C (Ch 3)
**Figured Bass** — a way to write music based on numbers (Roman numerals) that refer to a specific chord in a scale; requires the key to be stated (Ch 23)
**First Inversion** — third lowest, fifth above it, root on top; written `6` in figured bass or `Chord/3rd` in abbreviations (Ch 25)
**Flat (b)** — to play one note down from the given letter; may fall on a white key (Cb, Fb) (Ch 1, 10)
**Half Step** — a note directly next to another; one fret on guitar or bass (Ch 1)
**Half-Diminished Seventh** — 1 (3) 3 (3) 5 (4) 7 (Ch 11)
**Harmonic Minor** — natural minor with the third of the V chord raised one half step, making the Dominant major; the most widely used minor scale (Ch 21)
**Harmony** — a combination of notes that when played together produces a pleasing effect (Ch 6)
**Interval** — the distance between two notes (Ch 6)
**Inversion** — an arrangement of a chord in which a note other than the root is lowest (Ch 25)
**Key** — a group of notes based on a particular note and scale that forms the tonal center of the music (Ch 3)
**Line Note** — a note drawn directly on one of the five staff lines (Ch 2)
**Major Chord** — consonant three-note chord, formula 1 (4) 3 (3) 5; "think of a sunny day" (Ch 6, 7)
**Major Scale** — a joyful scale of 8 notes with the pattern W W H W W W H (Ch 4)
**Major Seventh** — 1 (4) 3 (3) 5 (4) 7 (Ch 11)
**Mediant** — degree 3 of a scale, and the chord built on it (Ch 18)
**Melodic Minor** — a minor scale with one pattern ascending (W H W W W W H) and the natural minor pattern descending; the least used minor scale (Ch 22)
**Minor Chord** — consonant three-note chord, formula 1 (3) 3 (4) 5; "think of a rainy day" (Ch 6, 8)
**Minor Seventh** — 1 (3) 3 (4) 5 (3) 7 (Ch 11)
**Minor-Major Seventh** — 1 (3) 3 (4) 5 (4) 7; a minor chord plus a major seventh (Ch 11)
**"Mommy, please hold my hand"** — Steiner's name for spelling a progression out chord by chord in longhand instead of using figured bass or abbreviations (Ch 23)
**Musical Alphabet** — the 7 letters A B C D E F G, repeating after G (Ch 1)
**Natural Minor Scale** — a solemn scale of 8 notes with the pattern W H W W H W W (Ch 5)
**Ninth Chord** — a five-note chord using slots 1, 3, 5, 7, 9; e.g. C9 = C E G B D (Ch 17)
**Out of Key / Out of Tune / Sour** — playing or singing a note that is not in the current chord or not in the scale (Ch 4, 27)
**Plagal Cadence** — IV → I; the "Amen cadence" (Ch 28)
**Pop Quiz** — Steiner's in-chapter multiple-choice interruption, used in Chapter 4 to test the seventh degree of D major (Ch 4)
**Root** — slot 1 of any chord; the note the chord is named after (Ch 6)
**Root Position** — root lowest, third directly above, fifth directly above that (Ch 25)
**Scale** — a specific arrangement of notes with a distinct pattern of half steps and whole steps (Ch 3)
**Second Inversion** — fifth lowest, root above it, third on top; written `64` in figured bass or `Chord/5th` in abbreviations (Ch 25)
**Seventh Chord** — a four-note chord using slots 1, 3, 5, 7 (Ch 11)
**Sharp (#)** — to play one note up from the given letter; may fall on a white key (B#, E#) (Ch 1, 9)
**Space Note** — a note drawn in the space between two staff lines (Ch 2)
**Subdominant** — degree 4 of a scale, and the chord built on it (Ch 18)
**Submediant** — degree 6 of a scale, and the chord built on it (Ch 18)
**Subtonic** — degree 7 of a scale, and the chord built on it (Ch 18)
**Supertonic** — degree 2 of a scale, and the chord built on it (Ch 18)
**Sus Chord (Suspended Chord)** — a chord with no third; the third is replaced by a second, fourth or sixth (Ch 12)
**Sus2** — 1 (2) 2 (5) 5 (Ch 12, 14)
**Sus4** — 1 (5) 4 (2) 5; the most common suspended chord (Ch 12, 13)
**Sus6** — 1 (7) 5 (2) 6 (Ch 12, 15)
**7sus4** — 1 (5) 4 (2) 5 (3) 7; the only suspended seventh chord the book teaches (Ch 16)
**Tonal Center** — the note a song is built around; the first half of a key (Ch 3)
**Tonic** — degree 1 of a scale, and the chord built on it; establishes tonality (Ch 18, 20, 28)
**Treble Clef / G Clef** — a clef placing G on the second line up in the staff (Ch 2)
**Triad** — a three-note chord: root, third, fifth (Ch 6)
**Voicing** — how you choose to invert a chord (Ch 26)
**Whole Step** — two notes away from each other; two half steps (Ch 1)
**5 Line Staff** — 5 lines drawn parallel and equidistant from each other in order to write musical notation (Ch 2)
