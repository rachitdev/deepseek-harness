# Chapter 9 — Building Augmented Chords

## Core Idea
Augmented chords stack two 4-half-step gaps. They are rare — "these little buggers
aren't used too often, and you'll hear the reason why when you play them" — but the
build procedure is unchanged, and they are the chapter where exotic spellings (E#, B#,
and double sharps written **x**) first become unavoidable.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Augmented Chord Formula
```
            Augmented
        1        3        5
           (4)      (4)
```

### Spelling under pressure
Because the fifth is raised, the alphabet check will routinely force a sharp onto a
letter that has no black key above it (B→B#, E→E#) or a double sharp (F→Fx). Steiner's
rule holds regardless: the letter is set by the alphabet check; the accidental does
whatever it must to hit the counted pitch.

## Key Concepts
- Root→third: **4 half steps**. Third→fifth: **4 half steps**.
- Augmented is dissonant with an unsettling, mysterious sound (Ch 6: "think of a
  haunted house").
- Even though they are uncommon, "you might find yourself using them in your
  compositions regardless."
- **There are no naturally occurring augmented chords in a major scale** or in a
  natural minor scale (stated in Chs 18 and 19) — augmented triads are always
  imported.

## Mental Models
- **Symmetry warning.** With two equal 4-step gaps, every augmented triad sounds like
  every inversion of itself; the *spelling* is the only thing that distinguishes them.
  This is why Steiner ends the chapter with "Be sure to check your spellings!
  Augmented chords often produce some interesting spellings of notes."
- **Extra credit as difficulty gradient.** The homework escalates: normal roots →
  extra credit (C#, G#) → extra extra credit (A#), because each tier forces more
  double sharps.

## Anti-patterns
- Refusing to write E#, B#, or a double sharp because "that's just F / C / a natural
  note." The letter is non-negotiable.
- Spelling the third of D augmented as Gb — G is slot 4, not slot 3.
- Assuming an augmented chord can be used diatonically in a major or natural minor key.

## Worked Example
*Building a D augmented chord — the chapter's build.*

1. **Root (1) = D.**
2. **Third (3):** count up 4 half steps from D → D#/Eb (1), E (2), F (3), F#/Gb (4).
   Black key.
3. **Alphabet check:** D=1, E=2, F=3, G=4, A=5. Slot 3 must be the letter **F**, slot 5
   the letter **A**. So the third is **F#** — "because the letter G is non-existent in
   this chord."
4. **Fifth (5):** count up 4 half steps from F# → G (1), G#/Ab (2), A (3), A#/Bb (4).
   Black key again. It must be **A#**, "because the letter B is non-existent in this
   chord."

**D augmented = D F# A#.**

What this means:
- Play D, F# and A# at the same time and you sound a "D augmented chord."
- If someone asks for a D augmented chord, you play D, F# and A#.

*(The book's second bullet here mistakenly prints "D, F# and A" — the answer key and the
formula both confirm A#.)*

## Key Takeaways
- Augmented = 1 –(4)– 3 –(4)– 5.
- Dissonant, mysterious, uncommon, but buildable by the same procedure.
- Expect E#, B#, and double sharps; the alphabet check outranks convenience.
- Never occurs naturally in a major or natural minor key.
