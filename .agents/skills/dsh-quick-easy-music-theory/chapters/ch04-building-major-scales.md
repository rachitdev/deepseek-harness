# Chapter 4 — Building Major Scales

## Core Idea
The major scale is "arguably the most important scale in music." It is one fixed
pattern of whole and half steps applied to any starting note, and the five spelling
rules from Chapter 3 decide every sharp-or-flat question along the way. Build it by
walking the formula one interval at a time from the root, then tick the checklist.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Major Scale Formula
```
 1     2     3     4     5     6     7     8
    W     W     H     W     W     W     H
```
Eight positions, seven intervals. Position 8 is always the same letter as position 1 —
"if it isn't, go back and double-check your work."

### The build loop
1. Write the formula down (do this every time until it is memorised).
2. Put the chosen root in slot 1.
3. Walk one interval at a time on the piano diagram, filling slots 2→8.
4. At every black key, ask which spelling keeps rules 1–5 intact.
5. Run all five rules against the finished scale.

## Key Concepts
- **Major Scale** — "a joyful scale of 8 notes" with the W W H W W W H pattern.
- Use: happy, joyful, bright songs — and also "the ever-popular ballad and love songs."
- **C major is the only major scale with no sharps and no flats.**
- Spelling matters as much as in English. Steiner's analogy: respelling "the" as "uhe"
  because it suits your mood — "You'll be sure to fail every spelling test (and music
  theory test) if you simply use spellings that suit your mood."
- The 13 major scales in the homework (C, Db, D, Eb, E, F, F#, Gb, G, Ab, A, Bb, B) are
  "all of the major scales you will ever use."

## Mental Models
- **The alphabet check beats the ear.** When you land on a black key, you do not decide
  by sound; you decide by which letter is still unused and in order.
- **Order the scales by accidental count.** Homework 2 asks the reader to find the
  significance of the order C, G, D, A, E, B, F#, F, Bb, Eb, Ab, Db, Gb. The answer
  key: "The scales are written in order of the number of sharps and flats contained in
  the scale." (This is the circle of fifths, arrived at empirically.)
- **Out of key = wrong, not just unusual.** "If your band is playing a song in C major,
  and you sing or play an A#, you are now 'out of key,' 'out of tune,' 'sour,' or just
  plain wrong!"

## Anti-patterns
- Calling the third degree of D major "Gb" — skips F (rule 1) and puts G before F
  (rule 2). Both violated.
- Answering "Db" for the seventh of D major — violates rules 1, 2 and 4.
- Answering "B#" for the seventh of D major — violates rules 1, 2 and 3.
- Answering "C" for the seventh of D major — rule-legal but *wrong interval*: C is a
  half step above B, not a whole step. Steiner calls this out separately: "Study the
  layout of the piano and take note of which keys are not separated by black keys."
- Ending on a different letter than you started on.

## Worked Example
*Building the D major scale, step by step, exactly as the chapter does it.*

Formula: `1 –W– 2 –W– 3 –H– 4 –W– 5 –W– 6 –W– 7 –H– 8`

| Step | Reasoning | Result |
|------|-----------|--------|
| 1 | Chosen root | **D** |
| 2 | Whole step up from D | **E** |
| 3 | Whole step up from E lands on a black key. Gb would skip F (rule 1) and put G out of order (rule 2). | **F#** |
| 4 | Half step up from F# | **G** |
| 5 | Whole step up from G | **A** |
| 6 | Whole step up from A | **B** |
| 7 | *Pop Quiz.* Whole step up from B. Options given: a. Db, b. C#, c. B#, d. C. Db breaks rules 1, 2, 4. B# breaks rules 1, 2, 3. C is rule-legal but is only a half step above B. | **C#** (answer b) |
| 8 | Half step up from C# | **D** |

**D major = D E F# G A B C# D.** Starts and ends on the same letter — checklist passes.

For comparison, the chapter's first build, C major = C D E F G A B C, passes all five
rules with no accidentals at all — the only major scale that does.

## Key Takeaways
- W W H W W W H, always, from any root.
- Spell by rule, not by ear; the alphabet check resolves every black key.
- 13 major scales cover everything you will ever need.
- F# and Gb are the enharmonic pair, at 6 sharps and 6 flats respectively.
- Scales listed C G D A E B F# / F Bb Eb Ab Db Gb are ordered by accidental count.
