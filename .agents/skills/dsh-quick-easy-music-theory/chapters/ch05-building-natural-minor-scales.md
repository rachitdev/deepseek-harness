# Chapter 5 — Building Natural Minor Scales

## Core Idea
Building a minor scale is mechanically identical to building a major scale — same five
rules, same walk-the-formula procedure. Only the pattern of whole and half steps
changes. The natural minor is the base form; harmonic minor (Ch 21) and melodic minor
(Ch 22) are both derived from it, so this scale must be solid first.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Natural Minor Scale Formula
```
 1     2     3     4     5     6     7     8
    W     H     W     W     H     W     W
```
Compare the major: `W W H W W W H`. The half steps have moved from positions 3–4 and
7–8 to positions 2–3 and 5–6.

### Pre-committing to an accidental type
Before building Bb natural minor, Steiner looks at the root, predicts "this scale
having a lot of black keys (just a hunch!)" and locks rule 5 in advance: *if we come
across more black keys, we have to spell them as flats, and not as sharps.* This turns
the rules from a post-hoc check into a forward constraint.

## Key Concepts
- **Natural Minor Scale** — "a solemn scale of 8 notes" with the W H W W H W W pattern.
- There are **three minor scales** in the book: natural, harmonic, melodic. Natural
  comes first because the others are built on it.
- The same five spelling rules from Chapter 3 apply verbatim.
- The 13 natural minor scales in the homework (C, C#, D, D#, Eb, E, F, F#, G, G#, A,
  Bb, B) are "every natural minor scale you will ever use."
- **Relative-key observation:** Bb natural minor has 5 flats, and so does Db major —
  and they contain the same notes in a different order. Steiner leaves this as a
  question: "Perhaps the two are related?" He does not name it *relative minor*.

## Mental Models
- **One engine, many patterns.** The only thing that ever changes between scale types
  in this book is the string of W's and H's. Learn the engine once.
- **Rule 3 as a tie-breaker.** In Bb minor the third degree could be C# or Db by sound;
  C# is impossible because C is already used as degree 2. Two independent rules (3 and
  5) both point at Db.
- **PRACTICE, PRACTICE, PRACTICE.** The chapter's closing instruction — write the
  formulas out, write every scale out, then *play* them.

## Anti-patterns
- Mixing a sharp into a flat-heavy minor scale (rules 4/5).
- Spelling the third of Bb minor as C# — repeats the letter C, breaking rule 3.
- Jumping to harmonic or melodic minor before natural minor is memorised: "A solid
  understanding of the natural minor scale is required in order to understand both the
  harmonic and melodic minor scales" (stated in Ch 21).
- Writing scales out but never playing them on your instrument.

## Worked Example
*Building the Bb natural minor scale, the chapter's own build.*

Formula: `1 –W– 2 –H– 3 –W– 4 –W– 5 –H– 6 –W– 7 –W– 8`

| Step | Reasoning | Result |
|------|-----------|--------|
| 1 | Chosen root | **Bb** |
| 2 | Whole step up from Bb | **C** |
| 3 | Half step up from C = black key. Must be flat (rule 5, pre-committed) and cannot be C# because C is already used (rule 3). | **Db** |
| 4 | Whole step up from Db | **Eb** |
| 5 | Whole step up from Eb | **F** |
| 6 | Half step up from F | **Gb** |
| 7 | Whole step up from Gb | **Ab** |
| 8 | Whole step up from Ab — must return to the root | **Bb** |

**Bb natural minor = Bb C Db Eb F Gb Ab Bb** — five flats.

Steiner's follow-up observation: Db major (Db Eb F Gb Ab Bb C Db) also has five flats
and the same seven pitch classes, just starting somewhere else.

## Key Takeaways
- W H W W H W W, from any root, using the same five rules.
- Natural minor is the parent of harmonic and melodic minor.
- 13 natural minor scales cover everything.
- D# and Eb minor are the 6-sharp / 6-flat enharmonic pair.
- Bb natural minor and Db major share notes — the seed of the relative-key idea.
