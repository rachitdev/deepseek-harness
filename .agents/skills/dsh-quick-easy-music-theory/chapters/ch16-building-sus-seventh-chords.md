# Chapter 16 — Building Suspended Seventh Chords

## Core Idea
"Sometimes a three-note chord just doesn't cut it." You can add a seventh to a
suspended chord, but Steiner teaches only one such chord: the **7sus4**. Sus2 and sus6
sevenths are legal — "be my guest" — but he judges them "a little less than desirable
or useful."

## Frameworks Introduced

### The 7sus4 Formula (shown on C)
```
             C7sus4
    1        4        5        7
       (5)      (2)      (3)
```
The sus4 formula with one more interval of 3 half steps stacked on top.

### Seven-slot alphabet check
Root C → C D E F G A B. Slots 1, 4, 5, 7 → **C, F, G, B**.

## Key Concepts
- Root→fourth: **5 half steps**. Fourth→fifth: **2**. Fifth→seventh: **3**.
- The added interval (3 half steps above the fifth) is the same top interval as a
  dominant seventh — which is why the chord is written `C7sus4`, not `Cmaj7sus4`.
- "The only suspended seventh chord that is discussed in this book uses the sus4."
- A combined abbreviation like `C#maj7sus4` does exist (Ch 24) and resolves to C#, F#,
  G#, B — a *major* seventh over a sus4.
- Homework: the same 17 roots as Chapters 13–15.

## Mental Models
- **Editorial pruning as a teaching tool.** Steiner repeatedly narrows the space to
  what is used: only 6 of the many seventh chords in Ch 11, only 3 sus types in Ch 12,
  only 1 suspended seventh here. The message is that theory's job is to give you
  usable options, not exhaustive ones.
- **Add-on, not rebuild.** Build the sus4 you already know, then take three more half
  steps.

## Anti-patterns
- Building 7sus2 or 7sus6 chords under the impression they are standard. They are
  buildable but Steiner considers them low-value.
- Spelling the seventh of C7sus4 as A# — "any form of the letter A cannot exist in this
  chord"; slot 6 is A and slot 7 is B.
- Measuring the seventh from the root rather than from the fifth.

## Worked Example
*Building a C7sus4 — the chapter's build.*

Formula: `1 (5) 4 (2) 5 (3) 7`

1. **Alphabet check:** C(1) D(2) E(3) F(4) G(5) A(6) B(7). Required letters:
   **C, F, G, B.**
2. **Fourth (4):** from C count up 5 half steps → C#/Db (1), D (2), D#/Eb (3), E (4),
   F (5). **Answer: F.**
3. **Fifth (5):** from F count up 2 half steps → F#/Gb (1), G (2). **Answer: G.**
4. **Seventh (7):** from G count up 3 half steps → G#/Ab (1), A (2), A#/Bb (3). "The
   correct spelling would be Bb instead of A# because any form of the letter A cannot
   exist in this chord." **Answer: Bb.**

```
              C7sus4
    1       4       5       7
    C       F       G       Bb
```

What this means:
- Play C, F, G and Bb at the same time and you sound a "C7sus4 chord."
- If someone asks for a C7sus4 chord, you play C, F, G and Bb at the same time.

## Key Takeaways
- 7sus4 = 1 –(5)– 4 –(2)– 5 –(3)– 7.
- The only suspended seventh the book teaches.
- The top interval (3) matches the dominant seventh, hence the bare `7` in the name.
- Alphabet check runs to seven slots; slot 6 (A) is skipped, so A-anything is illegal.
