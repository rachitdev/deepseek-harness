# Chapter 25 — Chord Inversions

## Core Idea
A chord is defined by *which notes* it contains, not by the order they are stacked in.
Rearranging them changes the **inversion**, not the chord. Inversions are notated in
both systems — superscript 6 and 64 in figured bass, a slash plus a bass letter in chord
abbreviations — and they are the raw material for good arrangement in Chapter 26.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The three positions
- **Root Position** — root (1) lowest, third (3) directly above, fifth (5) above that.
  C major: **C E G**.
- **First Inversion** — third (3) lowest, fifth (5) above, root (1) on top.
  C major: **E G C**.
- **Second Inversion** — fifth (5) lowest, root (1) above, third (3) on top.
  C major: **G C E**.

### Figured bass inversion symbols
| Written | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| `I`, `ii`, `iii`, `IV`, `V`, `vi`, `viiº` | Root position |
| `I6`, `ii6`, `V6`, `viiº6` … | First inversion (superscript 6) |
| `I64`, `ii64`, `V64`, `viiº64` … | Second inversion (superscript 6, subscript 4) |

### Chord abbreviation slash notation
| Written | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| `C`, `Ebm`, `F#dim`, `D#aug`, `Gº`, `B+` | Root position |
| `Letter/Letter` | Inverted. First letter = the chord; second letter = the note on the bottom. |

"Although the chord abbreviations will not tell you directly what inversion a chord is
in, it will tell you what note is on the bottom (thus ultimately revealing its
inversion)."

## Key Concepts
- **C Major Chord** — "any combination of the notes C, E, and G played at the same
  time." The word *any* is what licenses inversions.
- "It is still a C major chord no matter how the notes are arranged. Although the actual
  chord did not change, the inversion changed."
- Deriving the inversion from a slash chord: identify whether the bass letter is the
  chord's root, third or fifth.
- "Inversions are incredibly important. They provide more specific instructions on which
  way to play the chord desired."

## Mental Models
- **Bass note = inversion.** The only thing that determines the inversion is which
  member of the chord is on the bottom. (Chapter 27 makes this literal: only the lowest
  *instrument* determines it.)
- **Two notations, one idea.** `V6` in C major and `G/B` say the same thing; the first
  needs a key, the second does not.
- **Inversions are a creative tool, not bookkeeping.** "They will help expand your
  creativity when composing music."

## Anti-patterns
- Believing a rearranged chord is a different chord.
- Reading `Dm/A` as "D minor plus an A." The A is a chord member being placed in the
  bass, not an addition.
- Using figured bass inversions without stating the key.
- Ignoring inversions and jumping the hand around the instrument (see Chapter 26).

## Key Takeaways
- Three positions: root, first (third in bass), second (fifth in bass).
- Figured bass: bare numeral / `6` / `64`.
- Abbreviations: `Chord/BassNote`.
- The chord's identity never changes; only its inversion does.
- Inversions are the input to arrangement.
