# Chapter 21 — Diatonic Triads (Harmonic Minor)

## Core Idea
Harmonic minor exists to fix one problem: the natural minor's Dominant chord is minor,
so i–v–i does not deliver the tension and resolution that I–V–I does. The fix is
minimal and surgical — **raise the third of the V chord by one half step, and change
nothing else.** That single altered note is the entire difference, and it is why
harmonic minor is the most widely used minor scale.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The reasoning chain (Steiner's argument, in order)
1. I – V – I is unmatched for beginning → tension → resolution (Ch 20).
2. In natural minor both the tonic and the dominant are minor, so i – v – i falls short.
3. Making both major would destroy the minor character — "then there would really be no
   point in having the minor scale in the first place."
4. Changing the **Tonic** makes it "sound far too much like the major scale" and
   "changes the tonal center too much, and the ear becomes lost."
5. Therefore change the **Dominant**: "a drastically more pleasing effect is achieved,
   while still keeping the overall sound quality of the minor scale."
6. "We shall call it harmonic minor."

### The alteration rule
> The difference "occurs in the V (Dominant) chord where the third (3) of the chord is
> sharped (raised one half step)."

And the containment rule:
> "Do not substitute the altered note (in this case it's A#) in any other chords
> containing an A. The only one that needed change in the first place was the V, so
> leave it that way!"

### The universal harmonic-minor pattern
```
    i    iiº    III    iv    V    VI    VII
```
Identical to natural minor except degree 5, which is now **major**.

## Key Concepts
- "A solid understanding of the natural minor scale is required in order to understand
  both the harmonic and melodic minor scales."
- **This is why harmonic minor is the most widely used minor scale, even more so than
  the natural minor scale.**
- "There are only seven chords and seven notes that will work in each key."
- The ear test Steiner prescribes: play i – v – i (B minor – F# minor – B minor), then
  play i – V – i (B minor – F# major – B minor). "Hear the difference?"

## Mental Models
- **Minimum viable alteration.** One note in one chord. Music theory here is presented
  as engineering: identify the defect, change the smallest thing that fixes it.
- **The altered note is local.** It belongs to the V chord only. This is the book's most
  easily broken rule and it says so explicitly.
- **Film borrowed the arc from music.** "If you think of music in terms of film, most
  (successful) films follow the same formula of: Beginning – tension – resolution in the
  story line. Films 'borrowed' that idea from music."

## Anti-patterns
- Raising the altered note everywhere it appears in the key. Only the V chord takes it.
- Making the tonic major to "brighten" a minor key — that destroys the tonal centre.
- Writing a harmonic minor piece with no V chord in it. The chapter's homework requires
  "at least one Dominant (V) chord" in every composition.
- Expecting the *scale* to look different. In this book's presentation the seven scale
  letters are unchanged; only the V chord's third moves.

## Worked Example
*Converting B natural minor to B harmonic minor, and hearing the result.*

1. Write out B natural minor's diatonic triads: Bm, C#º, D, Em, **F#m**, G, A.
2. Locate the V chord: **F# A C#** (F# minor).
3. Raise its third one half step: A → **A#**.
4. The chord becomes **F# A# C#** — F# major. Everything else stays.
5. Ear test:
   - `i – v – i` = B minor → F# minor → B minor
   - `i – V – i` = B minor → **F# major** → B minor

The second version supplies the tension and resolution the first lacks.

Full B harmonic minor chord set: **B minor, C# diminished, D major, E minor, F# major,
G major, A major.**

Homework: harmonise all 13 harmonic minor keys; write three instrument-free songs of at
least six chords in three different harmonic minor keys, each containing at least one
V chord; play them all. Homework 6 asks which minor scale is more widely used — the
answer is harmonic minor.

## Key Takeaways
- Harmonic minor = natural minor with the V chord's third raised one half step.
- The alteration is local to the V chord; do not propagate it.
- Pattern: i iiº III iv **V** VI VII.
- It is the most widely used minor scale precisely because of this one change.
