# Chapter 15 — Building Sus6 Chords

## Core Idea
The sus6 has a root, a fifth and a sixth — and unusually, the formula is written in that
order, so the first move is a 7-half-step leap. Steiner frames it as the sus2 with its
intervals reversed, but the slots are different (1-5-6 rather than 1-2-5), so it spans
nine half steps rather than seven.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Sus6 Formula
```
              Sus6
        1        5        6
           (7)      (2)
```

### Six-slot alphabet check
Root A → A B C D E F. Slots 1, 5, 6 → **A, E, F**. "In this case you must use the
letters A, E, and F."

## Key Concepts
- Root→fifth: **7 half steps**. Fifth→sixth: **2 half steps**.
- "The sus6 formula looks very similar to the sus2 formula, the only difference is that
  the intervals are reversed" — sus2 is (2)(5), sus6 is (7)(2).
- Written `Dsus6`, `Asus6`, etc.
- Same 17-root homework list as the other sus chapters.

## Mental Models
- **Leap first, step second.** Unlike every previous chord, the biggest interval comes
  first. Counting seven half steps by hand is error-prone; Steiner spells the count out
  key by key rather than shortcutting it.
- **The sixth is only two half steps above the fifth**, so once you have the fifth the
  chord is nearly done.

## Anti-patterns
- Miscounting the 7-half-step jump. Count aloud: on A that is A#/Bb, B, C, C#/Db, D,
  D#/Eb, E.
- Spelling the sixth of Asus6 as Gb — the letter G is not in the chord; slots are A, E, F.
- Treating sus6 as an inversion of something else. It is its own formula.

`A#sus6 = A# E# Fx` — the sixth must be a form of F, and the pitch is G, so it is
written as F double sharp.

**The three sus types compared**

| Type | Slots | Intervals | Span |
|------|-------|-----------|------|
| sus2 | 1 – 2 – 5 | (2)(5) | 7 |
| sus4 | 1 – 4 – 5 | (5)(2) | 7 |
| sus6 | 1 – 5 – 6 | (7)(2) | 9 |

## Worked Example
*Building an Asus6 — the chapter's build.*

Formula: `1 (7) 5 (2) 6`

1. **Alphabet check:** root A → A(1) B(2) C(3) D(4) E(5) F(6). Required letters:
   **A, E, F.**
2. **Fifth (5):** from A count up 7 half steps → A#/Bb (1), B (2), C (3), C#/Db (4),
   D (5), D#/Eb (6), E (7). **Answer: E.**
3. **Sixth (6):** from E count up 2 half steps → F (1), F#/Gb (2). "You must call this
   F# instead of Gb because the letter G does not exist in this chord."
   **Answer: F#.**

```
              Asus6
        1       5       6
        A       E       F#
```

What this means:
- Play A, E and F# at the same time and you sound an "Asus6 chord."
- If someone asks for an Asus6 chord, you play A, E and F# at the same time.

## Key Takeaways
- sus6 = 1 –(7)– 5 –(2)– 6.
- Slots are 1, 5, 6 — the widest sus chord at nine half steps.
- Steiner explicitly calls sus2 and sus6 interval-reversals of each other.
- A#sus6 needs a double sharp (Fx).
