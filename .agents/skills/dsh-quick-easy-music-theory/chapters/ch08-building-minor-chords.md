# Chapter 8 — Building Minor Chords

## Core Idea
"The principles of building minor chords are exactly the same as building major chords.
The only difference is the number of half steps between the root (1), third (3) and
fifth (5)." Major is 4-then-3; minor is 3-then-4. Everything else — the alphabet check,
the spelling rules, the naming convention — is unchanged.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Minor Chord Formula
```
              Minor
        1        3        5
           (3)      (4)
```

### Alphabet check, unchanged
For a C-rooted chord:

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C | D | E | F | G |

Letters C, E, G are mandatory. Any accidental attaches to those letters.

## Key Concepts
- Root→third in any minor chord: **3 half steps**. Third→fifth: **4 half steps**.
- The minor triad is consonant with a sad, solemn sound (Ch 6: "think of a rainy day").
- Spelling discipline persists: the third of C minor is Eb, never D#, because the
  letter D cannot exist in a C-rooted triad.

## Mental Models
- **Swap the parentheses.** The mental move from major to minor is literally exchanging
  (4)(3) for (3)(4). Nothing else in the procedure changes.
- **Letter set first, accidentals second.** Deciding C-E-G before counting means the
  black key answer is never ambiguous.

## Anti-patterns
- Spelling C minor as C, D#, G. Same sound, wrong chord spelling — the letter D is not
  allowed in a C-rooted triad.
- Saying "I played a minor chord" without the letter (see Ch 7's banned phrases).
- Recounting from the root to find the fifth. The fifth is measured from the **third**,
  not from the root.

**Major vs minor at a glance**

| Type | 1→3 | 3→5 |
|------|-----|-----|
| Major | 4 | 3 |
| Minor | 3 | 4 |

## Worked Example
*Building a C minor chord — the chapter's build.*

1. **Root (1) = C.**
2. **Third (3):** count up 3 half steps from C → C#/Db (1), D (2), D#/Eb (3). A black
   key.
3. **Alphabet check:** C=1, D=2, E=3, F=4, G=5. Slot 3 must be the letter **E** and
   slot 5 the letter **G**. So the black key is spelled **Eb**, not D# — "since the
   letter D cannot exist in this chord."
4. **Fifth (5):** count up 4 half steps from Eb → E (1), F (2), F# (3), G (4). The
   fifth is **G**.

**C minor = C Eb G.**

What this means:
- Play C, Eb and G at the same time and you sound a "C minor chord."
- If someone asks for a C minor chord, you play C, Eb and G at the same time.

## Key Takeaways
- Minor = 1 –(3)– 3 –(4)– 5.
- The procedure is identical to major; only the interval sizes swap.
- Double flats are legal and sometimes required (Gb minor → Bbb).
- Measure the fifth from the third, not the root.
