# Glossary — Music Theory for Computer Musicians (Michael Hewitt, 2008)

**Accented passing note** — A non-chord tone placed on a strong beat; a classical appoggiatura. References the harmony through its resolution to a chord tone (Ch 24)
**Added note chord** — A basic triad enriched with an extra pentatonic note; gives added sixth, added ninth, or added sixth/ninth chords (Ch 16)
**Additive rhythm** — Building meters by multiplying a short value into cells of two and three and chaining them, rather than subdividing a beat (Ch 14)
**Aeolian mode** — The sixth diatonic mode, tonic on A; identical to the natural minor scale; the mode most popular music is written in (Ch 20)
**Amen cadence** — See Plagal cadence (Ch 8)
**Amplitude** — The height of a sound wave; perceived as intensity or volume (Ch 1)
**Anhemitonic** — A pentatonic scale containing no semitones (Ch 22)
**Appoggiatura** — See Accented passing note (Ch 24)
**Arpeggio** — The notes of a chord played successively rather than simultaneously; creates motion within a static harmony (Ch 24)
**Artificial scale** — A scale invented by a composer for a particular purpose (Ch 22)
**Asymmetric meter** — A metric cycle with an odd number of beats such as 7/8 or 11/4 (Ch 7, 14)
**Attack** — The first component of the sound envelope: the rise from an initial low to a peak value (Ch 1)
**Augmented interval** — A perfect or major interval increased by a semitone (Ch 12)
**Augmented second** — The F–G# gap in the harmonic minor's upper tetrachord; source of its exotic character (Ch 11, 12)
**Augmented triad** — Two stacked major thirds (4 + 4 semitones); a major triad with a sharpened fifth; symbol C+ (Ch 17)
**Bar** — One complete metric cycle; also a measure. In most electronic tracks, four quarter notes (Ch 4, 7)
**Bar line** — The vertical line drawn after each completed metric cycle (Ch 7)
**Bass clef** — Also the F clef; covers notes below Middle C; its symbol coils around the F line (Ch 5)
**Beaming** — Connecting notes of a common value within a bar; applies to eighths and shorter (Ch 4, 5)
**Beat (musical)** — A regular pulse of consistent duration (Ch 4)
**Beats (acoustic)** — Roughness caused by clashing wavelengths from complex ratios; the physical cause of discord (Ch 8)
**Blue notes** — Characteristic inflections of ordinary scale notes in blues scales; Eb, F#, and Bb in the blues hexatonic, seven-note, and nine-note scales (Ch 22)
**Brace** — Binds treble and bass staffs into a staff system for keyboards and harps (Ch 5)
**Cadence** — The way a chord progression is satisfactorily brought to a close (Ch 8)
**Cent** — 1/1200 of an octave; one equally tempered semitone = 100 cents (Ch 25)
**Chain of thirds** — The stacking principle generating triad → seventh → ninth → eleventh → thirteenth; stops at the thirteenth (Ch 23)
**Characteristic register** — The pitch range typical of a given instrument (Ch 1)
**Chladni figures** — Geometric sand patterns on bowed metal plates; a demonstration that musical vibration is ordered (Ch 1)
**Chord progression** — A sequence of chords placed in an order that makes sense to the ear; requires root movement (Ch 8, 18)
**Chordal inversion** — Using a chord with a note other than the root in the bass (Ch 13)
**Chromatic alteration** — Altering a chord using a note not in the scale; standard in complex harmony (Ch 23)
**Chromatic scale** — The twelve semitones of the octave; the pool from which any scale's notes are selected (Ch 3)
**Closed spacing** — Chord notes crowded together as closely as possible (Ch 13)
**Common triads** — Major and minor triads collectively (Ch 9, 17)
**Compound interval** — An interval larger than an octave; a simple interval plus one or more octaves (Ch 6)
**Compound time** — A simple time signature with each beat subdivided into three: 6/8, 9/8, 12/8 (Ch 7)
**Concord** — An interval or combination that sounds harmonious to the ear, owing to a simple frequency ratio (Ch 6, 8)
**Cross-rhythm** — Triplets played against pairs; three notes in one part against two in another (Ch 7)
**Decay** — The second envelope component: the fade from the peak. A piano has decay; an organ has none (Ch 1)
**Decibel (dB)** — Unit of volume; 0 dB is the threshold of hearing (Ch 1)
**Diabolus in musica** — "The devil in music"; the diminished fifth B–F (and its inversion, the devil's fourth F–B), banned by medieval monks (Ch 8, 12)
**Diminished interval** — A perfect or minor interval shrunk by a semitone (Ch 12)
**Diminished triad** — Two stacked minor thirds (3 + 3 semitones); symbol C° or Cdim; chord vii in major (Ch 17)
**Discord** — An interval or combination expressing tension, owing to a complex frequency ratio (Ch 6, 8)
**Divisive rhythm** — The Western classical approach of subdividing beats into twos and threes (Ch 14)
**Dominant** — The chord on the fifth degree of the scale; the active pole of tonality (Ch 8)
**Dominant seventh** — The seventh chord on degree five; the most popular seventh chord and the main agent of modulation (Ch 21)
**Dorian mode** — Second diatonic mode, tonic on D; like natural minor but with a major sixth (Ch 20)
**Dotted note** — A note extended by another half its value (Ch 4)
**Double flat** — Lowers a note by two semitones; required to spell diminished intervals (Ch 12)
**Drone** — A sustained tonic (often with its fifth) fixing the modal centre for the listener, as on the sitar (Ch 22)
**Duple time** — Beats grouped in twos, stress pattern s w (Ch 7)
**Enharmonic equivalents** — Two names for the same pitch, e.g. C# and Db; also enharmonically equivalent keys such as F# and Gb major (Ch 2, 19)
**Equal temperament** — Tuning that divides the octave into twelve mathematically equal semitones (Ch 25)
**Event editor** — A DAW view listing note events by name; e.g. in Cakewalk SONAR (Ch 2)
**F clef** — See Bass clef (Ch 5)
**First (unison, prime)** — The interval of two notes at the same pitch (Ch 6)
**Flat (b)** — Sign lowering a note by a semitone (Ch 2, 5)
**Free time** — Music with no sense of meter; Hewitt's example is Christian plainchant (Ch 7)
**Fundamental frequency** — The first partial/harmonic; determines the pitch we hear (Ch 1)
**G clef** — See Treble clef (Ch 5)
**Gapped scale** — Another name for the pentatonic scale, from the gaps left by the omitted notes (Ch 16)
**Guiro** — A scraped percussion instrument; Hewitt's example of studying real instruments (Ch 4)
**Gypsy scale** — Another name for the Hungarian minor mode (Ch 22)
**Harmonic (partial)** — A partial whose frequency is a whole-number multiple of the fundamental (Ch 1)
**Harmonic form of chromatic scale** — All twelve notes referenced as intervals against a single fixed tonic; the composer's palette for modal work (Ch 20, 22)
**Harmonic minor scale** — Natural minor with a sharpened seventh, giving a major dominant chord and a decisive V–i cadence (Ch 11)
**Harmonic mixing** — DJ practice of mixing with key awareness (Ch 15)
**Harmonic rhythm** — The rhythm created by chord changes in a song (Ch 4)
**Harmonic scale** — The Lydian mode with a flat seventh; suggested by the first 11 members of the harmonic series (Ch 20)
**Harmonic series** — The succession of whole-number-multiple partials of a fundamental (Ch 1)
**Harmonic interval** — An interval whose notes sound simultaneously (Ch 6)
**Harmony** — The working together of the simultaneous layers of a track (Ch 13)
**Hemiola** — The classical name for the three-against-two cross-rhythm (Ch 7)
**Hemitonic** — A pentatonic scale that uses semitones (Ch 22)
**Hertz (Hz)** — Sound waves per second (Ch 1)
**Hungarian Major** — Exotic scale with sharp second, sharp fourth, and flat seventh (Ch 22)
**Hungarian Minor** — The harmonic minor scale with a sharpened fourth; the Gypsy scale (Ch 22)
**Imperfect intervals** — Seconds, thirds, sixths, sevenths; variable, occurring in major and minor forms (Ch 8)
**Inharmonic partials** — Partials that are not whole-number multiples of the fundamental; produce indefinite pitch (gongs, bells) (Ch 1)
**Intensity** — Volume; governed by wave amplitude (Ch 1)
**Interval** — The relationship between two notes in terms of pitch (Ch 3, 6)
**Interval inversion** — Turning an interval upside down; numbers sum to 9 and qualities reverse (Ch 12)
**Intonation** — The fine points of how a scale is tuned (Ch 25)
**Ionian mode** — First diatonic mode, tonic on C; the major scale. Medieval nickname the Wanton mode (Ch 20)
**Jazz minor scale** — The ascending melodic minor, so called for its jazz use (Ch 11)
**Just intonation** — Tuning using pure intervals defined by their ratios (Ch 25)
**Key** — The note on which a scale is built (Ch 3, 9)
**Key signature** — The sharps or flats belonging to a key, placed at the beginning of the staff (Ch 15)
**Keynote** — See Tonic (Ch 15)
**Leading note** — The seventh degree, a semitone below the tonic, that pulls up to it (Ch 11, 17)
**Leading note triad** — Chord vii, the diminished triad in the major mode (Ch 17)
**Leading whole tone scale** — The whole tone scale with a semitone added below the tonic, to imply a leading note (Ch 22)
**Ledger line** — A short supplementary line for notes beyond the staff (Ch 5)
**Locrian mode** — Seventh diatonic mode, tonic on B; lowered fifth; no proper tonic triad; used in techno, drum and bass, hard trance (Ch 20)
**Lydian mode** — Fourth diatonic mode, tonic on F; sharpened fourth; euphoric (Ch 20)
**Major scale** — Seven-note scale with the pattern TTSTTTS (2212221) (Ch 3)
**Major triad** — Perfect fifth plus major third; 4 + 3 semitones (Ch 8)
**Measure** — See Bar (Ch 7)
**Mela** — One of the 72 basic scales of the Carnatic system of Hindustani music (Ch 22)
**Melodic interval** — An interval between successive notes of a line (Ch 6)
**Melodic minor scale** — Sharpened sixth and seventh ascending; identical to the natural minor descending (Ch 11)
**Meter** — The way beats are combined into groups that make sense to the ear (Ch 7)
**Metric cycle** — One repeating pattern of beat stresses; a measure or bar (Ch 7)
**Middle C** — The central reference point of the pitch register; C3 in MIDI convention (Ch 1, 2)
**Minor seventh** — Ten semitones; a mild discord (Ch 8, 21)
**Minor triad** — Perfect fifth plus minor third; 3 + 4 semitones (Ch 8)
**Mixolydian mode** — Fifth diatonic mode, tonic on G; flattened seventh; folk flavor (Ch 20)
**Mode** — The type of scale and its characteristic features; from the Greek word for "mood" (Ch 9, 16, 20)
**Mode of vibration** — A fractional length of a vibrating string producing its own frequency (Ch 1)
**Modulation** — Change of key; the ear accepts it only on hearing a perfect cadence in the new key (Ch 9, 21)
**Monophonic** — Capable of producing only one note at a time (Ch 6)
**Motive** — A short snippet of melody made recognizable by its distinctive rhythm (Ch 10)
**Musical alphabet** — The seven letters A B C D E F G, cycling (Ch 2)
**Mystic scale** — A six-note scale based on the harmonic series, developed by Alexander Scriabin (Ch 22)
**Natural minor scale** — Pattern TSTTSTT; the basic minor mode (Ch 9)
**Natural sign** — Cancels a sharp or flat (Ch 5)
**Neapolitan mode** — Any mode with a flattened second degree (Ch 22)
**Neapolitan triad** — The chord of F in E Phrygian (Ch 20)
**Neighbor note** — A note adjacent to a chord tone, used to embellish it (Ch 16, 24)
**Ninth chord** — Five notes; a seventh chord plus another third. Defined by its seventh and ninth (Ch 23)
**Non-chord tone** — Any note not part of the current chord, used for melodic interest (Ch 24)
**Octave** — The interval of eight scale steps; frequency ratio 2:1 (Ch 1, 2)
**Octave doubling** — Repeating chord tones at the octave to thicken a chord; double root and fifth first (Ch 13)
**Open spacing** — Chord notes given a wide spread over the register (Ch 13)
**Oscillator** — The sound-producing component of a synthesizer, generating frequency and harmonic content (Ch 1)
**Parallelism** — Harmonizing by shadowing a tune at fourths, fifths, or octaves (Ch 8)
**Partial** — One frequency component of a complex tone (Ch 1)
**Passing note** — A non-chord tone on a weak beat between two chord tones a third apart (Ch 24)
**Pentatonic scale** — A five-note scale; from C: C D E G A. The earliest known musical scale (Ch 16)
**Perfect cadence** — V to I; creates a complete sense of closure (Ch 8)
**Perfect concords** — Octave (2:1), perfect fifth (3:2), perfect fourth (4:3) (Ch 8)
**Perfect intervals** — Prime, fourth, fifth, octave; fixed, having only one mode (Ch 8)
**Phrase** — Two motives balancing each other in a question/answer relationship (Ch 10)
**Phrygian mode** — Third diatonic mode, tonic on E; flattened second; almost the exclusive mode of psy-trance (Ch 20)
**Pitch** — The ear's perception of wavelength; how high or low a note is (Ch 1)
**Plagal cadence** — IV to I; the "Amen" cadence; mellow settling on the tonic (Ch 8)
**Polyphonic** — Capable of producing more than one note at a time (Ch 6)
**Polyrhythm** — Different rhythms combined, e.g. cycles of five or seven against four (Ch 14, 24)
**Power chord** — Rock harmony built on perfect fifths/fourths (Ch 8)
**Primary triads** — Chords I, IV, V (or i, iv, v); they define all seven notes of the scale (Ch 8, 9)
**Prime** — See First (Ch 6)
**Psychoacoustics** — The study of how we hear and perceive vibrations (Ch 1)
**Pythagorean comma** — About 1/5 of a semitone; the gap between F# and Gb under pure tuning (Ch 19)
**Pythagorean intonation** — Tuning every note from pure 3/2 fifths (Ch 25)
**Quadruple time** — Beats grouped in fours, stress s w m w; the dominant modern meter (Ch 7)
**Quantization** — Snap-to-grid applied while recording (Ch 4)
**Quartal / quintal harmony** — Chords built by stacking fourths or fifths (Ch 16)
**Quintuple time** — Five beats to the bar, from additive cells 2+3 or 3+2 (Ch 14)
**Raga** — An Indian mode plus its conventions of important notes, melodic turns, time of day, and ethos (Ch 25)
**Relative minor / relative major** — Two keys sharing the same notes and key signature; count three notes down (or six up) from a major tonic (Ch 9, 15)
**Release** — The final envelope component: the time a sound takes to disappear after the note is released (Ch 1)
**Resolution (DAW)** — The note value set for the Pencil tool and grid (Ch 4)
**Rest** — A notated silence with a note-value equivalent (Ch 4)
**Returning tone** — A non-chord tone reached by step and immediately returned from (Ch 24)
**Root** — The note from which a chord takes its identity (Ch 8, 13, 18)
**Root movement** — The movement between the roots of successive chords; by fourths, thirds, or seconds (Ch 18)
**Root note (sampler)** — The keyboard note a sample is assigned to (Ch 2)
**Root position** — A chord with its root in the bass; the most stable position (Ch 13)
**Scale** — A rising or falling series of notes within an octave, repeated in other octaves (Ch 3)
**Scale degree** — The numbered position of a note within its scale (Ch 3)
**Secondary triads** — The remaining scale triads beyond I, IV, V; in C major, Dm, Em, Am (Ch 8)
**Semitone** — The gap between two adjacent keys or frets; 1/12 of an octave (Ch 3)
**Semitone rule** — Dim 3+3, min 3+4, maj 4+3, aug 4+4; works from any chromatic note (Ch 17)
**Seventh chord** — A triad plus a seventh above the root; a four-note tetrad (Ch 21)
**Sharp (#)** — Sign raising a note by a semitone (Ch 2, 5)
**Shuffle rhythm** — A triplet with its central note omitted (Ch 7)
**Simple interval** — An interval within the range of an octave (Ch 6)
**Slash chord** — An inverted chord written as root/bass, e.g. C/E, C/G (Ch 13)
**Snap to grid** — Automatic placement of drawn notes at the start of the set beat (Ch 4)
**Sonority** — Any simultaneous combination of notes; the interval is the simplest (Ch 8)
**Sound envelope** — Attack, decay, sustain, release: how a sound develops through time (Ch 1)
**Spacing** — How chord notes are spread within a sonority; closed or open (Ch 13)
**Spanish Phrygian** — The Phrygian mode with a sharpened third; Flamenco's mode (Ch 20)
**Staff** — Five lines on which notes are placed, on and between the lines (Ch 5)
**Static harmonic field** — Music based on a single sustained chord (Ch 13)
**Sub-audible / super-audible** — Below ~20 Hz / above ~20 kHz (Ch 1)
**Subdominant** — The chord on degree four; counterbalance to the dominant (Ch 8)
**Subtractive synthesis** — Starting from a harmonically rich waveform and filtering frequencies out (Ch 1)
**Suspended chord** — A chord in which the third is swapped for a note on either side of it (Ch 16)
**Sustain** — The envelope component during which the peak value is maintained (Ch 1)
**Syncopation** — Placing stress on a normally weak point of the bar (Ch 7)
**Sweet note** — Popular-music name for the third of a chord (Ch 13, 17)
**Tempo** — The speed of the beat, measured in beats per minute (Ch 4)
**Tetrachord** — A series of four notes spanning a fourth; scales use a lower and an upper tetrachord (Ch 11)
**Tetrad** — A four-note sonority such as a seventh chord (Ch 21)
**Thirteenth chord** — Seven notes, theoretically the whole scale; defined by root, seventh, thirteenth (Ch 23)
**Tie** — Joins two notes into one duration; the second does not re-sound (Ch 7)
**Timbre** — Tone color or tone quality; determined by harmonic content (Ch 1)
**Time signature** — Two numbers: beats per cycle (numerator) over their value (denominator) (Ch 7)
**Tonality** — The system in which one chord, the tonic, is raised above all others (Ch 8)
**Tone (interval)** — Two semitones (Ch 3)
**Tone (musical sound)** — Regular, periodic vibration; what distinguishes music from noise (Ch 1)
**Tonic** — The first degree of a scale; the tonal centre and home chord (Ch 3, 8)
**Transposition** — Transference of music from one key to another (Ch 15)
**Treble clef** — Also the G clef; covers notes above Middle C (Ch 5)
**Triad** — Three different notes sounded simultaneously: root, third, fifth (Ch 8)
**Triple time** — Beats grouped in threes, stress s w m (Ch 7)
**Triplet** — Three notes played in the time of two (Ch 7)
**Tritone** — The dissonant interval of six semitones; in the dominant seventh it resolves outward into the tonic third (Ch 21)
**Twelve-bar blues** — A chord sequence built on I, IV, and V (Ch 8)
**Velocity** — The intensity of an individual note or drum hit, on a 0–127 scale (Ch 1)
**Voice leading** — The smooth, flowing connection between different parts, exploiting shared notes (Ch 13)
**Waveform** — The shape of a sound wave; sine, square, triangle, sawtooth are the basic synthesis types (Ch 1)
**Whole tone scale** — A six-note scale of whole tones only; every chord in it is an augmented triad; no leading note, hence no tonic (Ch 22)
