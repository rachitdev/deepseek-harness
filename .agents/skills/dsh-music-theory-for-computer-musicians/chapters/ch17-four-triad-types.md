# Chapter 17 — Major, Minor, Augmented, and Diminished Triads

## Core Idea
A triad is two stacked thirds; there are two kinds of third (major, 4 semitones; minor, 3
semitones); therefore **there are exactly four triad types**. Major and minor are the "common
triads" that account for six of the seven triads in any major scale and its relative minor. The
other two — **diminished** (two minor thirds) and **augmented** (two major thirds) — are unstable,
rarely used in popular music, and expressively potent when deployed deliberately.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Semitone Rule
"A useful rule of thumb to work out any of these four triads from a given note":
| Triad | Semitone formula | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **Diminished** | 3 plus 3 | minor 3rd + minor 3rd |
| **Minor** | 3 plus 4 | minor 3rd + major 3rd |
| **Major** | 4 plus 3 | major 3rd + minor 3rd |
| **Augmented** | 4 plus 4 | major 3rd + major 3rd |

"Armed with this rule, you can work out all four triads as they occur on any note of the chromatic
scale."

### The Major/Minor Balance of the Seven-Note Scale
"There are three major chords and three minor chords in the scale. The seven-toned scale thus
offers a balance between major and minor influences. **What swings it in favor of the major or
minor influence is emphasis.** The three major chords are the primary chords of the major key,
while the minor chords are secondary. In the minor key, it reverses."

### The Diminished Triad
- **B D F in C major** — the seventh, missing triad. "It occurs when the fifth of the minor triad
  is flattened." Built from two minor thirds: B to D is three semitones, D to F is three semitones.
- **Why it sounds strange:** the root-to-fifth interval B/F is a **diminished fifth** or, inverted,
  the "devil's fourth" — "a dissonant interval, which makes the diminished triad that uses it an
  unstable harmony."
- **Classical function:** as chord VII in the major mode it is the **leading note triad**, built on
  the seventh degree (the leading note), which "rising definitively up to the tonic… helps the ear
  to recognize the tonal centre" (Track 39). "In chord progressions, therefore, the diminished
  triad most often acts as a **substitute for the dominant or as an incomplete dominant seventh
  chord**."
- **Popular-music example:** "used to good effect right at the opening of Madonna's 'Like a Prayer.'"

### Triads in the Harmonic Minor Scale
"The harmonic minor scale also has some oddities you should be aware of. It has only **four common
triads** (major or minor chords), those on **degrees I, IV, V, and VI**. These form the staples as
far as chord progressions in the harmonic minor scale are concerned. It also has **two diminished
triads — on degree II and degree VII**. Because these are unstable, they contribute a sense of
tension and pathos to harmony in this scale."

**Warning:** "Using this tension effectively in modern electronic music requires considerable
skill. This is because it was over-exploited by classical composers to create dramatic impact.
This formula was further worn thin by its overuse in silent-movie music. Suffice it to say, use
these chords with extreme caution; otherwise, they can raise a giggle!"

### The Augmented Triad
- **Built from two major thirds.** "It is like a major triad except the fifth has been sharpened by
  a semitone."
- **Where it occurs:** on **degree III of the harmonic minor scale** and **degrees III and IV of
  the rising melodic minor scale**.
- **Character:** unstable, "owing to the dissonant augmented fifth. This gives it an almost
  otherworldly quality." Rare in popular music, but "it does figure prominently in film scores
  because it can produce some very atmospheric harmonic effects. It is most often used to suggest
  the ethereal quality of outer space or the mystery of the oceanic worlds."
- **Technique:** "These effects are often enhanced through the use of **numerous augmented triads
  a whole tone apart**. This gives a distinct effect of the type of weightlessness that you would
  encounter in outer space. Harmony of this type has evolved into its own separate scale system
  called the **whole tone scale**." (Track 40.)
- **In jazz:** used more often, and "often used as part of a seventh chord (**Cmaj7/5+**), where it
  gives a very atmospheric harmony."

## Key Concepts
- **Common triads** — major and minor together; "most of the chords you use in your music will
  probably be major and minor."
- **The sweet note again** — "Major and minor triads differ only in respect to their 'sweet note' —
  the third."
- **Foundation for extended chords** — the four triad types are what sevenths, ninths, and
  elevenths "tend to use… as their foundation."

## Mental Models
- **Two thirds, two flavors, four chords.** The whole taxonomy falls out of one combinatorial fact.
- **Emphasis makes the mode.** The same six common triads serve both major and minor keys; which
  three you make primary determines the tonality.
- **Instability is an expressive resource, not a defect** — but a heavily worn one. Diminished
  tension reads as silent-movie melodrama if overused; augmented reads as outer space.

## Anti-patterns
- Piling on diminished chords for drama — "use these chords with extreme caution; otherwise, they
  can raise a giggle!"
- Treating the augmented triad as a general-purpose chord; it is atmospheric and situational.
- Trying to build a progression on the Locrian-style diminished tonic — see Chapter 20 for why
  that mode is problematic.

## Reference Tables

### The four triads built on C
| Triad | Notes | Semitone formula | Songbook symbol |
|---|---|---|---|
| C major | C E G | 4 + 3 | **C** |
| C minor | C Eb G | 3 + 4 | **Cm** |
| C diminished | C Eb Gb | 3 + 3 | **C°** (or **Cdim**) |
| C augmented | C E G# | 4 + 4 | **C+** |

### Chord symbol conventions (Hewitt's own summary)
| Triad type | How written | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Major | Capital letter of the root | C, F# |
| Minor | Capital letter + small **m** | Dm, Ebm |
| Augmented | Capital letter + **plus sign** | C+, G+ |
| Diminished | Capital letter + small **o**, or root + **dim** | B°, G#°, Ddim |

### Triad quality by scale and degree
| Scale | I/i | II/ii | III | IV/iv | V/v | VI/vi | VII/vii |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Major (C) | major | minor | minor | major | major | minor | **diminished** |
| Natural minor (A) | minor | **diminished** | major | minor | minor | major | major |
| Harmonic minor | minor | **diminished** | **augmented** | minor | **major** | major | **diminished** |
| Melodic minor (rising) | — | — | **augmented** | **augmented** | — | — | — |

(The harmonic minor's four common triads are on I, IV, V, and VI; its two diminished triads on
II and VII; its augmented triad on III.)

## Worked Example
**Exercise 1a and 1b**, the chapter's own identification drill, with the book supplying case 1 as
the model. Applying the semitone rule to each:

| Chord | Notes | Semitone gaps | Type | Symbol (1b) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (given) | C E G | 4 + 3 | **major triad** | **C** |
| 2 | C Eb G | 3 + 4 | **minor triad** | **Cm** (given) |
| 3 | C Eb Gb | 3 + 3 | **diminished triad** | **C°** |
| 4 | C E G# | 4 + 4 | **augmented triad** | **C+** |

So: **C+ denotes chord 4**, **C° denotes chord 3**, **C denotes chord 1**.

**Exercise 2 — the semitone rule**, answered from the chapter's own list:
- Diminished Triad: **3 plus 3** semitones.
- Minor Triad: **3 plus 4** semitones.
- Major Triad: **4 plus 3** semitones.
- Augmented Triad: **4 plus 4** semitones.

**Working the diminished triad by hand, as Figure 17.4 does:** B → C → C# → D is three semitones;
D → D# → E → F is three semitones. Two minor thirds; total span B to F = six semitones = a
diminished fifth = *diabolus in musica*.

## Key Takeaways
1. Four triads exist because a triad is two thirds and a third is major or minor.
2. The semitone rule: dim 3+3, min 3+4, maj 4+3, aug 4+4 — it works from any chromatic note.
3. Six of the seven scale triads are common (three major, three minor); emphasis decides the tonality.
4. The diminished triad is chord vii in major, the leading note triad, and usually functions as a
   dominant substitute.
5. The augmented triad lives on III of the harmonic minor and III/IV of the rising melodic minor;
   chains of them a whole tone apart give the whole tone scale's weightless effect.
6. Symbols: C, Cm, C+, C° (or Cdim).
7. The harmonic minor has four common triads (I IV V VI), two diminished (II VII), one augmented (III).
