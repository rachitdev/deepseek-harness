# Cheat Sheet — The Design Thinking Toolbox

Dense reference. Durations and group sizes are the book's own printed figures; where a page's timing box did not survive text extraction it is marked *n/p* (not printed / not recoverable).

Phase codes: **U** understand · **O** observe · **D** define point of view · **I** ideate · **P** prototype · **T** test · **R** reflect · **X** cross-phase / implementation & scaling.

## 2. Defaults and thresholds

| Thing | Default | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Team size | **3–7; five is ideal**; over 10 → split into teams of teams | Ch 1 |
| Space | **≥15 m² per group of four**; work standing | Ch 1 |
| Facilitator preparation | **As long as the workshop itself** | Ch 1 |
| Brainstorming session | **5–15 min**, then change the stimulus | Ch 6 |
| Ideas per Post-it | **One** | Ch 6 |
| 6-3-5 arithmetic | **6 people × 3 ideas × 6 rounds = up to 108 ideas in <30 min**; 3–5 min per round | Ch 6 |
| Dot voting | **3–5 dots per person**, private, simultaneous, boss last | Ch 6 |
| 2x2 pacing | **30–60 seconds per idea**; take the **top 3** from the top-right quadrant | Ch 6 |
| Detailed idea rating scale | **0 (do not implement) – 10 (definitely implement)** | Ch 6 |
| Functions per lo-fi prototype | **One or two** | Ch 7 |
| Prototype timing signal | **If you aren't ashamed of it, you invested too much time** | Ch 7 |
| Feedback capture grid printout | **A3**, one per tester per interview; 2–3 tests minimum | Ch 8 |
| Experience test sample | **≥5 users × 15 min each** | Ch 8 |
| Solution interview | **30 min** is the orientation; knowledge gain is the real criterion | Ch 8 |
| Usability task rating | **success / struggle / fail** | Ch 8 |
| A/B test | **One assumption; two variants differing in one property** | Ch 8 |
| IL/IW/IW Post-its | **At least 3 per participant**, read aloud before sticking | Ch 9 |
| Lessons learned after a bad project | **Wait ~6 weeks** | Ch 9 |
| Lean canvas density | **3–4 Post-its per field**, one statement each | Ch 9 |
| Lean canvas order | **Iterate blocks 1–5 first** (problem, segments, UVP, solution, existing alternatives) | Ch 9 |
| Lean canvas risk colours | **pink = high risk, test now · yellow = medium · green = tested/low** | Ch 9 |
| Pitch | **≤10 slides**; Kawasaki **10/20/30** (10 slides, 20 min, font 30); repeat **2–3** key messages | Ch 9 |
| Funnel project size brackets | **< 5 m · 5–50 m · > 50 m** (adapt to the org's value system) | Ch 9 |
| Funnel update cadence | **Monthly**, 30–60 min | Ch 9 |
| Kickbox budgets | **RedBox** 20% time / 2 months · **BlueBox** CHF 10–30k / 4–6 months · **GoldBox** CHF 100–500k, full time | Ch 9 |

## 3. Decision tables

### Which prototype next?

| You need to know | Build | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Whether one experience or function is even real | CEP / CFP | Very low |
| Whether a radical assumption survives contact | Dark horse (Wizard of Oz, video) | Very low |
| What combinations are possible | Funky (morphological box) | Low |
| How to get the team out of the groan zone | Vision prototype (one sentence) | Very low |
| Whether the main function works technically | Functional (system) prototype | Medium |
| What the essential sub-functions cost | "X is finished" | Med-high |
| Whether you have problem/solution fit | Final prototype | High |

### Which test tool?

| Situation | Use | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Any test at all — plan and document it | Testing sheet | 213 |
| Quick structured capture of reactions to a lo-fi prototype | Feedback capture grid | 217 |
| You need the right questions for this resolution | Powerful questions in experience testing | 221 |
| Late phase; does the user accept it, and at what price? | Solution interview | 225 |
| Is it operable — and can variants be compared fairly? | Structured usability testing | 229 |
| Users give vague feedback on one prototype | A/B testing (two variants, one difference) | 233 |

### Which reflection tool?

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Feedback in the moment, sensitive project or hierarchy present | I like, I wish, I wonder |
| End of an iteration; process and collaboration | Retrospective sailboat |
| End of project (or milestone); learning to carry forward | Lessons learned (project + meta level) |
| Team knows each other well and wants to skip it | Do it anyway — "That's a pity" |
| Mood is bad right after the project | Postpone ~6 weeks |
| A major retro, or hierarchical tension in the room | External moderator + anonymity + "Las Vegas principle" |

### Which selection tool?

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Many ideas, need quick prioritization by two criteria | 2x2 matrix (SMART axes) |
| Group >8, or ideas already clustered | Dot voting |
| Tie after a vote | Second vote on the winners, or move to a 2x2 |
| Very early phase, ideas still wild | 2x2 with "cool" × "doable" |
| Near problem/solution fit | 2x2 with impact × implementation expenditure |
| Later phases with investors in mind | Quantify as a cost/yield chart |

### Lean canvas or business model canvas?

| Condition | Choose |
|---|---|
| Still validating the solution as an MVP; outside-in view needed | **Lean canvas** (start here) |
| Lean canvas is valid and stable; cost structure now dominates | **Business model canvas** |

## 4. Tells and smells — symptom → diagnosis → fix

| Symptom | Diagnosis | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Ideation produces the same ideas over and over | Stimulus exhausted; group in circles | Switch to special brainstorming (negative / figuring / bodystorming) or analogies |
| Nobody but the loudest three people has spoken | Hierarchy or extroversion dominating | Move to 6-3-5 (silent) or structured brainstorming; in voting, boss last |
| Everyone's dots landed on one idea | Bandwagon effect | Vote privately, place simultaneously; run a second round on the winners |
| Team argues about whether a hypothesis passed | No verification criteria were set before the test | Define "criteria for a thesis to be considered verified" at plan time |
| Test feedback is polite and vague | You asked for a verdict on one artifact, or you sold the idea | A/B two variants; or stop explaining and let them think aloud |
| Users say it's great but nobody would buy | Resolution too high for honest desirability feedback, or wrong question set | Drop resolution; use the lo-fi/med-fi question set; solution interview for pricing |
| Testers can't complete a task and you keep helping | Moderator is invested / rescuing | Uninvolved moderator; "ask what should happen next" instead of helping |
| Prototype is beautiful and slow to change | Invested too much time; resolution crept up | If you're not embarrassed, you've overinvested — rebuild smaller |
| Team can't let go of a prototype | Fallen in love | "Love is for people, not for prototypes" — let the customer say it, in a solution interview |
| Findings can't be reconstructed a week later | Documentation gap | Quotes verbatim, photos, short videos; photograph every Post-it wall |
| One lean canvas field is crammed | Lack of focus | Reduce to 3–4 Post-its, one statement each |
| One lean canvas field is empty | Unworked area | Work on it before proceeding |
| Everything on the canvas is green | Only easy assumptions tested | Colour-code by risk; test the pink ones first |
| Road map can't name all the implementers | The goal is not clear yet | Re-run the "who" questions before continuing |
| Retro produces complaints, not changes | Stopped at symptoms | Dig for causes; end with concrete, documented measures |
| Feedback session sours the mood | Recipient argued back; criticism went personal | Restrict to I like / I wish / I wonder; recipient says thank you only |
| Innovation portfolio is all incremental | Funnel is fed only by extrapolation | Add retropolation from a chosen future scenario; add "pictures of the future" |
| Nobody knows why an initiative died | Discontinued projects removed from the funnel | Keep them on the board; review causes in a retrospective |
| Pitch runs long and lands flat | Text slides, no story, too many facts | Story entry, ≤10 slides, show the prototype, repeat 2–3 messages |
| Workshop was rigid and went badly | Detailed inflexible agenda + wrong tools at the wrong time | Adapt tools to setting and participants; keep a spare warm-up and alternative tool ready |
| Empty quadrant on the 2x2 and nobody noticed | Reading the matrix only for winners | Empty quadrants "signify potential for further opportunities and unfulfilled needs" |
| Great ideas keep getting killed mid-session | Criticism during generation | Enforce no-criticism; park off-problem ideas instead of discarding |

## 5. Pipeline templates

### The micro-cycle, with default entry tools

```
UNDERSTAND  → Problem statement (49) · Interview for empathy (57) · 5W+H (71) · Extreme users (79)
OBSERVE     → AEIOU (107) · Persona (97) · Customer journey (103) · Trend analysis (119)
DEFINE POV  → Context mapping (133) · Storytelling (129) · Vision cone (141) · HMW (125) · Critical items (145)
IDEATE      → Brainstorming (151) · 6-3-5 (163) · Analogies (171) → 2x2 (155) · Dot voting (159) → NABC (177)
PROTOTYPE   → CEP/CFP (188) → Dark horse (189) → Funky (190) → [GROAN ZONE: Vision (191)]
              → Functional (192) → "X is finished" (193) → Final (194) · MVP (207)
TEST        → Testing sheet (213) · Feedback capture grid (217) · Powerful questions (221)
              · Usability test (229) · A/B (233) · Solution interview (225)
REFLECT     → I like/I wish/I wonder (239) · Retrospective sailboat (243) · Lessons learned (255)
```
Problem space = Understand · Observe · Define POV. Solution space = Ideate · Prototype · Test.

### The macro-cycle / growth path

```
Problem identification  ──(extrapolation + retropolation)──►  Problem/customer fit
        │
        ▼
Problem/solution fit  [final prototype · lean canvas]
        │
        ▼
Test & validate  [solution interview · MVP]
        │
        ▼
Product/market fit  [MVP metrics · A/B]
        │
        ▼
Business ecosystem design  [MVE]
        │
        ▼
Scale  [innovation funnel · road map for implementation · GoldBox]
```

### Test session template (from the testing sheet)

```
1  PLAN     Location (ideally the user's premises)
            Test criteria — what counts as verified?
            Sequence · roles: who asks / who documents / who observes
            Key questions (use the resolution-matched set, p. 222)
2  RUN      Tester sees and experiences the prototype FIRST
            Think aloud · no explaining · no leading questions
            Ask "Why?" repeatedly · capture verbatim quotes
3  DOCUMENT Photos, short videos (permission first; data use agreement if reused)
            Summarize findings and learnings
4  CLUSTER  Collect all grids · group by answer and by persona · new point of view
```

### Retrospective template (sailboat)

```
1  OPEN      Objectives and sequence
2  COLLECT   WIND   – what pushed us forward
             ANCHOR – what slowed us down
             CLIFFS – risks we cannot control (market, tech, competitors)
             ISLAND – our shared vision and motivation
             (everyone reads their Post-its aloud)
3  PRIORITIZE Cluster · dig for CAUSES, not symptoms · surface the unpleasant issues
4  MEASURES  Document precisely what will change or be tried next iteration
5  CLOSE     Brief feedback on the retro itself · part with good feelings
```
*Alternative board:* continue the same way / do differently next time / not relevant / want to try out.

### Pitch template

```
ENTRY (STORY)   Attract attention
PROBLEM         Greatest problem · why it is a problem · who is affected
CUSTOMERS       For whom · extent · early adopters · co-creation partners
SOLUTION/IDEA   Prototype/demo + test feedback · value proposition · why only we
BUSINESS MODEL  How we make money · challenges and risks
NEXT STEPS      What next · what we need · why worthwhile, why now
SUMMARY         Repeat 2–3 key messages · introduce the team (external pitches)
```
Rules: KISS · ≤10 slides (Kawasaki 10/20/30) · figures > words · pictures > words · videos > pictures · prototype > slides · practise it.

### Lean canvas fill order

```
① Problem (+ existing alternatives)      ② Customer segments (+ early adopters)
③ Unique value proposition (+ high-level concept)
④ Solution                               ⑤ Channels
        ↑ iterate ①–⑤ until stable = problem/solution fit ↑
⑥ Revenue streams   ⑦ Cost structure   ⑧ Key figures (AARRR)   ⑨ Unfair advantage
→ then: mark the riskiest assumptions and test them in experiments
```

### Digital transformation road map

```
1 Digital reality      existing business model · value chains · actors · customer requirements
2 Digital ambition     goals in time / finance / space, prioritized
3 Digital potentials   best practices · enablers · own options
4 Digital fit          evaluate on business-model fit & customer requirements
                       (+ time, budget, feasibility, competitors, AI, culture)
5 Digital implementation  customer interactions · partners · consequences for
                       processes, resources, skills, IT, contracts, group structure
```

### Kickbox gates

```
RedBox   (U · D · I)   20% time / 2 months   small budget + expert access
                       → pitch to sponsors
BlueBox  (P · T)       >20% time / 4–6 months   CHF 10,000–30,000
                       → prototype + experiment with real customers
GoldBox  (implement)   100% full time         CHF 100,000–500,000
                       → company, spin-off, joint venture, or internal department
Scoring: kickbox scoreboard, lean start-up logic. Idea owner stays as "CEO" throughout.
```

## 6. The book's own closing rules

1. Establish a positive feedback culture and do not criticize ideas prematurely.
2. Build on the ideas of the others.
3. Fail early, fail often, and improve prototypes gradually.
4. Listen and hear others out.
5. Visualize and build physical prototypes.
6. Leave many ideas to the diverging phase.
7. Place the user/customer at the center of considerations.
8. **Do it and don't just talk about it — design thinking lives from the application.**
