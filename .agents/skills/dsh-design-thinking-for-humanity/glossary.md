# Glossary — Design Thinking for Humanity

Terms as the book uses them. Chapter references point to `chapters/chNN-*.md`.

**9 Rs of circular strategies** — The circularity ladder from Refuse and Reduce through Resell/reuse, Repair/refurbish, Remanufacture, Repurpose, Recycle materials, Recover energy, to Re-mine, grouped into three loops (Ch 9)
**Adaptive Strategy** — A strategy designed to evolve with a dynamic system rather than a rigid plan that breaks when conditions change; tool 7 of the systems toolbox (Ch 12)
**AGI (artificial general intelligence)** — Systems "capable of human-level reasoning, learning, and generalized problem-solving across vast domains"; the technology at the center of the book's second-order impact analysis (Ch 13, Ch 14)
**Anticipatory resilience** — The advantage futures thinking contributes: stress-testing a concept against scenarios to find no-regret moves (Ch 13)
**Archetype (system)** — A recurring problematic structure, e.g. Tragedy of the Commons, Shifting the Burden, Fixes That Fail; recognizing one avoids "reinventing the diagnostic wheel" (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Backcasting** — Working backward from a defined future state to the steps required to reach it (Ch 6)
**Balancing loop (B)** — A feedback loop that seeks stability or a goal; has an odd number of negative links (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Beginner's mind (Shoshin)** — Approaching a problem without the filter of prior expertise (Ch 7)
**Beneficiaries** — Ecosystem role: "the users, communities, and non-human stakeholders who receive the value" (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Biomimicry** — "The conscious emulation of nature's genius," at the level of form, process, or ecosystem (Ch 9, Ch 10)
**Biomimetic intelligence** — AI algorithms modeled on biological systems (e.g. swarm intelligence) to optimize decentralized resource distribution (Ch 13)
**Black swan** — An unforeseeable, high-impact disruption; the Impact Checklist asks whether strategy survives one (Ch 4, Ch 5)
**Boundary (system)** — A deliberately chosen scope for analysis: "broad enough to capture root cause, narrow enough to be actionable" (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Butterfly effect** — Small initial differences producing large divergences in outcome (Ch 5)
**Carbon Harvest Platform** — The digital orchestrator designed across the book's five-part case study: verifies regenerative practice data and issues tradable regeneration credits (Ch 7–15)
**Catalytic Entrepreneur** — One of the book's leadership roles, driving change bottom-up (Ch 3)
**Causal Layered Analysis (CLA)** — Four-layer deconstruction of an issue: litany, systemic cause, worldview, metaphor/myth (Ch 6)
**Causal Loop Diagramming (CLD)** — "The classic tool for visualizing the underlying feedback loops that drive a system's behavior over time" (Ch 12)
**Circular design / circular economy** — Designing so that waste is eliminated by intention rather than managed after the fact (Ch 9, Ch 10)
**Close the feedback loop** — A leverage-point move: deliver impact data back to the actors insulated from their own consequences (Ch 12)
**Collective Impact Canvas** — Five-element operational framework for a multi-stakeholder collaboration: common agenda, shared metrics, mutually reinforcing activities, communication, backbone support (Ch 12)
**Costs block** — The Resilience Blueprint block holding financial, resource, and externality costs, each with a stated mitigation (Ch 15)
**Creative Mash-Up** — Forcing the combination of a present-day human need with a speculative future technology or trend (Ch 5, Ch 6)
**Critical thinking** — "The paramount human capability" and final gate of the Exponential Innovation Cycle; its most important output is the ability to say no (Ch 13)
**Data justice** — The case study's ethical guardrail: farmers own their data and the platform is governed as a cooperative to prevent extraction by tech giants (Ch 13, Ch 15)
**Decay** — System response to shock: "the system collapses"; lose–lose (Ch 3)
**Deception of False Scarcity** — The book's re-aiming of the Six Ds' deceptive phase: use the quiet growth stage to co-design with marginalized communities (Ch 14)
**Decoupling Test** — Ecosystem stress test: "Is the financial success of this ecosystem explicitly contingent upon the delivery of ecological value?" (Ch 12)
**Dematerialization of Harm and Waste** — Six Ds phase 4, re-aimed: design out waste, energy use, and unintended consequences at the systemic level (Ch 14)
**Democratization of Power and Co-creation** — Six Ds phase 6, re-aimed: users hold ownership and influence over the technology's evolution and governance (Ch 14)
**Demonetization of Money, Access, and Information** — Six Ds phase 5, re-aimed: make essential resources freely or near-freely available to underserved populations (Ch 14)
**Design for Durability, Repair & Disassembly** — Product-level circularity tool: modularity, spare parts, mono-materials (Ch 10)
**Design Thinking** — Lens 2: "Empathizing with people to deeply understand needs, challenges and desires" (Ch 3, Ch 7)
**Digital twin** — "Virtual replicas of physical systems, cities, or ecosystems," used to compress time, inject shocks, and expose second-order effects before deployment (Ch 13)
**Digitization for System Clarity** — Six Ds phase 1, re-aimed: digitize systemic data to achieve radical transparency of total planetary and social cost (Ch 14)
**Disruption of Degenerative Systems** — Six Ds phase 3, re-aimed: disrupt the degenerative system itself, not merely an industry (Ch 14)
**Double Diamond** — The macro process: problem space (solving the right problem) then solution space (solving the problem right) (Ch 2)
**Doughnut Economics Model** — Framing between a social foundation and an ecological ceiling — "the safe and just space for humanity" (Ch 9, Ch 10)
**Ecological ceiling** — The outer planetary boundary a solution must not exceed (Ch 9, Ch 11)
**Ecological Thinking** — Lens 3: "Recognizing the interdependence of humans and nature… minimizing negative impacts and creating regenerative outcomes"; also the outer, non-negotiable context for all other lenses (Ch 3, Ch 9)
**Elegant reduction** — Principle 4: "Resist the urge to solve problems by adding layers of complexity" (Ch 3)
**Embedded Ecological Thinking Cycle** — The ecological lens's process model (Ch 9)
**Enablers** — Ecosystem role: policymakers, investors, research institutions providing essential support (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Epistemic integrity** — The transparency standard demanded of regenerative AI, in contrast to opaque "black box" systems (Ch 13)
**Ethical Consequences Scanner** — Design toolbox instrument for surfacing unintended consequence paths (Ch 8)
**Ethical pause / friction point** — A mandatory stop in the design process "to evaluate the output not for technical bugs, but for systemic bias and potential harm" (Ch 13)
**Exponential Empathy Mapping** — Scaling empathy from one persona (1x) to community (100x) to society (1,000,000x) (Ch 14)
**Exponential Ethics Compass** — 2×2 framework (individual↔society × intended↔unintended) for evaluating a solution's ethics before it scales, ending in mitigation strategies (Ch 14)
**Exponential Innovation Cycle** — Four steps: Frame & Align (human led) → Expand & Generate (AI led) → Simulate & Validate (hybrid) → Curate & Refine (human led) (Ch 13)
**Exponential Thinking** — Lens 5: "A nonlinear mindset leveraging accelerating technologies for rapid, global, restorative impact" (Ch 3, Ch 13)
**Exponential Trends Scanning** — A specialized Horizon Scanning that hunts only for trends whose performance doubles or costs halve at a consistent rate (Ch 14)
**Exponentializing** — System response to shock: "the system evolves," bouncing forward stronger, more equitable, more vibrant; win–win–win (Ch 3)
**Experiential Futures** — Making a future tangible enough to be felt rather than described (Ch 6)
**Finite game** — A game with clear endpoint, defined rules, and winners; contrasted with the infinite game (Ch 1, Ch 11)
**Fixes That Fail** — System archetype: a short-term solution creates a worse long-term unintended consequence (Ch 12)
**Flow** — "The actions, decisions, or forces that increase (inflows) or decrease (outflows) a stock… the primary area where interventions are applied" (Ch 11)
**Focus Filter** — MTP function: "Does this get us closer to achieving our MTP?" If no, reconsider regardless of profit (Ch 13, Ch 14)
**Future Headlines** — Write a news headline from a desired future, then work backward to what must exist for it to be true (Ch 6)
**Future Persona** — A persona situated in a specific future scenario rather than the present (Ch 8)
**Future space** — The preliminary phase added ahead of the Double Diamond, guided by futures thinking (Ch 3)
**Future-Forward Design Process** — The five-phase futures method: immersion & empathy, future framing, future-informed ideation, future-fit prototyping, strategic navigation (Ch 5, Ch 6)
**Futures Thinking** — Lens 1: "Anticipating and exploring multiple plausible futures to inform present-day decisions and design" (Ch 3, Ch 5)
**Futures Wheel** — Structured brainstorm of cascading ripple effects: central event → first-, second-, third-order consequences (Ch 6)
**Green labelling** — Anti-pattern: "merely slapping 'green' or 'responsible' labels on a product" (Ch 3, Ch 4, Ch 9)
**Handprint** — The positive contribution a solution makes, as opposed to the footprint it reduces (Ch 9)
**Horizon Scanning** — Structured search for signals of change across domains, typically via PESTLE (Ch 6)
**Hotspot (LCA)** — The lifecycle stage with the largest impact; "the hotspot is the brief for redesign" (Ch 10)
**How Might We (HMW)** — The reframing question after synthesis; must aim at a leverage point, not a symptom (Ch 2, Ch 4, Ch 8)
**Impact Checklist** — Fifteen diagnostic questions, three per lens, scored yes / partly / not yet; "a strategic compass," not an audit (Ch 4)
**Infinite game** — Play with no endpoint, evolving rules and players; "the goal is not to be the best *in* the system but to be the best *for* the system" (Ch 1, Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Just transition** — Designing explicit support for individuals and communities negatively affected by a systemic shift (Ch 12)
**Ladder of Inference** — Model showing how teams move from observable data to conclusions, surfacing hidden assumptions (Ch 6)
**Leverage point** — "Strategic places in a system where a small, well-focused intervention can produce a significant and lasting positive ripple effect"; the most powerful sit in rules, information flows, and mindset (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Leverage Points Analysis** — Meadows-based hierarchy ranking candidate interventions from parameters (weakest) to paradigm (strongest) (Ch 12)
**Life Cycle Assessment (LCA), Simplified** — Cradle-to-grave footprint mapping to find redesign hotspots (Ch 10)
**Linear empathy** — Design thinking's default failure mode: empathy that optimizes for the immediate user and misses systemic consequences at scale (Ch 1)
**Litmus Test (Design Thinking for Humanity)** — Veto questions at the ethical pause: does this centralize or democratize power; does it extract or regenerate? (Ch 8, Ch 13)
**Living Capitals** — The regenerative business model's stock of natural, social and human capital (Ch 9)
**Living Soil Mandate** — The case study's MTP: "To make the regeneration of the world's topsoil the primary measure of agricultural success by 2050" (Ch 5–15)
**Massive Transformative Purpose (MTP)** — "A short, memorable, and highly aspirational statement that defines the purpose of an organization or project"; tested as massive / transformational / purpose-driven / resilient (Ch 13, Ch 14)
**Meadows, Donella** — Systems thinker whose hierarchy of intervention points underpins Leverage Points Analysis (Ch 12)
**Megatrend** — A large-scale, slow-moving force shaping the operating environment (Ch 5)
**Minimum Viable Ecosystem (MVE)** — "The smallest viable network of partners, resources, and governance structures required to deliver the core regenerative value proposition" (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Minimum Viable Transition (MVT)** — The first small, high-leverage political or pilot win that proves a transition is beginning (Ch 12)
**Movement Catalyst Design** — Framework for engineering a movement: core narrative and identity, low-friction onboarding, engineered viral loop (Ch 14)
**Multi-stakeholder Value Network Mapping** — Mapping financial, social, ecological and knowledge value flows across an ecosystem (Ch 12)
**Multivalue flows** — The financial, social and ecological exchanges an ecosystem must make explicit (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Narrative Design Canvas** — Three-column, nine-prompt canvas for building the story of a change (Ch 7, Ch 12)
**Net Water Balance** — One of the case study's regenerative metrics (Ch 9, Ch 15)
**No-regret move** — An action worth taking regardless of which scenario unfolds — e.g. low-cost soil sensors (Ch 5, Ch 13)
**Non-human stakeholder** — Soil health, watersheds, biodiversity — mapped as primary, non-negotiable stakeholders, each assigned a human steward (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Object from the Future** — A prototyped artifact from a specific future, such as a food label from a plastic-free world (Ch 6)
**OODA loop (Transition)** — Observe (the radar) → Orient (the system check) → Decide (the pivot) → Act (the experiment); Step 5 of the Transition Strategy Canvas (Ch 12)
**Orchestrator** — "The central entity that facilitates and governs the ecosystem"; must act as a system steward, not a commander (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Orchestrator's Ego Test** — "If the central Orchestrator disappears tomorrow, does the ecosystem collapse?" (Ch 12)
**Paradigm / mindset** — Leverage point 1, the most powerful: "shifting the underlying worldview" (Ch 12)
**PESTLE** — Macro-environment scan across political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental domains (Ch 6, Ch 12)
**Planetary boundaries** — The nine limits within which a solution must operate (Ch 9)
**Planetary Steward** — The identity the case study restores to the farmer; also a leadership posture (Ch 9, Ch 15)
**Plausible / possible / preferable / probable / preposterous futures** — The bands of the Vision Cone (Ch 5)
**Precision restoration** — "The shift from broad environmental goals to hyper-targeted, measurable action," e.g. the Google Tree Canopy project (Ch 13)
**Preferable future** — The 20+ year future the strategy is reverse-engineered from (Ch 3, Ch 5, Ch 15)
**Problem Framing Funnel** — The design lens's narrowing from broad context to a framed wicked problem (Ch 7)
**Radical Collaboration** — I Can Do / AI Can Do / We Can Do — the division of labor between human and machine (Ch 7)
**Regeneration credits** — The case study's tradable, verified unit of ecological improvement (carbon, water, biodiversity) (Ch 11, Ch 15)
**Regenerative AI** — AI "designed to restore ecological and social health," with explicit social and ecological alignment mandated by governance (Ch 13)
**Regenerative Business Model Canvas** — Canvas embedding regenerative principles in the core value logic; net-positive impact and living capitals (Ch 9, Ch 10)
**Regenerative Ecological Metrics** — Metrics that make ecological value countable, so it can be designed for (Ch 9)
**Reinforcing loop (R)** — A feedback loop that amplifies change; even number of negative links; creates exponential growth or collapse (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Resilience Blueprint** — "The ultimate strategic tool," a nine-block canvas integrating all five lenses into one living document (Ch 3, Ch 14, Ch 15)
**Resilience Leader** — The enterprise/startup leadership role: builds robust systems and adaptable teams, fosters decentralized decision-making (Ch 3)
**Resilient** — System response to shock: "the system recovers," returning to its original — possibly still vulnerable — state (Ch 3)
**Responsible scaling** — Naming the unintended negative consequences of an ecosystem at scale and the non-negotiable ethical principles guiding growth (Ch 11, Ch 15)
**Robust** — System response to shock: "the system resists" without breaking, but stays static and protects the status quo (Ch 3)
**Safe Harbor Contract** — The case study's transition financial product: floor price plus yield insurance, bridging the "valley of death" (Ch 11, Ch 15)
**Scenario Planning / 2×2 Matrix** — Turning two critical uncertainties into four distinct, plausible narratives (Ch 6)
**Second-Order Impact Analysis** — Three-ring chain-reaction analysis of one exponential technology, driven by "If this happens, then what happens?" (Ch 14)
**Shifting the Burden** — System archetype: "a quick, symptomatic fix distracts from the deeper systemic cause, worsening the problem over time" (Ch 11)
**Signpost** — An observable marker that a particular scenario is materializing (Ch 6)
**Silent Stakeholder Test** — "If the soil (or the watershed or the forest) had a seat on the board, would they vote to approve this business model?" (Ch 12)
**Six Ds of Exponentials (Adjusted)** — Digitization, Deception, Disruption, Dematerialization, Demonetization, Democratization — each re-aimed toward regenerative outcomes (Ch 13, Ch 14)
**Social foundation** — The inner boundary of the Doughnut: the minimum every person is entitled to (Ch 9)
**Soil Health Score** — A case study metric, and the score the transition strategy works to get recognized by crop insurance programs (Ch 11, Ch 15)
**Spotlight → Floodlight** — The book's core reframe: standard human-centered design spotlights the user and leaves the environment dark; this framework floods the whole ecosystem with light (Ch 1)
**Stock** — "The accumulated variable, representing the memory of the system that changes slowly over time" — e.g. soil organic carbon, community trust (Ch 11)
**Success as a Threat Test** — "If this solution scales 100x next year, what new systemic problem does it accidentally create?" (Ch 12)
**Sustainable Behavior Design Canvas** — Nudges, social norms and feedback loops making the sustainable choice easy, attractive and socially rewarding (Ch 10)
**System Boundaries & Stakeholder Mapping** — The first tool of systemic analysis; defines scope and inventories human, non-human, abstract and institutional actors (Ch 12)
**System steward** — The orchestrator's true role: "to maintain the health of the entire ecosystem… not to command and control" (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Systemic Empathy Map** — Empathy map extended from a person to a system: what the system says, does, thinks, feels (Ch 8)
**Systems Steward** — The government/policy leadership role: foresight-guided policy, lean multi-level governance networks (Ch 3)
**Systems Thinking** — Lens 4: "Analyzing complex problems within their broader context to identify underlying causes and leverage interconnected relationships" (Ch 3, Ch 11)
**Tragedy of the Commons** — System archetype: "individual rational actions collectively deplete a shared resource" (Ch 11)
**Transition design** — "The crucial 'missing middle' between understanding a problem and achieving a preferable future"; requires a portfolio of interventions over a multigenerational timeframe (Ch 11)
**Transition Strategy Canvas** — Four quadrants — power & politics, economics & incentives, narrative & persuasion, pathways to legitimacy — plus the OODA extension (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Unlikely allies** — Stakeholders who appear opposed to a change but hold a hidden long-term interest in it (Ch 12)
**Valley of death** — "The three-year transition period where yields often drop before soil health recovers," which current bank loans do not support (Ch 11, Ch 15)
**Value circulation** — Designing so the output of one stakeholder becomes a valuable input for another (Ch 12)
**Value Proposition for Humanity Canvas** — Value proposition extended across people, society, and planet (Ch 8)
**Viral loop** — The designed feedback cycle in which each participant's action creates new awareness and recruits new members (Ch 13, Ch 14)
**Vision Cone** — The futures diagram spanning projected, probable, plausible, possible, preferable and preposterous futures (Ch 5)
**Voice of the system** — The practice of assigning a team member as steward and advocate for each non-human or future stakeholder (Ch 12)
**Wicked problem** — Interconnected, contested, with no stopping rule; the class of problem the five lenses exist to attack (Ch 1, Ch 7)
**Wicked Problem Framing** — The canvas that articulates the core challenge before boundaries are set (Ch 8, Ch 12)
**Wind Tunneling** — Testing a strategy against multiple scenarios to see where it breaks (Ch 6)
**Win-Win-Win** — The target state where society, environment and economy thrive together "rather than at each other's expense" (Ch 1, Ch 11)
**"Win-Win-Win" Business Ecosystem Canvas** — Six-block synthesis canvas: purpose, vision, actors, multivalue flows, governance & adoption, MVE — plus responsible scaling (Ch 11, Ch 12)
**Worthy rival** — Another actor working the same wicked problem, whose role is to push you to improve rather than to be defeated (Ch 12)
**Yield Maximization Complex** — The case study's named systemic challenge: the degenerative cycle of debt, chemical dependency, and volatile commodity markets (Ch 11, Ch 15)
