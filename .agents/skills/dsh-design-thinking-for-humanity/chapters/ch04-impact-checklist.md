# Chapter 4 — The Impact Checklist: Your Team's Strategic Compass

*Pages 50–53. Fifteen diagnostic questions, three per lens, scored yes / partly / not yet.*

## Core Idea

Before any tool is picked up, a team runs a fast diagnostic. The checklist "is designed to guide practitioners in developing solutions that are not only human-centered but also future-proof, ecologically sound, systemically informed, and capable of generating exponential impact." It is explicitly **not exhaustive** — it is "a strategic compass" and "a tool for reflection, prompting the critical conversations necessary to move from abstract theory to concrete, responsible action."

Its output is not a score but a map of weakness: "you will begin to see where your efforts are strong and, more importantly, where they need to be strengthened … identify the areas that require more attention, deeper empathy, and greater creativity."

## Frameworks Introduced

**The 5×3 checklist structure.** One block per lens, each with a stated objective, a page range pointing at the corresponding toolbox, and three questions. Each question is answered on a three-point scale: **yes / partly / not yet**. "Partly" and "not yet" are the useful answers — they route the team to a specific toolbox.

## Key Concepts

- **Strategic compass, not audit** — the checklist prompts conversation; it does not certify a project.
- **First orientation** — it is "the first step in a continuous process of inquiry and adaptation."
- **Routing device** — every block cites the pages of its lens and toolbox, so a "not yet" is an instruction about which chapter to open next.
- **Leverage point vs symptom** — the design-thinking block makes this the test of a How Might We question.
- **"Less harm" vs regeneration** — the ecological block makes this the pass/fail line.

## Mental Models

- **Diagnose before designing.** A quick honest scan beats a thorough plan aimed at the wrong weakness.
- **A "partly" is a backlog item.** Each weak answer names the work: more attention, deeper empathy, or greater creativity.
- **Ask the whole checklist, not your favorite lens.** Teams over-answer the lens they are already good at; the compass exists to surface the neglected ones.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating the checklist as a compliance gate to be passed once.
- Answering "yes" on the basis of intent rather than evidence in the artifact.
- Skipping the exponential block because the project "isn't a tech project" — the block is about scale-matching and ethical guardrails, not about technology adoption.

## Worked Example — The full Impact Checklist, reproduced

**Futures Thinking** — *Objective: envision and prepare for multiple possible futures to ensure long-term relevance and resilience of solutions (see pages 55–88).*
1. Have you defined a compelling "preferable future" that is ambitious enough to inspire a movement? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet
2. Have you mapped the potential unintended consequences of your solution for the next generation? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet
3. Is your strategy resilient enough to thrive in a world of unexpected shocks and "black swan" events? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet

**Design Thinking** — *Objective: apply the design thinking mindset to deeply understand the problem needs, generate creative solutions, and iterate based on real-world feedback (see pages 121–164).*
4. Have you moved beyond a single user's needs to build deep, systemic empathy for the entire community and the planet? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet
5. Is your "How Might We" question aimed at a true leverage point, or are you still just addressing a surface-level symptom? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet
6. Are you actively prototyping and testing not just your solution but your core, underlying assumptions about the problem itself? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet

**Ecological Thinking** — *Objective: understand and respect the interconnectedness of our solutions with natural systems, aiming for regenerative and sustainable outcomes (see pages 167–216).*
7. Does your solution actively regenerate the ecosystems it touches, or does it merely do "less harm"? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet
8. Have you designed your business model for circularity, eliminating the concept of "waste" from the beginning? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet
9. Does your narrative inspire a genuine shift toward more sustainable behaviors, or does it just label the old way "green"? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet

**Systems Thinking** — *Objective: analyze and understand complex interdependencies within the problem space to identify high-leverage intervention points (see pages 219–261).*
10. Does your business model create a clear "win-win-win," where a healthier planet and a more equitable society are direct drivers of your success? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet
11. Have you designed the collaborative ecosystem of partners required to bring your systemic solution to life? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet
12. Have you considered how your solution will shift power dynamics, and have you designed for equity and fairness from the start? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet

**Exponential Thinking** — *Objective: develop solutions that are scalable, transformative, and capable of generating disproportionately large positive impact (see pages 263–300).*
13. Is your solution designed to leverage exponential trends to create impact at a scale that truly matches the size of the challenge? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet
14. Have you established clear ethical guardrails to ensure your solution is scaled responsibly and serves humanity's best interests? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet
15. Is your purpose framed as a "Massive Transformative Purpose" that is powerful enough to unite and mobilize a global community? ☐ yes ☐ partly ☐ not yet

## Key Takeaways

1. Fifteen questions, five lenses, three answers each — run it in under an hour.
2. Its purpose is routing and conversation, not certification.
3. Three questions encode the book's hardest standards: leverage point (not symptom), regeneration (not "less harm"), scale matched to the size of the problem with ethical guardrails.
4. Re-run it at each major milestone; answers should migrate from "not yet" to "yes" as artifacts accumulate.
