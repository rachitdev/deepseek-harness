# Chapter 6 — Futures Thinking Toolbox

*Pages 77–119. Nineteen tools, organized by the five phases of the Future-Forward Design Process, closing with the Futures Thinking Strategic Grid.*

## Core Idea

Each tool page follows one template: **"I would like to…"** (the intent), **"What you can do with the tool"** (five outcomes), **"How this tool is applied"** (numbered steps), plus **Expert Tips** and cross-references to the tools that feed it and consume its output. The toolbox is a pipeline, not a menu: Horizon Scanning and PESTLE produce signals → Probability/Impact prioritizes them → Scenario Planning turns the critical uncertainties into axes → Wind Tunneling stress-tests strategy against those scenarios → Backcasting and Future-Ready Roadmapping return to today.

## Reference Table — The nineteen tools by phase

**Phase 1 — Futures Immersion & Empathy**

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Horizon Scanning** | "A structured process for looking beyond the immediate and familiar to identify potential future developments," actively searching for signals of change across domains, typically using PESTLE. Steps: define scope and domains → gather signals → document and assess (signal cards; *Is this signal novel? Does it have the potential to grow?*) → filter and analyse → interpret and synthesize → communicate and disseminate → monitor and update. Sources named: scientific journals, patent filings, niche blogs, art projects, expert interviews. |
| **Stakeholder Analysis & Mapping** | Identifies actors and forces and visualizes relationships and influence; power–interest grids and stakeholder journey maps. Crucially, "for a 'for Humanity' approach, also include non-human or abstract stakeholders like 'the planet,' 'ecosystems,' 'future generations,' or 'public trust.'" Layers: key / primary / secondary stakeholders around the challenge, with objectives and issues at stake. |
| **PESTLE Analysis** | Scans the macro-environment across six domains — political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental — to identify large-scale drivers of change. (STEEP variant also named.) |
| **Unconscious Bias Model / Analysis** | Identifies and mitigates cognitive biases (confirmation bias, anchoring bias, affinity bias) that skew perception of present and future. |
| **Ladder of Inference** | "Illustrates how we move from" observable data to conclusions; surfaces the assumptions and interpretations made when observing data, so a team can see where it jumps to conclusions and revisit its observations. |

**Phase 2 — Future Framing & Problem Definition**

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Causal Layered Analysis (CLA)** | Four-layer deconstruction and reconstruction — see Worked Example structure below. |
| **Scenario Planning & the 2×2 Matrix** | Turns high-level uncertainty into a set of distinct, plausible narratives. |
| **Probability/Impact Matrix** | Sorts trends and events by likelihood × magnitude of impact, "moving from a long list of possibilities to a focused set of high-priority items." |
| **Pattern Recognition** | Looks for recurring themes, connections and relationships among diverse signals; "making sense of seemingly disparate pieces of information." |
| **Sensemaking** | "Interpreting ambiguous or complex information to develop a coherent understanding of its implications" — framing, contextualizing, narrating. |

**Phase 3 — Future-Informed Ideation**

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Futures Wheel** | Structured brainstorm of cascading ripple effects: central event → first-order → second-order → third-order consequences. |
| **Creative Mash-Up** | Forces the combination of two or more unrelated concepts — typically a present-day human need "mashed up" with a speculative future technology or trend. |
| **Future Headlines** | Write a compelling news headline from a desired future, then work backward to the products, services or social shifts that would have to exist for it to be true. |
| **Object from the Future** | Design and prototype a physical or digital artifact from a specific future — "a food packaging label from a world without plastic," a UI for a service that does not yet exist. |

**Phase 4 — Future-Fit Prototyping & Testing**

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Experiential Futures** | Moves beyond describing a future to creating an immersive experience that lets people "feel" it — artifacts from the future, staged interactions. |
| **Wind Tunneling** | "A strategic stress test": metaphorically place the idea in the wind tunnel of each scenario and assess "whether it thrives, adapts, or breaks under different future conditions." Steps: articulate the strategy's core value proposition and key assumptions → revisit the future scenarios → run the wind tunnel test with critical questions per scenario. |
| **Future Role-Playing** | Participants act out a specific situation within a scenario to uncover unforeseen social norms, conflicts and human experiences a new technology or system might create. |

**Phase 5 — Strategic Navigation**

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Backcasting** | Steps: (1) Define your desired future — a specific vision "typically dated 10–30 years from now," ideally the preferable future from the Vision Cone or a Future Headline; (2) Map major milestones backward in time, repeatedly asking what must have happened just before; (3) Detail the necessary actions, policies, technological breakthroughs or social shifts for each milestone. |
| **Future-Ready Roadmapping** | Builds on Backcasting. Steps: (1) Define the core path — the central trunk of milestones; (2) Identify key uncertainties and alternative paths, with adaptive tactics for each; (3) Define **Signposts** ("What early signals or metrics will tell us how the future is actually unfolding?" e.g. a technology reaching a price point) and **Triggers** ("At what specific threshold do we make a decision?" e.g. "If consumer adoption of X exceeds Y, we will activate Pathway B"). |

## Frameworks Introduced

### Causal Layered Analysis — the pyramid
*Present pyramid (top-down deconstruction):*
- **Litany** — "the surface level of the issue: the headlines, data, and events we see and hear."
- **Systemic cause** — "the social, economic, and political systems that create the litany."
- **Worldview** — "the cultural and ideological assumptions that support the systems."
- **Metaphor / myth** — "the deep, unconscious stories" beneath the collective worldview.

*Then:* **Step 2 — Value transformation.** Replace the limiting myth at the base with an empowering one. The book's example: "the economy as a machine" → **"the economy as a garden"**, prioritizing regeneration over pure output.

*Then:* **Step 3 — Bottom-up future development.** "The new myth inspires a new worldview. This new worldview allows for the design of new systems. These new systems ultimately produce a new, more desirable set of outcomes" — a new litany, which can seed a Future Headline. A named narrative example: reframing the human role as **"a steward within a living system."**

### The Futures Thinking Strategic Grid
A 2×2 that maps tools against **strategic intent** (vertical) and **perceived unpredictability** (horizontal):

| | Lower unpredictability | Higher unpredictability |
|---|---|---|
| **Higher intent / agency** | **Predictive Planning (targeted agency)** — Backcasting, strategic pathways, adaptive tactics, plausible scenarios | **Transformative Agency (futures immersion)** — speculative artifacts, experiential futures, creative mash-ups, future-fit prototyping |
| **Lower intent / exploration** | **Descriptive Exploration (current understanding)** — horizon scanning, PESTLE, CLA, assumptions surfacing | **Resilient Adaptation (scenario validation)** — scenario-based validation, resilience testing, 2×2 matrix, object from the future |

Its purpose: "a final filter … ensuring that every chosen method strengthens our roadmap [and] prepares us to transition seamlessly into the iterative prototyping of design thinking."

## Mental Models

- **Test for resilience, not for accuracy.** "Do not aim to predict which scenario is 'correct.' Test current strategies against all four" — a resilient solution is viable, or at least adaptable, across possible futures.
- **Explore the unthinkable quadrant.** "The most insightful strategic conversations" come from the most counter-intuitive scenario; teams instinctively skip it. Preparing for the unwanted future "is a powerful way to identify hidden vulnerabilities and build true anti-fragility."
- **Focus on the wild cards.** The transformative insights sit in **High Impact / Low Probability**, not in the high-probability corner traditional planning defends.
- **Define your scales first.** Before plotting, agree what "high impact" means in this context — a significant change in human well-being? ecological disruption?
- **Make your scenarios human.** Populate each future world with Future Personas: "How would a specific person — a farmer, a city dweller, a community leader — experience life in this world?"
- **Use CLA to build, not only to diagnose.** Deconstruct the old story, then construct a new resilient narrative from the new myth upward.

## Anti-patterns

- Leaving scenarios as abstract lists of trends rather than lived worlds.
- Plotting a Probability/Impact Matrix before agreeing on the scales.
- Treating a roadmap as a fixed plan — without signposts and triggers "a strategy cannot adapt."
- Skipping the uncomfortable quadrant of the 2×2, which is where the vulnerabilities live.
- Running Horizon Scanning on mainstream sources only; the method requires fringe sources (patent filings, niche blogs, art projects).

## Worked Example — The Future of Mobility 2×2 (Scenario Planning, page 92)

**Step 1 — Identify and prioritize drivers.** Brainstorm forces from Horizon Scanning and PESTLE; plot them by impact × uncertainty. "Those that land in the top-right quadrant (high impact, high uncertainty) are your **critical uncertainties**."

**Step 2 — Build the framework.** Select two critical uncertainties as axes, each with two plausible extremes. Here: **Level of Regulation** (high / low) × **Dominant Energy Source** (renewable / fossil-fuel).

**Step 3 — Develop the four scenarios,** each with a memorable name:

| | **Renewable energy** | **Fossil-fuel energy** |
|---|---|---|
| **High regulation** | **Regulated Renaissance** — "Public & shared transit dominates": cities invest heavily in high-speed rail, integrated public transport, and autonomous shared vehicle fleets. "Strict emissions & zoning laws": zero-emission urban zones; personal car ownership heavily taxed and restricted. | **Eco-Gridlock** — "Legacy systems endure": cities struggle to maintain aging public transport while enforcing strict congestion charges. "High cost of personal mobility": extremely high fuel taxes and significant tolls on internal-combustion vehicles. |
| **Low regulation** | **Automated Paradise** — "Corporate tech dominance": large technology companies operate vast fleets of autonomous electric vehicles competing for subscribers. "Personalized Mobility-as-a-Service (MaaS)": hyper-personalized travel with on-demand subscription models. | **Tech-Fueled Chaos** — "Hyper-individualism reigns": personal ownership of fossil-fuel vehicles remains the norm, producing extreme urban sprawl and congestion. "Fragmented innovation": new mobility services compete fiercely in a deregulated market, "creating a chaotic and inefficient system." |

Each quadrant is then enriched with Future Personas and, in the next step, stress-tested with Wind Tunneling.

**Futures Wheel example from the same toolbox (page ~100):** central event — *"By 2040, brain-computer interfaces are widely adopted, co-existing with the emergence of advanced AI."* First-order: communication becomes instantaneous and telepathic; human intelligence is seamlessly augmented. Second-order (from the telepathy branch): *"If communication is telepathic, then privacy is fundamentally redefined."* Third-order: *"new social norms and laws are required to govern 'thought crime' and mental access."*

## Key Takeaways

1. The tools chain in a fixed order; using one in isolation wastes its output.
2. Critical uncertainties = high impact **and** high uncertainty; they become scenario axes.
3. CLA is the deepest reframing instrument in the book — it changes the myth, not the metric.
4. A roadmap without signposts and triggers is a plan, not a strategy.
5. Always name your scenarios; names carry the strategy conversation.
