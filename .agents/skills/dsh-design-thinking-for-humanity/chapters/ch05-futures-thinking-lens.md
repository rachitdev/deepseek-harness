# Chapter 5 — The Futures Thinking Lens

*Pages 54–76 plus Case Study Part 1 (pp. 68–75): the Vision Cone, the Strategic Awareness Framework, the Future-Forward Design Process, and "The Living Soil" mandate.*

## Core Idea

"Futures thinking is not about predicting the future. It is about illuminating the potential, often unexpected, implications of today's problems." It is the **future space** prepended to the Double Diamond: before empathizing with a user, "we empathize with the future." The output is not a forecast but a **preferable future** that acts as a North Star from which solutions are reverse-engineered.

The governing sentence of the whole lens: *"The goal is not just to prepare for the risks we know, or to withstand the shocks we can imagine, but to build a system that grows stronger from the surprises we cannot."*

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Vision Cone
Expands outward from the present through layers of increasing uncertainty. The book's layers, with its own glosses:

| Layer | Gloss |
|---|---|
| **Preposterous futures** | "seemingly impossible today, challenges current paradigms" |
| **Possible futures** | "might happen" |
| **Plausible futures** | "could happen" |
| **Projected future** | "continuation of the present" |
| **Probable futures** | "likely to happen" |
| **Preferable futures** | "want to happen, should happen" — note the plural: "multiple pathways toward different preferable futures" |

The cone "forms the foundational framework for many tools in this toolbox … the canvas upon which we paint all subsequent futures and foresight work." Horizon Scanning and PESTLE populate its layers; the Probability/Impact Matrix prioritizes the signals; Scenario Planning builds stories from the tensions between layers; Backcasting and Future-Ready Roadmapping connect a chosen preferable future back to today. "Preferable" is explicitly not organizational preference — it "optimizes human well-being, equity, and ecological regeneration."

### The Strategic Awareness Framework (2×2: external evidence × felt certainty)

| | **External evidence exists** | **External evidence is missing** |
|---|---|---|
| **We feel certain** | **Certainties (known knowns)** — "the facts and evidence we currently possess." *Stance:* leverage and build upon this established knowledge. | **Hidden biases & blind spots (unknown knowns)** — "unconscious assumptions and flawed mental models that make us believe we understand a situation when we do not." *Stance:* challenge assumptions, actively seek diverse outside perspectives. |
| **We feel uncertain** | **Identifiable risks (known unknowns)** — "risks and variables we know exist but cannot fully predict." *Stance:* actively plan and prepare for these foreseeable uncertainties. | **Unforeseeable disruptions (unknown unknowns)** — "black swan events … impossible to predict because they lie outside our current experience." *Stance:* build genuine, systemic resilience to adapt and transform in the face of true surprise. |

### The Future-Forward Design Process — five phases

| Phase | Purpose | Example tools named |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Futures Immersion & Empathy** | Deep investigation of signals, trends, behaviors, needs; uncover biases, unquestioned assumptions, systems, core values and pervasive myths | Horizon scanning, signal gathering, assumptions surfacing, questioning |
| **2 — Future Framing & Problem Definition** | Synthesize; examine emerging trends, disruptions, transformations around a focal issue; pattern recognition | Scenario exploration, future personas, identify focal issues & envision implications, sensemaking |
| **3 — Future-Informed Ideation** | Divergent thinking with "what if" questions; solutions adaptable across scenarios | Connecting dots, brainstorming forces, future headlines, futures wheels |
| **4 — Future-Fit Prototyping & Testing** | Build prototypes that reflect different potential future worlds; test resilience and desirability against alternative futures | Future prototyping, experiential futures, scenario-based validation, resilience testing |
| **5 — Strategic Navigation** | Translate insight into actionable strategy and vision for the present | Roadmapping, backcasting |

### Traditional Strategy vs Futures Thinking in Strategy

| Feature | Traditional strategy | Futures thinking in strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Perspective | Optimizing the status quo | Systemic transformation |
| Method | Historical data and linear trends | Scans for weak signals and disruptive shifts |
| Horizon | Immediate competitive advantage | Long-term planetary health |
| Response | Reactive adjustment to market forces | Proactive design of a preferable future |

### Fractal scaling of the method
Same method, four scopes — **global** (climate change, societal polarization, human–AI relationship), **national** (proactive "health-care" rather than reactive sick-care), **local/community** (urban resilience, local food systems, mobility, community garden networks), and focal variants for a **specific company** ("Future of Employee Engagement and Well-Being in an AI-Driven Hybrid World"), an **industry** ("Achieving True Circularity in Manufacturing"), or a **startup** ("Building Trust and Ethical AI").

### Three Pillars of Future-Forward Strategy
1. **Developing strategic agency** — "stop viewing the future as a destination that happens to us."
2. **Prototyping systems, not just products** — "Every strategy is a hypothesis"; stress-test governance, policy and business models before deployment.
3. **Activating the mandate** — translate high-level futures work into a real strategic pathway.

## Key Concepts

- **Weak signal** — ambiguous and subtle (not yet mainstream), potentially significant (carries the seed of major disruption), requires interpretation ("they demand that we connect the dots").
- **Megatrend** — the long-term driver a weak signal is an early indicator of; the book maps them on PESTLE axes with a confidence level (high / medium / low).
- **Hybrid interview** — blends structured and unstructured questions; used with scientists, economists, community activists and indigenous leaders.
- **No-regret move** — a strategic action that is a good idea in *all* scenarios.
- **Contingent strategy** — a pre-committed response triggered by early signals that a specific scenario is emerging.
- **Butterfly effect** — small events triggering massive consequences in an interconnected world.
- **Facilitator as sensemaker** — "While AI collaborators can analyze data, the human facilitator remains the crucial sensemaker."

## Mental Models

- **Prediction is only valid for narrow, stable, fully-parameterized questions.** Everything else needs the possibility space.
- **The future is unpredictable, not predetermined; plausible, not singular.** Individual and collective choices shape it; understanding potential futures empowers present-day action.
- **Psychological safety is a foresight requirement.** "Exploring potential futures, especially disruptive or dystopian ones, can provoke anxiety"; the facilitator must let people confront these without fear and reframe emotion as insight.
- **Divergent phases need expansion, convergent phases need commitment** — the facilitator switches mode deliberately between phases 1/3 and 2/5.
- **Use the future as a laboratory** to test assumptions and prototype resilience.

## Anti-patterns

- **Single-point forecasting** — "the assumed certainty of a single predicted future."
- **Decision-makers who focus only on "the predicted future" that unfolds gradually from current patterns.**
- **Stopping at an abstract vision** — the process must "conclude with a real strategic pathway that bridges the gap between our future ambition and today's execution."
- **Foresight as a passive academic exercise** rather than an engine for action.
- **Treating scenarios as predictions** rather than as narratives used to stress-test strategy.

## Worked Example — Case Study Part 1: The Living Soil Mandate

**The mandate:** "Design a new global economic and agricultural model that makes the regeneration of the world's topsoil the primary measure of agricultural success, ensuring long-term food security, reversing climate change, and restoring biodiversity **by 2050**."

**Phase 1 — Futures Immersion & Empathy.**

*Weak signals collected (the book's five):*
1. A consortium of food companies invests in a satellite platform to monitor soil carbon levels in real time.
2. A "soil microbiome" health test for farms becomes commercially available.
3. A fashion brand launches a clothing line made from fibers grown using regenerative agricultural practices.
4. The government of a small nation officially includes "Natural Capital" in its national accounting.
5. A popular health documentary links human gut health directly to the soil health where food is grown.

*Clustered into four driving forces:*
- **The Soil-Health Nexus** — growing scientific and public understanding of the link between soil health, human health, and climate stability.
- **Verifiable Regeneration** — new technologies that transparently and affordably measure ecological outcomes like soil carbon.
- **The Bio-Economy** — agricultural products as feedstock for everything from fashion to construction materials.
- **New Economic Models** — economics that account for the value of nature.

*Hybrid interview prompts used with experts:*
- *Far-sighted:* "If you could speak with an oracle from 2050, what three things would you want to know about the state of the world's soil?"
- *Optimistic scenario:* "If humanity were to successfully regenerate the majority of its agricultural soils by 2050, what would be true of our food systems?"
- *Pessimistic scenario:* "If our soils were to continue to degrade, what might be the single most catastrophic, unforeseen consequence?"
- *System constraints:* "What is the single most powerful force currently preventing widespread adoption of regenerative agriculture?"
- *Leverage points:* "What is the single most important intervention we could make in the next five years to accelerate the transition to a soil-first food system?"

**Phase 2 — Four scenarios (each with drivers, economic model, challenge):**

| Scenario | Story | Key drivers | Economic model | Challenge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Regenerative Abundance** (optimistic) | Global awakening; regenerative agriculture becomes the norm; food is abundant, nutritious, and a primary carbon-sequestration tool | Political will; consumer awareness; breakthroughs in soil science and monitoring | Regenerative circular bio-economy; farmers rewarded for ecological outcomes as well as food | Scaling globally and ensuring an equitable transition for all farmers |
| **2. The Techno-Food System** (centralized efficiency) | Soil degradation ignored; lab-grown food and synthetic biology feed the population | Belief in purely technological salvation; powerful biotech corporates; reactive policy | Centralized, patent-driven food-tech industry; traditional agriculture a niche premium market | Unforeseen ecological side effects, biodiversity loss, disconnection from natural food systems |
| **3. Local Havens, Global Famine** (adaptive) | Global food systems collapse; resilient communities build self-sufficient regenerative "food havens"; famine and conflict elsewhere | Geopolitical fragmentation; supply-chain collapse; strong local leadership | Network of decentralized, self-sufficient local food economies | No global coordination; severe inequality between havens and collapsed regions |
| **4. The Great Dust Bowl** (pessimistic) | Business-as-usual degenerative agriculture; catastrophic erosion and desertification | Political inertia; vested interests in chemical-industrial agriculture; failure to act on warnings | Degenerative, fragile, resource-constrained agricultural economy | Mass suffering and migration; potential large-scale civilizational collapse |

**Phase 3 — Creative Mash-Up.**
*Intersection of signals:* AI-powered real-time soil health monitoring × a social platform based on verifiable contributions × the legal concept of "natural capital" in national accounting.
*Novel user need:* "A way for farmers to be directly and transparently rewarded for the ecological value they create (sequestering carbon, increasing biodiversity), not just for the volume of crops they produce."
*Result — **The "Carbon Harvest" Platform:*** a global digital platform where farmers use verifiable real-time data from soil sensors and satellites to issue **"regeneration credits"** representing sequestered carbon and increased biodiversity, sold on an open market to corporations and individuals.

**Phases 4 & 5 — Scenario readiness and strategy.**
*Readiness questions across six dimensions:* **Human** (does it genuinely serve human needs across all demographics; what new skills/mindsets are needed), **Planet** (regeneration rather than depletion), **Knowledge** (what critical information is missing), **Tools** (what technologies and infrastructures are required), **Rules** (what policies, regulations and ethical frameworks must be established), **Networks** (what new collaborations and partnerships must be forged).

*No-regret moves* (good in all four scenarios):
- Investment in low-cost, verifiable soil sensors — "valuable whether the future is regenerative or techno-utopian."
- Building a powerful, farmer-centric educational platform — "empowering farmers with knowledge is a resilient strategy in any future."

*Contingent strategies:*
- If early signals of **Regenerative Abundance** appear (e.g. major governments begin to subsidize soil carbon) → rapidly scale the market-making function.
- If early signals of **The Techno-Food System** appear (e.g. massive investment in lab-grown food) → pivot to position the platform as a premium "natural food" verification system for a niche high-value market.

## Key Takeaways

1. Start in the future space; the preferable future becomes the North Star that solutions are reverse-engineered from.
2. Audit your ignorance honestly with the Strategic Awareness Framework — the dangerous quadrant is *unknown knowns*, where confidence outruns evidence.
3. Build four scenarios, not one forecast, and give each drivers, an economic model, and a challenge.
4. Convert scenarios to strategy through **no-regret moves plus contingent strategies**.
5. Weak signals are only useful once clustered into driving forces.
